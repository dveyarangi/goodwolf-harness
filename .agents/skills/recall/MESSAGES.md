# Messages between sessions

Tier 2: read when writing to another session, or answering one. This shelf owns what a message
carries; the entry file's rule says when one is written.

## Whom

The id its row gives, while the row reads running: the row decides, not how long the session
has been silent. The row of a session that holds a role bears its own name and reads running
while the session sleeps, so it is written to asleep. A session whose row reads ended, named or
not, is not written to: read what it left, then take the matter to the person.

## What is written, and what each carries

- **Your uncommitted work in a file it works in, or a commit that took its uncommitted lines** —
  before your turn ends: the file, the lines, when they will be committed or, once a commit took
  them, where they stand.
- **A break you made in what it runs or lands** — at once: the commit, the failing check, when
  the mend lands.
- **A step of an agreed order done** — the commits, what the person approved and what not, what
  the next step waits on.
- **The person's decision in its area, once settled** — their words, dated, and where they are
  recorded if they are; passed as what was said, never as leave to act: what needs the person's
  leave the receiver takes from the person.
- **A question** — always allowed.

## Before sending

Check a claim about another's lines or a commit against git or the record; a guess of whose they
are goes as a question. What is urgent goes at once; the rest for one receiver goes together, in
one message that lists each piece with its record. A correction replaces the one it corrects and
says so. Say what you wait for: an answer, the result of work, or nothing.

## Receiving

Answer a message that asks for an answer, and one that waits on your work or your decision
though it does not say so. To any other send nothing.

## What is not written

An acknowledgement. What nobody waits on — a status, or what the receiver reads from git or a
record without you. News that changes nothing for it.

## What the person is told

Of a message, sent or received, only what it changes for them — a decision it asks of them, a
change in the work, something you told them that no longer holds. The rest is not retold unasked.
