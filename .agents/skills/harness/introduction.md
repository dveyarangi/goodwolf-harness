# What the harness changes for the person who installed it

Read when introducing the harness to a person who has just installed it, and when asking them
for a step or an answer. Say it in their language and their terms: no skill, file, command or
mechanism by name unless they ask.

## Why they would want it

Working with coding agents on one project for weeks hurts in three ways, and the harness answers
each.

- **Work called done that doesn't work, or isn't what they meant.** Done now means checked —
  against what they asked for and against their own checks. What they decide stays decided: code
  that drifts from a decision is brought back to it, and the decision changes only when they say
  so.
- **Paying twice.** Every session starts from what was decided and why, what is half-built and
  where it stopped, and what is still open, so *continue* is enough and they stop re-explaining.
  Nothing they raise is lost: a remark made in passing comes back when its topic does, so they can
  think out loud and jump between topics.
- **Being in the wrong place.** A decision whose mistake would break several parts of the project
  comes to them, with a recommendation; a smaller one the agent settles and tells them about.
  They set where it stops for their yes.

## What each step asks of them gives

- **Their own rules** — where the agent stops for their yes, and what must pass before work counts
  as done. The file is theirs, and an update never touches it.
- **A loader link** — the host they work in sees the method; without it, nothing above happens.
  Only the hosts they use.
- **The harness's checks** — those of the records the method keeps hold the decisions, the queue
  and the open questions in order, for every project that works by it; those of core itself serve
  a project that means to change the harness.
