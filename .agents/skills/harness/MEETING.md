# The meeting — the study and the report

<straw-dog question="q-0037.0006.0020.0001">
Tier 2: read when an install meets a tree that already has a method — an entry file or an
`.agents/` of its own. This shelf owns the study and the report; when the install stops for the
person, and the two modes of taking the tree's method in, are the skill's. The shelf is read twice:
before an install, on the tree as it is; and after a migration, on the same tree with the same
sample and key, as that migration's verification — the second report beside the first, its ledger
beside the first's.

## The study

Read the tree as a capable newcomer would, by search and sampling, not whole; the tree is its
default branch as checked out, and what is deployed is not the study's subject. The study writes
nothing of its own into the tree: the report goes to the path it was given, scratch files where
the host puts them; what the project's own tools do when run is the project's. The study has three
parts, and the report says which part each statement comes from: a short bracketed mark on each
paragraph, the three marks named once in the report's first section. The read is quick; the key is the
study's largest cost, built from what the inventory and the readings meet, never from a second pass.

**The inventory.** The entry file and what it includes; skills of the tree's own; scripts, gates and
checks and the rules that name them; outside coordinators and contracts; the kinds of record the
tree keeps — decisions, plans, runbooks, specs — and where its current state lives; and the grain
of the project's work, read from its history — how large a change is, in lines and files per merged
PR, commits per PR, PRs per week — so that work offered to it later is cut at its own grain. This is
what the harness does not replace, said in the tree's own names.

**The readings.** Each is one way a project's knowledge fails to reach the person who needs it, or
to hold once it has, counted and quoted in the tree's own words:

<straw-dog question="q-0037.0006.0020.0013">
1. *What it costs to reach what is written* — files, searches, steps between a question and its
   answer; chains of records that supersede one another; documents readable only by search.
2. *Kinds of record mixed in one file* — instructions, decisions and history in one place;
   instructions in records the entry file names only as a whole, which reach a reader only if that
   record is opened.
3. *One fact in several homes* — the same rule or fact stated twice, and where the two have parted;
   what was met on the way, never claimed as all there is.
4. *What no one records* — the tree's memory of its own operation: sessions, handovers, why a piece
   of work stopped; the gap shows as history leaking into rules and as work no record explains.
5. *Where the project does not follow its own rules* — a rule stated and a case against it standing
   in the tree; a rule whose gate covers less than the rule claims; a rule with no gate at all, held
   by attention alone.
6. *Who owns what* — which record governs which part of the tree, who may change it, who decides
   what; where a boundary is written — a contract, a coordinator, a role — and where it is unsaid,
   so two parties may both believe a thing is theirs, or neither.
7. *Where the person stands* — what waits on them and for how long; what was decided without them
   that should have come to them; what came to them that need not have.
8. *A decision without its reason* — records that hold what was decided and not why, so the next
   reader cannot tell a choice from an accident, nor see when the reason has lapsed.
</straw-dog>

**The ledger.** A measurement the report keeps so the same tasks can be measured again after a
migration. A blind run: a fresh agent, told nothing of the harness or of a measurement, is given the
repository and a sample of questions, answers read-only from files, git history and the pull-request
list where the host has one, and reports how it searched. The author of the report never answers the sample; the key is built from
the code and the records before the run and corrected only where the run proves it wrong. Where
the run proves it incomplete, the addition is marked as one and no score changes.

The sample, from the tree as it stands on the day:

| group | items | the key holds | scored |
|---|---|---|---|
| settled decisions | 5 | the decision and its reason; the question asks *why* | hit · partial · miss |
| in flight | 3 | the item and its state | hit · partial · miss |
| undecided | 3 | that it is open, and who decides | hit · partial · miss |
| resting on contradictory or incomplete records | 3 | the two records, or the record that stops short; asked about the topic, never the discrepancy | noticed and said · answered from one side · filled the gap |
| strong but hidden invariants or structures | 3 | what the code enforces and no record states as a general rule — a decision record's mention of one case does not state the rule; what the tree's shape implies and no record says | recovered · not |

Counted beside the answers: tokens, tool calls and wall time from the host's account of the blind
run — where the host gives none, the ledger says so and counts what the agent's own account gives;
files read from the blind agent's own account of how it searched. And what the project's method
asks of a change today, as numbers: rules read by every session — the entry file and every file in
the tree a rule orders read at every session, a read outside the tree named and not counted —, CI
jobs and steps per PR, checklist items, PR body length, decision records per merged PR, check
scripts. The project's practices stay its own, kept by its choice; the harness integrates with them
and replaces none. The count is there so the same count after a migration shows what the harness is
for: fewer steps, and each step more precise, because at each step what is needed is known. The
count is counted, never judged: the body cites its numbers where an argument needs one and draws no
conclusion from them; the table is the ledger's.

## The report

The body of the person's question, *what would the harness change here?*, headed by that question
in the project's language, and written in the project's names. No term or phrase of the harness's
own — mechanism, store, straw dog, tier, rung, install block, a session *standing on* a question —
appears in the harness's sense; a word the project uses itself is the project's, used as it uses it; the harness is named by its name, <straw-dog question="q-0037.0006.0020.0013">GoodWolf</straw-dog>, never by
the bare word; the study's
parts are described by what they do; what it brings is said as what it does for the project: a home for
each kind of record; a rule installed where it is read; open questions kept with their state and the
session's position among them; a record of each session; a disagreement between records repaired
where it is met; a decision kept with what it waits on.

Sections, in this order, each headed:

0. **How this was read.** What the study is — the inventory, the readings named and said in one
   line each, the ledger and how it was taken; that the read is quick and sampled; that what it
   found is what it met, not an inventory, and that the harness repairs a disagreement where a
   session meets it, every session meeting its own. A reader who has not seen the harness understands
   every later section from this one.
1. **What was found.** By the inventory and the readings, each a paragraph with its numbers and
   quotes; then what the method asks of a change, as the count; then what stays as it is.
2. **Main recommendations.** Three to five, each one sentence and its reason, about the project's
   documentation and its method, each said as what GoodWolf does about it; never about what the
   project builds.
3. **What clearly improves, and what the person will likely give up.** The report's main
   conclusion, in prose addressed to the person — what you will see change, and what you will
   likely let go of. What you will likely let go of is
   the project's own habits — a file that holds everything, history kept in rules, state kept in
   memory — never a limitation of GoodWolf: its own not-yets stand in section 5 alone, and only
   those the plan meets.
4. **The migration plan.** A table: what, what of GoodWolf carries it, what it touches and what
   depends on it, size; then the order, each item cut at the project's grain of work as the
   inventory read it. Taking GoodWolf in — beside the project's method or in its place — is the
   first item, and the rest are carried by it; an item GoodWolf has nothing for says so.
5. **What this report does not say.** No comparison of the tree's method with <straw-dog question="q-0037.0006.0020.0013">GoodWolf</straw-dog>
   before an install, a migration and a real check; no inventory of disagreements; the ledger measures one
   thing, the cost of recovering the project's state on a sample, and is far from the whole harness;
   the second reading, after a migration, is that migration's verification, and nothing is read
   from it while the migration is unfinished but which of the plan has landed.
6. **Appendix: the ledger.** The counts; the sample and its key; the blind run's return, whole and
   unedited. The ledger lives here and nowhere else in the report: the body argues from its counts
   and never restates it. It is kept so the check after a migration runs the same sample against the
   same key, scored the same way, and its ledger is appended beside this one, never merged into it.

The report is a product document: it shows the harness's strengths by finding where it helps, and
claims nothing the tree or the ledger does not show. No self-deprecation, no groundless comparison,
nothing about the project's substance. Its product faces — sections 0, 2, 3 and 5 — speak to the
person, in the second person; section 1 and the appendix state what was found and counted, without the
address.
</straw-dog>
