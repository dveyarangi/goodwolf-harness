# A straw dog is marked when it is written, and found when it was not

- **Status:** Planned (own align precedes)
- **Type:** HITL
- **Depends on:** [01-0011.0020](./done/01-0011.0020-rules-one-home.md) (the injector, if the
  reviewing rule reaches its skill as an installed block)
- **Outcome:** A sentence or part that serves only until a named ticket lands is wrapped as a
  temporary statement bound to that ticket at the moment it is written, by the entry file's
  instruction; the enumerator reports likely unmarked ones as findings a maintainer judges; and
  at every landing, a stated skill reviews the landed work against the pending tickets and marks
  what those tickets will change as a straw dog, so an expiry the enumerator cannot see stops
  depending on a person rereading the file by luck.

## Why this exists, and what found it

It does not. The enumerator finds what is wrapped in `<temporary>`; nothing looks for a sentence
that is temporary in substance and unwrapped, so such a sentence is found when a person happens to
reread the file. The injector's slice was the demonstration: its third verify pass found three
live sentences the slice had made false — *not a live question while nothing installs*, *no
installer exists, so today nothing reads it*, *`/maintain` owns none* — and a doc paragraph
carrying *by hand* and *until*. Every one was bound to a ticket in fact and none in form. The
enumerator listed none of them.

The term is the user's, 2026-09-08: a **straw dog** is a thing made to serve until the real one
arrives and then be discarded; *temporary statement* names only its marking. The glossary carries
it.

## What to build

- **The duty at write time, tier 1.** The entry file's general rules gain one sentence: a
  sentence true only until a named ticket lands is written inside a `<temporary>` bound to that
  ticket, when it is written. The *Temporary statements* section reads as instruction rather than
  description. Entry contract v5, with a findings entry.
- **Candidates from the enumerator.** `temporary_statements.py` reports, beside its statements
  and diagnostics, likely unmarked straw dogs: sentences outside any wrapper carrying words of the
  kind the demonstration carried — *until*, *not yet*, *no X exists*, *for now*, *once X lands*,
  *by hand*. A finding, never a diagnostic: false positives are certain, the run does not fail on
  one, and the maintainer judges each, which is C2's shape already. `/maintain`'s T1 says to read
  them.
- **The review at landing.** One skill, at every landing, reads the landed work against the
  pending tickets and marks what those tickets will change as a straw dog bound to the ticket.
  The reading is judgment — *What to build* names its targets in words, and no script derives
  them — so the mechanical half is the lint and the human half is this duty. If the rule is the
  maintenance mechanism's, it reaches its skill as an installed block from `maintain.rules.md`,
  the first amend-at-source-and-install the injector meets after its own slice.
- **A wrapper goes around an installed block, never inside it.** Marking a block as a straw dog
  is not editing it.
- **What the clock reads.** Wrapping a sentence changes the file it sits in; under
  [.0060](./01-0011.0060-mechanism-rechecked-when-governing-moves.md) that is the mechanism's own
  surface moving, and a marking pass makes mechanisms due at `output`. Named here so the align
  says it rather than discovers it.

## Decisions this ticket's align owns

- **Which skill reviews at landing.** `/verify` already compares landed work against its ticket
  and governing docs, and would add the pending tickets to the comparison; `/ticket` at mint
  already runs `/impact` on the draft and could mark what the new ticket will replace; `/impact`
  traces consequences. Life's practice is unread and is read first, as .0022's align read
  `maintenance`.
- **Whether the reviewing rule is the maintenance mechanism's**, installed into that skill, or
  the skill's own. The rule is about expiry, which `/maintain` owns; the moment is a landing,
  which `/verify` owns.
- **The word list**, and whether it lives in the enumerator or beside it. Small and stupid
  argues for the enumerator; a recipient's own words argue for a facet on
  [01-0010.0110](./01-0010.0110-project-facets-injected.md).
- **The v5 sentence's wording.**
