# A straw dog is marked when it is written, and found when it was not

- **Status:** Ready (aligned 2026-09-08)
- **Type:** HITL
- **Outcome:** A straw dog — anything written to serve until a named ticket replaces it — is
  wrapped in `<straw-dog>` bound to that ticket when it is written, by the entry file's
  instruction; the listing script reports likely unwrapped ones as candidates a person judges; and
  at every landing, `/verify` reads the landed work against the pending tickets and wraps what
  they will replace, so an expiry the script cannot see stops depending on a person rereading the
  file by luck.

## Why this exists, and what found it

It does not. The enumerator finds what is wrapped in `<temporary>`; nothing looks for a sentence
that is temporary in substance and unwrapped, so such a sentence is found when a person happens to
reread the file. The injector's slice was the demonstration: its third verify pass found three
live sentences the slice had made false — *not a live question while nothing installs*, *no
installer exists, so today nothing reads it*, *`/maintain` owns none* — and a doc paragraph
carrying *by hand* and *until*. Every one was bound to a ticket in fact and none in form. The
enumerator listed none of them.

The term is the user's, 2026-09-08: a **straw dog** is a thing made to serve until the real one
arrives and then be discarded; *temporary statement* names only its marking. The glossary carries
it.

## What to build

*Rewritten to the align's answers, 2026-09-08; the questions as asked, and each answer, are
under the next heading.*

- **One name, one tag.** `<temporary until= ticket=>` becomes `<straw-dog until= ticket=>`,
  attributes unchanged, in the four operative blocks, the scripts that parse it and their tests,
  the entry file, the architecture and `/maintain`'s T1 and T2; the listing script is renamed
  with it. Prose mentions in tickets and session records describe what was and stay.
- **The duty at root, in one home.** The entry file's section becomes *Straw dogs* and opens
  with the duty, addressed to the writer: wrap it when you write it, anywhere; what has no named
  successor is a claim and is not wrapped. Entry contract v5, with a findings entry. No
  general-rules bullet.
- **Candidates from the listing script.** Beside its statements and diagnostics, likely
  unwrapped straw dogs in the paths given: sentences outside code, wrapped spans and table rows,
  in landed surfaces only, carrying one of a fixed short list of words. Never a diagnostic;
  the exit status is untouched. `/maintain`'s T1 says to read them and judge each.
- **The review at landing, `/verify`'s own sentence.** One bullet under *Check and repair*:
  read the landed work against the pending tickets; what one of them will replace is a straw
  dog, wrapped and bound to it; what has no named successor is left alone. The reading is
  judgment — *What to build* names its targets in words, and no script derives them — so the
  script is the mechanical half and this duty the human half. **The bullet is itself a straw
  dog**, bound to [01-0016](./01-0016-responsibility-coherent.md), until that align elects the
  mechanism responsible for straw dogs.
- **A wrapper goes around an installed block, never inside it.** Marking a block as a straw dog
  is not editing it.
- **What the clock reads.** Wrapping a sentence changes the file it sits in; under
  [.0060](./01-0011.0060-mechanism-rechecked-when-governing-moves.md) that is the mechanism's own
  surface moving, and a marking pass makes mechanisms due at `output`. Named here so the align
  says it rather than discovers it.

## Decisions this ticket's align owns

- **One concept, one name — decided 2026-09-08 (the user): rename.** The tag becomes
  `<straw-dog until="…" ticket="…">`, attributes unchanged; the enumerator is renamed with it;
  *temporary statement* and *temporary* retire into the glossary entry's *Avoid* list; the entry
  file's section is retitled. The decisive fact: the enumerator reports four operative blocks in
  the whole tree, so the rename is four blocks, three scripts with two test files, the entry
  file, the architecture, `/maintain`'s T1 and T2, and the glossary; the hundred-odd prose
  mentions in tickets and session records describe what was and stay. Life was read first and
  offers nothing to borrow: it has no marking convention, and its own entry file carries an
  unwrapped *stays provisional until the operator replaces it*.
- **What a straw dog is not — settled 2026-09-08 at the user's question,** *why are `not yet` rows
  and provisional criteria straw dogs?* They are not. A `not yet` row declares an absence and is
  rewritten, not discarded, when its ticket lands: the opposite of a straw dog, which is a
  presence serving in place of the real thing. Provisional criteria are the ordinary state of a
  record not yet aligned, which its status already says; marking them would be *mark everything*
  inside one file. So: **one concept, one marking, and a scope** — a straw dog lives in a surface
  read as truth (a skill body, a doc, the entry file, code, evidence, a closed record), never in a
  working record that is provisional by status. The align's opening premise of *three forms
  unified* is withdrawn.
