# Changing how the project builds is one edit, not a refactor across skills

- **Status:** In progress (align underway; its boundary is the align's first question)
- **Type:** HITL
- **Related:** [01-0017.0010](./01-0017.0010-terms-defined-before-they-land.md) — the sibling
  conversion, and the first shape that wanted group targets;
  [01-0020](./01-0020-pacer.md) — holds `docs/process.md` under a straw dog, so the two must agree
  what remains in that document; [01-0016](./01-0016-responsibility-coherent.md) — *every skill
  states only what it owns* is the rule this mechanism would let a whole corpus obey at once
- **Outcome:** The transferable practice this harness carries — how tests are written, how
  architecture is documented — has one owner, so adopting a different practice is an edit to one
  mechanism instead of a refactor across scattered skills, and what belongs to that corpus rather
  than to the harness's own loop is settled rather than assumed.

## Practice is swappable; the loop is not

*(the user, 2026-09-10)* — **the process loop is not part of this corpus.** It is the harness's own
opinionated method, and separating it would make every description of the loop local, in a tree
where much of what matters sits outside the loop entirely. That is a materially larger task and
this ticket does not take it.

What is left is the material a project could hold a different opinion about without ceasing to use
this harness: **how tests are written, and how architecture is documented.** A project that
practises something other than TDD, or documents architecture to a different shape, is still
running this loop. That is the cut, stated as a test rather than as a list.

The observable cost today is that the corpus is scattered across skills that each restate a piece
of it, so adopting a different practice means finding and editing all of them.

**The list is deliberately not enumerated here** *(the user, 2026-09-10)*: the minting must not
presume it knows the membership. Named so far, as examples and not as the boundary — `/tdd` and
its five shelves, and the architecture documentation structure carried in `/align`'s format
shelves and `/review-architecture`. Roughly 850 lines sit in that neighbourhood; how much of it is
practice and how much is the harness's own is the first thing the align decides.

## What to build

This is the decision-bearing phase. `/mechanism` governs the declaration; `/ticket` decomposes
what remains.

- **The boundary, first and explicitly.** What is transferable practice, what is the harness's own
  loop, and what is neither. Everything else in this ticket waits on it, and a membership list
  written before the test exists would be the same defect this ticket is fixing.
- **A mechanism owning that corpus's text**, as any other mechanism owns text *(the user,
  2026-09-10)* — one instruction file, one rules file, its shelves as its parts.
- **The scatter collapses.** A rule of practice appears once, in the mechanism, and reaches the
  skills that need it as installed blocks rather than as restatements.

## Open issues

- **The repair policy does not travel, and this ticket may or may not own it.** `AGENTS.md` ships
  the `repair` switch to every recipient; the policy that switch selects lives in
  `docs/process.md#autonomy-and-repair`, which says *"This section owns the current policy"* and
  which a recipient replaces wholesale. A recipient gets the switch and not the rule. It is method
  rather than practice, so by this ticket's own cut it is probably **not** here — but it is the
  sharpest instance of method text sitting in the instance half, and it needs an owner. Raised at
  this align, 2026-09-10; no home yet.
- **Consequence for [01-0010.0140](./done/01-0010.0140-core-stands-alone.md), flagged the same day:**
  its proposed repair wraps `/verify`'s and `/implement`'s `docs/process.md` links in a
  `<project-local>` block. For `#verification` that is right. For `#autonomy-and-repair` it would
  make the check pass **and entrench the defect**, by declaring the repair policy a project-local
  fact when it is core. That ticket must not take the blanket repair.
- **What `docs/process.md` keeps.** Its straw dog gives the document to
  [01-0020](./01-0020-pacer.md) once the pacer exists. If method text leaves it for core, the
  pacer's claim narrows to sequencing. The two tickets decide this together or one of them is
  wrong.
- **Whether this mechanism is one or several.** TDD practice and architecture documentation are
  swappable independently. One mechanism with two corpora, or two mechanisms — the align decides,
  and one recipient's opinion is one shape.

## Recorded, not this ticket's

**The composite rule set** *(the user, 2026-09-10)*: rather than one mechanism injecting the
method's rules into everything, the entire rule set could become composite — no "pure core"
anywhere, every rule owned by an installed skill, and the loop expressed dynamically by what is
installed rather than stated in one place. This ticket takes the ordinary reading — a mechanism
owns text — and the composite reading is filed as [concern 1](../concerns.md) rather than built.

## Acceptance criteria — provisional, firmed at the align

- [ ] The boundary is stated as a test, not a list: given a rule, it says whether the rule is
      transferable practice, the harness's own loop, or neither.
- [ ] The corpus is declared as a mechanism (or mechanisms) to the shape, and
      `mechanisms.py --check` passes over it.
- [ ] A rule of practice appears once; every other occurrence is an installed block or a link.
- [ ] Swapping one practice is demonstrated as an edit to the mechanism, with no skill body edited
      by hand.
- [ ] The repair-policy finding has a disposition: carried here, routed to a named ticket, or
      recorded as not this ticket's with its reason.
- [ ] `docs/process.md`'s remaining contents are agreed with [01-0020](./01-0020-pacer.md).
- [ ] `/verify` has been run on this ticket against its ticket, RFC, and governing docs.
