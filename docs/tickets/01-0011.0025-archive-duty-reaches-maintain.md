## Why this exists

This is the slice that closes the defect the whole spec was written from. On 2026-09-06 five
tickets sat finished and unarchived. The duty that requires the move is written in `/ticket`'s
files; `/maintain` owns archiving and carries only the permission form — *a ticket moves only when
every acceptance box is checked*. Three `/maintain` passes each correctly declined to touch records
they had not examined, and nothing swept, so the backlog was invisible rather than ignored.

Writing the duty into `/maintain` by hand would fix this instance and leave the next to chance.
Installing it from `/ticket`'s rules file is what makes the fix mechanical in both directions.

**Paired close is a behavior of `/ticket`, not a mechanism.** It is not a skill, and under one
instruction file per mechanism it cannot be one. `/maintain` is not a part of it: it is the target
the rule is installed into, and it is the mechanism for maintaining mechanisms.

## What to build

- `.agents/mechanisms/ticket/` with its doc, declaring `/ticket`. Parts with their standing:
  `/ticket`'s skill and `TICKET-FORMAT.md`, `move_doc.py` and its five test files owned;
  `docs_corpus.py` and `tests/harness.py` **relied-on**, and this is the mechanism that owns them —
  which closes the gap `01-0011.0022` declared.
- A moments table, and the authority on each rule.
- `ticket.rules.md` — the paired-close rules in one file, one demarcated section per rule, each
  naming its target, its anchor and its tier. Installed into `/maintain` by the injector, so
  `/maintain` carries the **duty** and not only the permission.
- `docs/tickets/` and `docs/rfc/` declared as records: what a row is, its fields, its tier, and
  what removes an entry when it is finished with. The status vocabulary and the header block are
  that shape.
- A maintainer script for those records, checking them against the declared shape. **Format never
  content, live rows only**; `done/` is archived history and exempt. Whether a ticket should have
  been written, or is any good, is judgment and not the script's. Both header forms are already in
  the tree — sixteen records use `- **Status:**` and two use bare `**Status:**` — so the shape
  either admits both or the two are repaired.
- What paired close owns leaves `docs/process.md`. What belongs to mechanisms nobody has declared
  stays and waits for them.
- Its evidence at `docs/mechanisms/ticket.evidence.md`, and no link from `.agents/skills/ticket/`
  into `docs/` outside a `<project-local>` block.
- A register row in `.agents/README.md`.

## Input from `01-0011.0022`, 2026-09-07

`/maintain`'s body already carries paired close's rules as a hand copy inside a `<temporary>`
bound to this ticket, under *Installed from other mechanisms*, numbered **P1–P4** — finished is
every box checked; close the pair in one invocation; header and queue row are the maintainer's,
the mover changes nothing else; a spec's agreements, obligations and open issues before archiving
it. `ticket.rules.md` keeps those IDs so a report cites the same name before and after the
install, and the installer's block replaces the temporary statement byte for byte in content.

## Decisions this ticket's align owns

- **`/ticket`'s record shape, field by field.** It is the first record shape in this tree and the
  one `01-0011.0030` and `.0040` both read; getting it wrong is expensive twice over.
- **The two divergent header forms** — admitted by the shape, or repaired to one. Repairing is
  cleaner and touches eighteen records; admitting is cheaper and permanent.
- **Whether the maintainer script is check-only or also fixes.** Format repair is mechanical, but a
  fixer that runs unasked is a writer nobody reviewed.
- **`/ticket`'s moments, and which absences are honest.**
