# The tickets' open issues are entries of the store

- **Status:** Ready
- **Type:** HITL
- **Plan:** [the RFC](../rfc/01-0011.0100.0020-open-issues-are-entries-of-the-store.md)
- **Depends on:** [01-0011.0100.0010 the-store-and-questions-writes-it](./done/01-0011.0100.0010-the-store-and-questions-writes-it.md)
  (the store to hold them); [01-0011.0100.0018 a-question-id-says-where-it-sits](./done/01-0011.0100.0018-a-question-id-says-where-it-sits.md)
  (the fold's entries born with nested ids)
- **Answers:** [q-0001.0014](../questions/done/q-0001.0014-are-the-tickets-open-questions-kept-in-the-store-with-their-arguments.md)
- **Outcome:** Every open issue a live ticket carries is an entry of the store with its argument,
  under the question the ticket answers; the *Open issues* section leaves the ticket format; and a
  reconciliation proves the fold lost nothing.

## Decisions landed at this ticket's align

Opened 2026-10-03.

1. **An open issue is an unresolved question the ticket owns** *(the user, 2026-10-03)*. The
   format's definition is kept — the unresolved decisions and HITL forks, nothing resolved — and
   from now on held to. Read on 2026-10-03, the 40 bullets of the 15 live tickets were about half
   that, and the rest what the format had no other slot for: pointers to a question another
   ticket owns, findings stated as facts, work not yet done or a warning to another ticket, a
   constraint, a placeholder and a struck-through resolution. The fold sorts each to its home: a
   pointer stays its owner's entry and is not copied; a finding is phrased as the question it
   argues and becomes an entry, the store owning concerns (parent decision 21); undone work goes
   to `What to build`, or becomes a question if it is a fork; a constraint goes to `What to
   build` or the criteria; a placeholder or a resolution is removed, as P7 already requires.
   Refused: widening the definition to whatever is unfinished, with kinds of entry other than a
   question — it undoes the parent's decision 12, the unit is the question, and a finding with
   no question closes by none of the six kinds.
2. ~~**The bullet keeps its argument and opens with its entry's id**~~ *(the user, 2026-10-03;
   superseded the same day by decision 3)*: it rested on parent decision 24's clause that the
   argument stays in the owner record.
3. **A question's entry holds its own argument** *(the user, 2026-10-03; amends parent decision
   24, supersedes decision 2)*. The entry admits a body after its parts: the argument, the
   positions, the evidence. The ticket keeps no text about its open questions; they are the
   subtree under the question it serves (q-0001.0009), rendered on request (R4), and the *Open
   issues* section leaves the ticket format. The owner stops meaning the record holding the
   deliberation and means the record of the work that answers the question. New evidence against
   the clause: parent decision 30 makes tickets a practice a project may swap, so an argument kept
   in one leaves with it; an entry may be born without an owner, and q-0016.0001, q-0016 and q-0017
   were, their arguments scattered over session records and other tickets; and the body costs
   the window nothing, since a script draws it from the parts alone (parent decisions 26 and 27).
   Refused: keeping decision 2, whose one gain — the argument read inline in the ticket — a render
   gives back. Outside this slice: the arguments of closed questions, which the tickets'
   *Decisions landed* sections hold today and which by the same reasoning belong to the closed
   entries.
4. **A ticket names its question in an `Answers` header field** *(the user, 2026-10-03)*: `-
   **Answers:** [q-N](../questions/q-N-….md)`, the question its goal answers — its *what for*
   (parent decisions 23 and 52). The ticket's open questions sit under that entry, and a child
   ticket's under its parent's. `tickets.py --check` holds that every live ticket has the field,
   that it links an entry, and that the entry's owner is the ticket. Decisive: the owner cannot
   say it — 01-0010 owns q-0018.0015, one of its open questions, not its goal — and decision 23 asked
   for the *what for* to be read in the ticket. Refused: deriving it as the topmost entry the
   ticket owns, which breaks on a ticket owning unrelated questions and stays invisible in the
   ticket; the names `Serves` and `Question`.
5. **The fold is drafted at `/plan` and reviewed by family** *(the user, 2026-10-03)*. The RFC
   holds one table per family of tickets — 01-0010, 01-0011, 01-0014 to 01-0020 — each ticket with
   its `Answers` question and its place in the tree, each bullet with its kind and its
   destination: an entry's question and parent, a line in `What to build`, or removal with its
   reason. The user corrects a table in place, one family at a time, and `/implement` writes what
   the tables say. An entry the store already holds takes its body from the bullet that argues
   it. Decisive: a ticket's *what for* is load-bearing phrasing, the person's by parent decisions
   51 and 52, and every slice of this work is HITL (parent decision 20). Refused: an interview
   ticket by ticket, some sixty rounds with the tree's shape unseen until the end.
6. **A question's id says where it sits, and stays true** *(the user, 2026-10-03, at this
   ticket's plan; amends parent decision 24)*. A root is `q-NNNN`; a question under `q-P` is
   `q-P.NNNN`, as a ticket's id nests under its parent's. A re-parent or an insertion `--between`
   renames the moved subtree in the same call — files, links across the tree, ids inside entries,
   the sessions file, and bare ids in the docs' prose — all mechanically; *part of* stays the line
   the check holds the id against. No forwarding of old ids: a call naming one is refused, writes
   nothing, and says to draw the window again, which every host's next message brings anyway. A
   path that would not fit the platform's limit cuts the slug at a word boundary, never the id.
   Occasion: the store's listing read as a flat list, with some sixty entries about to arrive.
   Refused: an id minted where the question was born and kept after a re-parent, as tickets keep
   theirs (`01-0010.0185` sits under `.0160`), which leaves the listing lying after every move;
   nested directories, refused already by parent decision 34; a forwarding record, which repairs a
   refusal that is already safe. *Amended at `.0018`'s verify (the user, 2026-10-03), on the store's
   own rename:* an id inside a fenced block is an example and is never rewritten — the rename had
   turned a done RFC's sample entry into one naming real questions. *Then, the same day:* a new
   id never collides with an entry, live or in `done/`; an id a rename freed may be given again,
   since renames are rare. The one risk kept — a stale id in another session's context landing
   silently on the question its id went to — is deferred as q-0001.0013; when it strikes, the
   remedy is designed afresh, not taken from the `formerly` part considered here. *And again,
   at this ticket's fold (the user, 2026-10-03):* a child takes the position after its siblings,
   stepping by one — `.0001`, `.0002` — not by ten as ticket positions do, since a question's
   place among its siblings orders nothing and a gap is never filled; the ids the store already
   held renumbered once.

## What to build

- The entry admits a body after its parts, and the owner's meaning becomes the record of the work
  that answers the question — in the format, the parser and `assign`'s wording (decision 3).
- Each open issue read from its ticket and written as an entry of the store, its argument the
  entry's body, owned by the ticket — or, for a pointer, by the record it pointed to — under
  the question its owner answers or with the relation the issue states; a bullet that is not an
  unresolved question is sorted to its home (decision 1).
- Every ticket names the question it answers in its `Answers` field — its *what for* — when it is
  minted, and a ticket that names none is reported *(parent decisions 23 and 52; decision 4)*.
- `/ticket`'s *hold the decision tree in the ticket* and its two straw dogs bound to this slice
  rewritten: an open question's evidence and alternatives are held in its entry (decision 3).
- `TICKET-FORMAT` loses the *Open issues* section; a ticket's open questions are rendered on
  request from the store (decision 3). P7 reworded against the store, with parent decision 50's
  clause: landing a decision repoints every entry closed against it.
- A closure that finishes a subtree moves it to `docs/questions/done/` in the same call, links
  repaired as the mover repairs them; `/maintain`'s move stays as the sweep *(parent decision 34,
  amended 2026-10-03)*.
- A reconciliation: the open issues read from the tickets before the fold and the entries the
  fold wrote are one set, each argument carried whole; and, kept as the guard afterwards, no live
  ticket holds an *Open issues* section.
