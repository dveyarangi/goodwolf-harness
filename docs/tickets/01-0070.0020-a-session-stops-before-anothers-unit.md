## What to build

- **The stop.** A turn that would finish, close, commit, continue or duplicate a unit another
  running session's row names settles nothing on it: the reply says whose the unit is and where the
  two touch, brings two ways to act with a recommendation, and nothing is done on the unit until
  the person names one. The weight is in the refusal to proceed by default, not in prose *(the
  user, 2026-10-24)*. *Serious* is not judged: a held unit stops, an unheld one does not. A shared
  position with no work on the unit stays the 🪢 row. The stop holds whatever `debug` says, since a
  tree with debug off must keep it. It is one rule of the session mechanism in the entry file, read
  off the window, which reaches the session with every message, so it stops every step, free
  conversation included *(the user, 2026-10-25)*; where the window does not reach the agent, the
  refusal at the hold is the stop.
- **The two ways.** This session takes the unit over by the person's word, acted on without a
  second ask once said, and the holder's row keeps the mark of who took it, so a holder still alive
  learns its unit is gone — from its window, and from a message where the host offers one; the
  session mechanism manages every session from any one of them *(the user, 2026-10-25)*. Or a
  sub-task is cut through `/ticket` and taken here, the rest left with the holder.
- **A new unit lets the old go.** A hold at a step's start, like a takeover, lets go the unit the
  session held and names it, now nobody's *(the user, 2026-10-25)*.
- **The judge is a subagent.** The one who built a thing does not judge it; where that applies — a
  ticket's criterion is one case — the builder starts a subagent, gives it what is to be judged and
  where its evidence stands, never its own conclusion, and closes on the verdict *(the user,
  2026-10-25)*; what a new chat receives, the subagent reads by running the wake itself. A principle in the entry file's
  general rules, waiting on the pass that goes over produced work; TICKET-FORMAT names the judge's
  role and holds no instruction.
- **A unit an ended row still holds** is marked taken when another session holds it, so the ended
  chat, reopened, finds the mark and does not hold it beside its new holder.
- **The bias.** The entry file's general rules name the agent's default to do as much as it can
  itself, and that work another session holds is not done without the person's word *(the user,
  2026-10-23)*, worded through `/skill-up`.
- **The session mechanism's doc** says sessions cannot call one another; a host that can message
  makes that untrue, and the doc says what the method relies on and what is the host's.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] Asked to verify, close, finish or commit a unit another running session holds, in a step or
      in free conversation, a session stops with the two ways and does nothing on the unit — tried
      live, read by a subagent judge from both sessions' rows and windows — 2026-10-25, three cold
      cases, close, verify in a step and continue, each stopped and wrote nothing, judged pass by a
      fresh subagent; finish, commit and a live run of two sessions not tried.
- [ ] Told to take a held unit over, a session takes it without asking again, letting go and naming
      any unit it held, and the holder's window shows who took it; the holder's next hold, release
      or end clears the mark.
- [ ] A holder whose window shows its unit taken stops work on it and says so, and the taking
      session commits the unit's work, the holder's uncommitted changes to it included.
- [ ] What needs a judge is judged by a subagent its builder starts, briefed with what is judged
      and its evidence and not the builder's conclusion, and the builder closes on it.
- [ ] The stop stands with `debug` off.
- [ ] The entry file's general rules carry the bias principle and the judge's.
- [ ] `/verify` passes against this ticket, its RFC and the governing docs.
