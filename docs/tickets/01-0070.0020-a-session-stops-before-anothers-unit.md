## What to build

- **The stop.** A turn that would finish, close, commit, continue or duplicate a unit another
  running session's row names settles nothing on it: the reply says whose the unit is and where the
  two touch, brings three ways to act with a recommendation, and nothing is done on the unit until
  the person names one. The weight is in the refusal to proceed by default, not in prose *(the
  user, 2026-10-24)*. *Serious* is not judged: a held unit stops, an unheld one does not. A shared
  position with no work on the unit stays the 🪢 row. The stop holds whatever `debug` says, since a
  tree with debug off must keep it.
- **The three ways.** The holder releases the unit and this session takes it; this session judges
  it, the verdict written into the unit's record and the close left to the holder, as
  TICKET-FORMAT's *Acceptance criteria* already says; or a sub-task is cut through `/ticket` and
  taken here, the rest left with the holder.
- **The handover.** What it carries — the unit, what was done or verified with its evidence, what
  is left, who holds it now — is written into the unit's record. A message, where the host can send
  one, wakes the holder to act on it and points at the record; where it cannot, the session that
  wrote the verdict closes, since a verdict written in the tree is the close *(the user,
  2026-10-23)*. A holder whose row has ended holds nothing.
- **The bias.** The entry file's general rules name the agent's default to do as much as it can
  itself, and that work another holds is handed over, not done *(the user, 2026-10-23)*, worded
  through `/skill-up`.
- **The session mechanism's doc** says sessions cannot call one another; a host that can message
  makes that untrue, and the doc says what the method relies on and what is the host's.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Asked to close, finish or commit a unit another running session holds, a session stops with
      the three ways and does nothing on the unit — tried live, read by a judge who did not build
      it, from the transcript.
- [ ] Told to judge, it writes its verdict into the unit's record and closes nothing; told the
      holder releases, the holder's row lets the unit go and this session's takes it.
- [ ] Where the host cannot reach the holder, the checking session closes on its own verdict.
- [ ] The stop stands with `debug` off.
- [ ] The entry file's general rules carry the bias principle.
- [ ] `/verify` passes against this ticket, its RFC and the governing docs.
