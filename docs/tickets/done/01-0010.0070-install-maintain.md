## Planning finding — interruption and recovery

**2026-09-06, `/plan`:** an isolated filesystem probe of the selected source mover failed
on the second move, leaving the ticket at its destination, the RFC at its original path,
and the queue pointing at the old ticket path. [Evidence](../../research/maintenance-findings.md).
The original one-pass agreement did not specify interruption/recovery behavior.

~~Whether the first version uses full preflight, then stops and reports
partial progress on an I/O failure for maintainer recovery, or requires persisted recovery
and rollback machinery.~~ **2026-09-06, user: full preflight, then stop and report partial
progress on failure, with maintainer recovery and no automatic rollback.** This bounds the
first version without claiming atomic multi-file writes. The maintainer completes recovery
under the existing repair policy before reporting a successful close. The current contract
is in [architecture](../../architecture.md#interruption-and-recovery).

**2026-09-06, `/plan` validation:** the RFC incorporates the decision, source adaptations,
behavioral proof cases and fresh-session discovery requirement. No remaining design decision
blocks implementation; delivery criteria remain unchecked until their evidence exists.

**2026-09-06, second `/plan` validation — repair and report:** the RFC allowed direct writes
that can truncate unrelated record content on failure, exempted absolute references even
when they target the moved record, and left nested-block removal ambiguous. These conflict
with [reference and content preservation](../../architecture.md#paired-record-close) and
[surviving temporary agreements](../../architecture.md#temporary-statements), under
[mechanical maintenance](../../process.md#mechanical-maintenance).
Amended the architecture and RFC to preserve originals through failed content replacement,
retarget internal absolute references, and refuse parent removal until children have a
disposition. The source truncation probe and expected behavioral tests are recorded;
documentation checks validate the repaired plan. Implementation proof remains outstanding.
The accepted first pair and stop/report/recover contract remain unchanged.

**2026-09-06, user:** ~~treat the per-file replacement technique as a maintenance architecture
requirement.~~ **Keep correct transformations and failure reporting as `/maintain`'s
responsibility; leave the writing technique to helper implementation and use Git as the
recovery fallback.** Git can restore available committed or staged versions, but generally
cannot recover overwritten unstaged edits. The architecture and RFC now state that boundary
without prescribing a replacement protocol or adding recovery machinery. Reference repairs
and protection of active nested statements remain correctness requirements.
