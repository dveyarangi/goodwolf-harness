## What to build

*Rewritten 2026-09-06 by this ticket's `/align`; see Decisions below for what changed and why.*

- `/mechanism` installed under `.agents/skills/mechanism/`, carrying **the shape**: the membership
  test, the three homes and the instruction/doc/evidence chain, the directory layout, the injection
  and retraction rules, the moments taxonomy, and how to incept and amend. These are read while
  incepting or amending a mechanism, which is the work this skill governs, so this is their only
  home. Its rules sections name a target, an anchor and a tier, and each rule states its authority
  inline.
- `.agents/mechanisms/mechanism-shape/` with its doc, declaring `/mechanism` itself. The doc names
  every part, marking each owned or relied-on with its owner, and states that this mechanism is
  **always on** — no lifecycle scripts, and that absence is a property rather than a gap.
- Its evidence at `docs/mechanisms/mechanism-shape.evidence.md`, named by the doc from inside a
  `<project-local>` block. Core carries the convention, not the path.
- A moments table in the doc: one row per occasion at which a person acts on this mechanism, each
  carrying its instruction or which kind of absence it is — `elsewhere`, `embedded`, `unowned by
  design`, or `not yet`. A `not yet` names a ticket that exists.
- ~~`.agents/README.md` becomes the register, with the shared shape outside a `<project-local>`
  block and this project's rows inside one.~~ **Superseded 2026-09-07: the register is derived.**
  `.agents/mechanisms/*/` is the enumeration, and `mechanisms.py --index` renders it on request.
  `.agents/README.md` keeps loaders, layout and symlink recovery — all of which travel — and its
  install narrative goes, being a copy of the queue and the tickets. It needs no `<project-local>`
  block. `AGENTS.md`'s `Installed:` line is deleted in the same pass.
- A check that the declaration is true, shipped with this slice rather than deferred: named parts
  exist, doc and instruction file are different files, every `not yet` names a real ticket, and
  every register row resolves. A shape asserted and unverified is the defect this spec exists to
  stop.

## Decisions this ticket's align owns

what it returned amended the parent spec, and those amendments are recorded there.

- ~~What counts as a part here. `move_doc.py` is used by `/ticket` and `/maintain`; several users,
  exactly one claimant, and this ticket says which.~~
  **The fork was wrong as posed, and dissolved twice.** First: claimants are mechanisms, not
  skills, so two skills of one mechanism never contest a part. Then the user's rule — **a mechanism
  is one skill** — removed the premise entirely: `/maintain` is not a part of any mechanism it
  maintains, it is the mechanism *for* maintaining mechanisms and acts from outside. Life then
  settled the residue: *the constraint is on the scripts, not on the doc*. A part may be claimed by
  several mechanisms, docs name relied-on parts freely with their owner, and uniqueness bites only
  where a script installs, removes or extracts. This harness has no such script, so ownership of
  `docs_corpus.py` and `tests/harness.py` is not a live question — both are declared relied-on.

- ~~Whether the evidence sidecar is a new file or whether an installed skill's existing
  `EVIDENCE.md` already does that job.~~
  **Answered by a rule rather than a placement** (the user): *the doc is the instruction's why; the
  evidence is the doc's why.* Evidence therefore belongs to a doc, never to an instruction file,
  and there is one per mechanism, beside its doc. Life left this open and flagged it for its
  operator; the chain dissolves the question. **Placement, settled separately** (the user): a
  mechanism's evidence lives in `docs/mechanisms/<slug>.evidence.md`, not in core — it is the one part of a
  mechanism that cites this project's own records — and the doc names it from inside a
  `<project-local>` block. Consequence: `.agents/skills/discover/EVIDENCE.md` and
  `docs/research/maintenance-findings.md` are unsorted mixtures in the wrong homes — sorted and
  moved when their mechanisms are declared, not here.

- ~~Which absences paired close actually carries today, and which of them are `not yet` rather than
  `elsewhere` or `embedded`.~~
  **The subject changed and so did the instrument.** The first mechanism is `/mechanism`, not
  paired close, on the user's decision: the mechanism that decides what counts as one is declared
  by its own shape, and `/maintain` then derives the second and `/ticket` the third. And the
  taxonomy attaches to **moments**, not to three properties — Life tried the whole-mechanism
  verdict and abandoned it, because *"M03 answered `none` while one of its four moments was
  instructed and three were not."* The honest absences are enumerated per moment when the doc is
  written.

Two decisions taken here that the spec now carries: **one instruction file per mechanism**, and
**the register is `.agents/README.md`** rather than a new file under `docs/`.

### Resolved 2026-09-07, during this ticket's `/implement`

- **The membership test names its subject** *(the user, 2026-09-07)*. A mechanism is part of **how
  the work gets done** — the development method and its machinery — never what the project
  produces. The user's question found it by asking what the tier-1 trigger would do in a recipient
  project asked to build a feature: *"mechanism only describes harness self-improvement, not any
  behaviour."* The spec's *reliance, not code* had been carrying the whole boundary, and it does
  not travel to the occasion — `AGENTS.md`'s line does, and it said *before building or amending a
  mechanism*, with **Mechanism** defined in the glossary as *"a behavior on which other work
  relies"*. On that reading a forecast pipeline other services depend on is a mechanism. Landed in
  three places: [the spec](../../spec/01-0011-mechanism-shape.md)'s membership test,
  [the glossary](../../../.agents/glossary.md)'s **Mechanism** — which is where the leak actually
  was — and the trigger, now *"Changing how the work is done — a skill, a check, a record, the
  loop — is mechanism work: use `/mechanism`. Building what the project produces is not."*
  **The boundary is drawn at the subject, not at this repository:** a recipient project's own
  development machinery is a mechanism and owes a declaration; only its product is out. That is
  the wider of the two readings of *harness self-improvement*, taken because a harness meant to be
  adopted must let a recipient declare its own release process the same way.

- **The skill body was too chewed, and the cure was its own chain** *(the user, 2026-09-07)*. The
  first draft explained each rule where it stated it. But *the doc is the instruction's why*, so
  rationale in the instruction file is this mechanism failing to hold its own shape on the first
  file it wrote. Cut roughly a third; what went is the material stage 3's doc carries anyway.
  No rule was dropped.

- **The header declares `state`; nothing declares the rules file** *(the user, 2026-09-07)*. Two
  bullets were added during stage 1 that the RFC's skeleton did not have, and stage 2's probe made
  the difference between them visible. **`state` stays**, with a closed vocabulary of `always on`
  and `installed` — the bullet is the RFC's, the vocabulary is this pass's, and it is load-bearing
  because it is the only thing telling a reader which way to read a table titled *Install adds,
  uninstall removes*. **The `rules` bullet goes.** `MECHANISM-FORMAT` already fixes the rules file
  as `<slug>.rules.md` beside the doc, so whether a mechanism injects is derivable by looking, and
  a bullet restating it can only disagree with the directory. That is the register argument one
  level down — *what will read it, isn't the script simply deriving it?* — and it applies the same
  way. The diagnostic changed with it: not *the bullet does not resolve*, but **a file in the
  directory under a name neither the doc nor the rules file accounts for**.
  **Its authority was corrected the same day** *(the user, 2026-09-07)*, on the question of whether
  this pass understood that a rules file is machine input read by the installer alone. It does; the
  justification did not follow from it. The diagnostic had been reasoned from the installer's
  lookup, which is the installer's own *refuse rather than guess* contract and belongs to
  [.0020](01-0011.0020-rules-one-home.md) — a second underwriting of it here is the duplication
  the mechanism exists to stop, and it reasons from a script that does not exist. The rule stands
  instead on the spec's *working parts stay where the harness needs them*: the directory holds the
  doc and the rules file, and the user's own statement of why it deserves a warning — **a file
  dropped there was dropped there to be read, and nothing reads it**. Same diagnostic, real
  authority.
  Taken with it: `/mechanism`'s body **loses the rules file's field list** — *one section per rule,
  each naming its target, its anchor and its tier* — which [.0020](01-0011.0020-rules-one-home.md)

  slice that builds the parser states it too, is the doc/check seam's failure repeated one file

### Third pass — 2026-09-07, after an amendment to the shape

**The shape gained a rule** *(the user, 2026-09-07)*: **everything a mechanism produces is read by
someone, or the doc says why nobody does.** It landed in all three homes at once — the rule in
`/mechanism`'s body, a required *What it produces, and who reads it* section in
`MECHANISM-FORMAT`, and a diagnostic in the check. Written in that order, so the check refused the
one existing declaration before the section that answers it was written; both polarities watched,
as the shape requires of its own amendments. Set green at 112, `--check` clean.

It generalises the stray-file diagnostic rather than sitting beside it. That one asks *does
anything read this file in a mechanism's directory*; this asks the same question of everything a
mechanism emits, wherever it lands. The narrow case is now an instance of the rule.

**Two findings, both from applying the new rule as a lens:**

- **`--index` had a reader but no moment.** Writing the produces section forced the sentence *read
  on request by someone asking what is declared* — which describes a person acting on the
  mechanism, and the moments table had no row for it. Folded into the existing `unowned by design`
  row rather than added beside it, since the reason is identical and a second row with the same
  clause is duplication: finding one doc and enumerating them all are the same act at two grains.
- **The check then caught the repair.** The reworded row carried a backticked `--index`, which the
  parser reads as a referent, and `unowned by design` may carry none. The diagnostic fired on the
  hand that had just written it. Reworded without the code span.

**What the rule found nowhere else.** Every other artifact this slice produced already had a named
reader: the skill and its shelf are read doing mechanism work, the two glossaries by the citations
re-aimed at each, the evidence at amend time, the tests by the runner, the check's report by
`/verify`. The one output with no automatic consumer is the index, and the declaration now says so
in the same breath as naming it — that it is rendered on request, that nothing runs it unasked, and
that this is deliberate rather than an omission.

**Noted, not repaired.** The third criterion asks for a doc that *names every part with its owner*.
The RFC then split parts into two tables, where owned parts carry no owner column because the
mechanism owns them and only relied-on parts name an owner. The declaration follows the RFC; the
criterion's wording predates that split and reads as though one table were expected. No work is
missing, and the wording is the parent's to reconcile if it recurs.
