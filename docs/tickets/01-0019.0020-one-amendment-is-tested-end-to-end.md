## What to build

First of six slices, the tracer bullet: one case through every part. The case is rule failure 53's
— a discovery came back and the next reply put a decision to the person without telling what it
found — with `/discover`'s text before and after its amendment as the standing and the new.

- The case is written in `docs/`, holding what the accepted design says a case holds: the
  situation, what each mode should give, its yes-or-no questions, the skills and the failure it
  belongs to.
- The run stages the harness apart, the cases out of its reach, and reaches the host through an
  adapter for Claude Code: lay the harness out as the host's files, run, read the record back,
  check health before anything is spent. Headless in a staged directory rather than through
  subagents, since a subagent reads the entry file it inherited, not the one on disk; the person's
  own user-level instructions and memory are kept out of the staging.
- Three runs in each of the three modes; a judge blind to the mode, each question first tried on a
  right and a wrong answer written by hand; a judge's failure left out, never scored.
- The result is one of four verdicts — helped, hurt, no observed effect, confounded — with the
  quotes behind each yes, recorded beside the case.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The case of rule failure 53 stands in `docs/`, and its questions pass their right-and-wrong trial before the first run.
- [ ] A run reports, for each mode, the three runs' answers with their quotes and a verdict: the case fails on the standing `/discover` and passes on the amended one — or the verdict says otherwise and why.
- [ ] A staged run reads neither the cases nor the person's own instructions and memory, seen in its record by someone who did not build it.
- [ ] The run's reported cost matches the count of runs and judges it made.
- [ ] `/verify` passes against this ticket, its RFC and the governing docs.

## Out of scope

Other cases, the subset, the audit, a second host and the rule that every amendment is tested — the slices after this one.
