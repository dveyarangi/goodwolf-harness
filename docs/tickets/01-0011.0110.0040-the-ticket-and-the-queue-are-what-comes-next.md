## What to build

- The ticket format admits the first kind alone: the header, `What to build`, the criteria, the
  parent and scope sections; the decisions section and the narrative go. A decision lands from
  the question's body into its durable home before the ticket closes; the story goes to evidence
  or a session record; the ticket links.
- The whiteboard is the body of the question the task stands on: the body's format gains a
  decisions section the mover carries, and a `close` refuses while a decision in the body cites
  no durable home.
- `/align`'s three rules that target the ticket — land and rewrite, open on what the ticket is,
  sweep — are retargeted to the question's body; the ticket is minted from it once the form is
  seen.
- The queue holds the order, the candidate and what follows what, hand-written; its table and
  counts are rendered from the ticket headers, a hand edit to the rendered part reported; the
  completed-step narrative becomes one pointer at the last step and its session record; the
  ordering rule stays a straw dog until a mechanism owns it.
- Core owns the ticket's contract — what a unit of agreed work must state, and that done is
  every criterion holding with verification among them — and the file is its default form; a
  project's board is admitted where it reads as the contract. The contract and the seam only;
  no adapter.
- Records written before this slice are brought to their kind at their next touch, by
  `/maintain`; the queue first.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A live ticket minted after the format moved holds no section the format no longer admits,
      and one minted before is not reported for it.
- [ ] The queue's table equals the ticket headers it is rendered from, and a hand edit to it is
      reported; its hand-written part holds the order and the candidate only.
- [ ] A `close` on a question whose body holds a decision with no home is refused naming the
      decision; one whose decisions all cite a home closes — both tested.
- [ ] `/align`'s rules name the question's body, not the ticket, as where a resolution is
      recorded and swept.
- [ ] The ticket's contract is stated in one place core owns, and names what a substitute board
      must read as.
- [ ] `/verify` has been run on this ticket against the spec and the governing docs.
