# A straw dog is bound to the question it waits on

- **Status:** In progress (implemented 2026-10-04; `/verify` next)
- **Type:** HITL
- **Plan:** [the RFC](../rfc/01-0011.0100.0030-a-straw-dog-is-bound-to-the-question-it-waits-on.md)
- **Depends on:** [01-0011.0100.0010 the-store-and-questions-writes-it](./done/01-0011.0100.0010-the-store-and-questions-writes-it.md)
  (the store); [01-0011.0100.0018 a-question-id-says-where-it-sits](./done/01-0011.0100.0018-a-question-id-says-where-it-sits.md)
  (the rename the bindings follow); [01-0011.0100.0020 open-issues-are-entries-of-the-store](./done/01-0011.0100.0020-open-issues-are-entries-of-the-store.md)
  (every ticket's question in `Answers`)
- **Answers:** [q-0001.0019](../questions/q-0001.0019-how-is-a-straw-dog-bound-to-the-question-it-waits-on.md)
- **Outcome:** Every straw dog in the tree is bound to the question of the store whose answer
  will rewrite its text, and to no ticket; it comes due, by a script, when that question's answer
  has left the work that owns it; and core names no ticket anywhere.

## Decisions landed at this ticket's align

Opened 2026-10-03.

1. **A straw dog is not a question; it is provisional text bound to one** *(the user,
   2026-10-03)*. The binding runs from the straw dog to a question of the store, and the question's
   `owner` names the work that answers it — a ticket here, whatever record a method keeps
   elsewhere. Today's chain, straw dog → ticket → the ticket's `Answers`, routes core through the
   ticket method, which decision 30 makes swappable, and the binding is the one place core names a
   ticket. Refuted: a straw dog as an entry of its own — the reading 01-0012's align filled in by
   analogy with Rust's unresolved questions and its `/impact` repeated as fact, never put to the
   user alone; on 2026-10-03, 43 of the 104 straw dogs waited only for their ticket to close and 18
   wrapped *Mechanism: not yet*, text that waits on work, with nothing to decide. Amends
   decision 38 of [the parent](./01-0011.0100-the-open-questions-are-kept-by-a-mechanism.md).
2. **A straw dog binds to the question whose answer will rewrite its text, and carries no
   condition of its own** *(the user, 2026-10-03)*. It is due when that question closes. A
   narrower `until` is rewritten as the question behind it, an existing entry where the store
   holds one: most narrow conditions name a milestone of the work — *01-0017 declares the commit
   mechanism*, *a listing exists* — and the question behind *mechanism X is declared* is *what
   does mechanism X own?*, not *is X declared?*, which is a status. Refuted: keeping `until` as a
   refinement beside the question, which leaves the narrow conditions to be judged by hand —
   `/maintain`'s C2, *the tool never interprets an `until`* — exactly where the condition is finer
   than the ticket.
3. **A straw dog is due when its question is closed and the answer has left the work that owns
   it** *(the user, 2026-10-03)*: the entry's `answer` links to a file other than its `owner`'s.
   Decision 50 of the parent already makes the step observable — a question is closed against a
   ticket's decision while the decision has not landed, and repointed to the decision's durable
   home when it lands — so the script reads dueness from the two links of one entry, knowing
   nothing of tickets. A link left unrepointed is a breach of P7, which keeps the straw dog from
   coming due until it is repaired. Refuted: due on any closure, which reports text still true
   between the decision and its landing — *Mechanism: not yet* until the mechanism is declared; a
   new state between decided and landed, which adds a part and a call for what the two links
   already say. *Extended at `/plan` (the user, 2026-10-04), per closure kind:* `decided` as
   above, and with no owner any cited answer is elsewhere; `moot` and `pruned` are due, since the
   question no longer stands; `merged` and `superseded` are never due — the binding follows the
   successor, and the listing says *rebind to q-N*; `deferred`, open and suspect are never due.
4. **The binding is the question's id, and a rename rewrites every binding** *(the user,
   2026-10-03)*: `<straw-dog question="q-N">`, and in code a `TODO` naming `q-N`. A re-parent
   renames a subtree's ids (`.0018`), and the rename's bare-id pass reaches only `docs/`, while 87 of
   the 104 straw dogs sit in core; the listing knows where every binding is, so the rename rewrites
   those exactly, and the check reports a binding to an id the store does not hold. *Core and
   instance*'s clause becomes: core names a question only in a straw dog's binding, and no ticket
   anywhere. The syntax was out of this ticket's scope as the entry file's; decision 1 cannot land
   without it. Refuted: the id with the question's words, checked against the entry — long, and
   every rewording carried to each binding; a link to the entry's file — its path breaks most
   often, and names a particular record under `docs/` from core.
5. **The rebinding is drafted at `/plan` and reviewed by family** *(the user, 2026-10-03)*, as
   `.0020`'s fold was: the RFC holds one table per family of tickets, each straw dog's place with
   the question it binds to — an existing entry, or a new one with its words and its parent — and
   the user corrects and accepts a family at a time; `/implement` writes what the tables say. The
   43 straw dogs that waited only for their ticket are shown too. Decisive: this align twice found a
   condition the agent had phrased that was not a real question — *a straw dog is a question*, *is
   mechanism X declared?* — and a question's words and place are the user's (parent decisions 51
   and 52). Refused: one table for the 48 narrow conditions alone, the rest rebound unseen.
6. **A straw dog's question needs no owner** *(the user, 2026-10-03)*: the entry file's duty
   becomes *find the question, or open one*. An owner is a record, not someone at work: on
   2026-10-03, 28 straw dogs had waited on 01-0017, Planned, since 2026-09-06, and 6 on
   01-0011.0040, Ready, since the same day. What lets provisional text become permanent is an open
   question nobody reaches, with straw dogs or without — the store's risk, not this slice's, held
   as q-0001.0018 "How is an open question that nobody reaches kept from sinking unseen?". Refused: an owner required, a formal guarantee that does not prevent the wait and
   makes wrapping cost a work record.

Landed at `/plan`, 2026-10-04:

7. **A narrow `until` becomes its own question; an existing entry serves only where it is the
   same question** *(the user, 2026-10-04)*. A straw dog that waited only for its ticket binds to
   the ticket's `Answers` question; any other `until` is already a question written as a
   condition — *the parts table says what goes and something says how* is *what does removing one
   mechanism take out of a tree, and what does the removing?* — and binding it to a broader entry,
   as the RFC's first draft bound it to q-0018.0013 "How does a project remove the harness?",
   loses what decision 2 keeps: the question whose answer rewrites this text.
8. **A `not yet` row's reason is the words of the question it waits on** *(the user,
   2026-10-04)*, read from the store by the declaration check; a reason written in the row's body
   stays stray. With decision 7 the reason loses nothing — it is the old `until`, phrased as the
   question it was. Refused: a reason in the body beside the binding, one thought written twice
   that drifts apart on the first rename.
9. **A question closed against a decision that has not landed is first assigned to the record
   that holds it** *(the user, 2026-10-04)*, so decision 3 can tell decided from landed: with no
   owner, any cited answer reads as elsewhere, and an ownerless S-*x* closed at 01-0017's align
   against that ticket's decision would bring its *Mechanism: not yet* due — and the declaration
   check red — before the mechanism is declared. A rule for the agent in P7 and the questions
   skill; decision 6 stands, since opening a question and wrapping text still need no owner.
   Refused: `close decided` refusing an ownerless entry, which forbids closing straight against a
   durable home; an ownerless decided question never due, which strands its straw dogs.

## What to build

- The syntax: `<straw-dog question="q-N">`, `until` and `ticket` gone, and in code a `TODO` naming
  `q-N` (decision 4); the listing reads it, and its check reports a binding to an id the store
  does not hold.
- Dueness: the listing reports a straw dog due when its question is closed and the entry's
  `answer` links to a file other than its `owner`'s (decision 3); the wake lists the due ones
  among the deferrals to re-check.
- The rename: a re-parent rewrites every binding to the ids it renames, wherever the listing
  finds them, core included (decision 4).
- The rebinding of every straw dog in the tree, by the tables `/plan` drafts and the user
  accepts family by family (decision 5): a binding to the ticket's `Answers` question where it
  waited only for its ticket; otherwise the question behind its condition, existing or new.
- The texts: the entry file's *Straw dogs* — find the question, or open one (decision 6) — and
  *Core and instance*, core naming a question only in a straw dog's binding and no ticket
  anywhere; the glossary's *Straw dog*; `/maintain`'s C2 and T rules; the questions doc's parts
  table.

## Acceptance criteria — firmed at the align, 2026-10-03

- [ ] Every straw dog in the tree binds to a question the store holds, by id, and none names a
  ticket; the listing's check reports a binding that names a ticket, an `until`, or an id the
  store does not hold.
- [ ] A straw dog is reported due exactly as decision 3 says for each closure kind, a binding to
  a merged or superseded question is reported as *rebind to q-N*, and the wake lists the due ones.
- [ ] A re-parent rewrites every binding to a renamed id, in core and in `docs/`.
- [ ] The rebinding tables, one per family, are in the RFC and each is accepted by the user; the
  tree's straw dogs bind as the tables say, and none was lost — the set before and after is one,
  place for place.
- [ ] Nothing under core names a ticket.
- [ ] P7 and the questions skill say that a question closed against a decision that has not
  landed is first assigned to the record holding it (decision 9).
- [ ] `/verify` has been run on this ticket against its ticket, RFC, and governing docs.
