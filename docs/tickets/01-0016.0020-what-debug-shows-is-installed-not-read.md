## What to build

The `debugging` mechanism's rules file holds everything a tree shows the person about its own
working, sent to the files that now say it under `debug=on`, each rule conditioned on `debug`: on,
a tree shows it; off, the same files carry its plain counterpart. One text never holds both readings behind a
switch a session reads *(the user, 2026-10-18)*, because a reader who must evaluate the switch pays
for both readings and can take the wrong one.

What moves is every straw dog bound to q-0037.0009.0021 on 2026-10-20 that says what shows under
`debug=on`, as `straw_dogs.py` lists them:

- the entry file's announce line, and the harness doc's two mentions of the session announcing it;
- the reply form's parts it gives to `debug=on`: the 💡 🕯️ 無 marks and the footnotes, 🐛 and the
  stop's `(🐛 <ladder>=<rung>)`, the ⚙️ row of skills read, and the 🪶 commits table with the
  counters line, placed as rule failure 72's amendment places them;
- *A reply speaks the method*'s last sentence;
- the two scripts' comments that call their lines debugging's — `questions.py`'s landed `at` line
  and the announce line `harness.py` stamps: neither script reads `debug`, each prints always, and
  what reaches the person is the installed text's to say *(the user, 2026-10-25,
  q-0037.0009.0021.0001.0003 closed moot)*.

Not moved *(the user, 2026-10-25, q-0037.0009.0021's decision 3)*: the ✏️ 🧩 ⚓ frames, which
the autonomy dial shows, not debugging, though v48 put them under the marks' `debug=on`; and the
🪢 table, the session mechanism's S4, already a rule conditioned `when debug=on`. The reply form's
parts that stand debug or not — the frames, the ⚖️ and 🍂 tables, the head table and its 🔁 row —
stay where they are, bound for the voice mechanism by q-0037.0009.0021's decision 4.

Debugging carries both readings as a pair, exactly one of them installed at a time *(the user,
2026-10-25, q-0037.0009.0021.0001.0002)*: **D2**, `when debug=off`, a reply keeps its machinery
out; **D3**, `when debug=on`, a reply shows it — each a rule in the debugging mechanism's rules
file targeting `AGENTS.md`, worded as shown at the align and written through `/skill-up`:

> **D2** A reply tells nothing of the machinery behind it: what the work read, ran or wrote on the
> way, or will read next — no script, call, check, report, hook, commit or its hash, rule's id,
> skill's name or record's internals.

> **D3** A reply shows the machinery behind it beside the method; an item shown for that alone and
> carrying no glyph of its own opens with 🐛.
>
> - A paragraph that claims or recommends ends with `(💡 [*<the principle governing it, named as
>   its home names it>*](<its home>:<line>))`, the name a link to the line its text stands on,
>   never a rule's id; one following a principle that stands in a document, where no skill brings
>   it into reach, `(🕯️ …)` in the same form — a harness error; rarely, one following a principle
>   the list lacks, `(無 *<that principle>*)`; one with none at work, nothing.
> - What a claim rests on from outside this session — a decision, evidence, the user's word — is a
>   footnote naming its record, or who and when.
> - A stop names its machinery after the sentence that says it, `(🐛 <ladder>=<rung>)`, never on
>   its own.
> - A turn that made commits opens the reply, after the 🪢 table where one stands, with a table
>   `| 🪶 Commits |`, a row each in order: the short hash and the subject's head; then the window's
>   counters line — its glyphs, numbers and changes copied character for character from this
>   turn's window, never words standing for them — a one-cell table of its own. The head table
>   comes after them.
> - A turn that read a skill's instructions closes the head table with a row `| ⚙️ <each such
>   skill, as /name, in the order read> |`.

What D2 says leaves *A reply speaks the method* — *what the work read, ran or wrote on the way, and
what it will read next, is not told* and *it names no script, call, check, report, record's
internals or hook* — and what D3 says leaves the reply form: its last sentence, *and cited by that
name*, every bullet of *A paragraph shows what it stands on* but the frames, the ⚙️ paragraph and the
commit paragraph whole. *Every principle, and every classification applied by judgement, is named
at its authored home* stays plain. `/maintain`'s *what was wrong in it told only under `debug=on`*
is cut, with no rule in its place: a maintain commit's subject names what was wrong, and D3 shows
it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each text listed under *What to build* stands in the debugging mechanism's rules file, and
      reaches its target as that mechanism's installed block.
- [ ] With `debug` on, a session's replies show what they showed under `debug=on` on
      2026-10-20 — read by a judge who did not build it in one real session.
- [ ] With `debug` off, the same session's replies show none of it, and the entry file carries D2
      and nothing of D3 — read by a judge who did not build it, and by the installer's check.
- [ ] Debugging is switched on and off by one stated call, `inject_rules.py --set debug=on|off`,
      made by the agent through `/harness` at the person's ask (q-0037.0009.0021.0001.0001,
      decided by 01-0010.0220); the switch swaps D3 for D2 and back, and neither reading is left
      in the authored text.
- [ ] No script or text in core reads `debug=on`; the two scripts' comments name no debugging.
- [ ] L4 is gone from `local.rules.md` and this tree's settings table carries `debug` `on`; a
      recipient whose local file says `debug=on` as a rule comes out of its next update with the
      setting on and the line gone.
- [ ] No straw dog in the tree is bound to q-0037.0009.0021 for what debug shows; the TODOs in
      `harness.py` and `questions.py` are gone; q-0037.0009.0021's decision 2 no longer says
      *meanwhile*.
- [ ] `/verify` passes against this ticket, its RFC and the governing docs.
