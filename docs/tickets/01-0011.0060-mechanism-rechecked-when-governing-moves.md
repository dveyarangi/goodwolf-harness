## Why this exists, and why it is not this tree's first idea

The re-check rule is already stated three times in this tree as prose — `/mechanism`'s *Amend and
retire*, the spec's *Re-check on governing change*, and `docs/process.md:91` — and nothing gives
*since* a meaning. Life built this mechanism from a specimen: records that carried evolution
inline, against a sidecar rule that arrived after they existed, with nothing scheduling the re-check
of the consuming end once the producing end moved. Its evidence records why the design is what it
is: a mark and not fire-on-edit, because a rule firing at the moment of the edit lands in the busy
session that made the edit, measured at ~8%; a hash and not a count, because the thing to catch is
an edit that bypassed the amend procedure; two levels, because a mechanism's own rules govern its
records the way the meta-rules govern its rules.

Until this lands, `/maintain`'s body carries an interim rule inside a `<temporary>` block bound to
this ticket: every declared mechanism in a pass's scope is due at every pass. With two or three
mechanisms that is two or three docs to reread; it stops being cheap when
[01-0017](./01-0017-io-graph-coherent.md) declares the rest, which is why this sits before it.

## What to build

Life's two levels, taken whole:

| level | due when | and when |
|---|---|---|
| `rules` | the meta-rules moved since the mark | the mechanism's own surfaces were edited outside the amend procedure |
| `output` | the mechanism's own surfaces moved since the mark | its records churned enough since the mark |

- **The governing surface for `rules` is the meta-rules in both their homes**: `/mechanism`'s
  skill directory, and `AGENTS.md` with its `<project-local>` block stripped. Life hashes
  `/mechanism`'s skill alone; here *Core and instance* landed in the entry file on 2026-09-07 and
  made four skills into violators the moment it was written, so a surface excluding the entry file
  would have stayed quiet through a meta-rule moving. The contract version line stays in the
  surface: it changes exactly when the contract does.
- **Hashes normalise line endings, strip installed blocks, and exclude evidence.** Installed blocks
  are policed as injection drift, not content; story does not govern, so story cannot stale; and
  this checkout runs on Windows over an LF repository, where a hash over raw bytes reads every flip
  as an edit.
- **The mark is a record, written only by the closing step of a maintenance**, one row per
  mechanism per level, rewritten in place, bounded by the mechanism set. No mark reads as
  never-maintained, loudly; nobody backfills. It declares its own shape inside its own file and has
  a maintainer script, because a malformed row makes a mechanism silently ineligible rather than
  reporting itself.
- **Dueness is derived at every look and never stored**, so nothing can go stale and no ticket is
  created for it. Where it is looked at is [01-0010.0120](./01-0010.0120-host-delivery-surfaces.md)'s
  answer; until a wake surface exists, the derivation is a command in the verification set, and the
  doc says that this is weaker than reading state at wake.
- **The derivation the clock hashes and the surfaces `mechanisms.py --check` parses are one
  derivation.** Life split them once and the turn report counted different rows from the wake.
- **The suite never writes the live marks**, watched failing.
- **`/maintain`'s `<temporary>` block retires through the enumerator**, and its moments row
  *knowing a re-check is due* and its record obligation stop reading `not yet`.

## Decisions this ticket's align owns

- **Whether Git is the clock.** The doc's last commit against the governing surface's last commit,
  with no marks file, no maintainer script and no churn: narrower by three parts, and this tree
  already depends on Git for its corpus. It reads *doc edited* as *re-checked*, which is weaker
  than a mark only a maintenance can move. The align rules on it first, because everything below
  is moot if it holds.
- **The churn unit.** Life counts distinct sessions whose wake saw the records changed, from a
  hook. This tree has no session identity at wake. Commits touching the records, or the
  `docs/sessions/` count, are the observable candidates; N is this project's number, not Life's.
- **Whether the entry file belongs in the governing surface at the cost of its parser.**
  `docs_corpus.py` parses `<temporary>` attributes and code spans already; stripping a
  `<project-local>` block is new code on a shared module.
- **Where the marks live.** Records are the instance half's; a recipient's marks are theirs and
  a fresh tree has honestly never looked. Life keeps them under `memory/`; this tree has no
  `memory/`.
- **Whether [.0030](./01-0011.0030-archive-backlog-listed.md)'s ratchet and this clock share one
  announcement.** Both are *loud when it rises* with nothing to be loud from.

## Acceptance criteria — provisional, firmed at the align

- [ ] A command reports, for every declared mechanism and each level, whether it is due and why —
  meta-rules moved, own surfaces moved, records churned — or that it has no mark.
- [ ] A mechanism with no mark is reported as never maintained, in words distinct from *fresh*.
- [ ] Moving a mark is one command, refuses a slug the mechanism set does not carry, and is the
  only writer of the marks.
- [ ] Editing `/mechanism`'s skill makes every declared mechanism due at `rules`; editing a
  mechanism's own doc makes it due at `output`; editing its evidence makes nothing due; a line
  ending flip makes nothing due; installing or retracting a block makes nothing due.
- [ ] The marks declare their own shape in their own file, and a maintainer script reports a
  malformed row and leaves a conforming file byte-identical.
- [ ] A full suite run leaves the live marks byte-identical.
- [ ] The clock and `mechanisms.py --check` read the same surfaces through one derivation.
- [ ] `/maintain`'s `<temporary>` block bound to this ticket is gone, and its doc's record
  obligation and *knowing a re-check is due* row are instructed.
- [ ] `/verify` has been run on this ticket against its ticket, RFC, and governing docs.
