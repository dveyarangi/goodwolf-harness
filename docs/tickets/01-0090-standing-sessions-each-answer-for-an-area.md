## What to build

Today nothing in the method says which standing sessions a tree needs, what each answers for, or
when that cut should change, and a session that answers for everything reads everything (the
question's *The problem*). The session mechanism gains a part kept apart, so it can leave as a
mechanism at the cost of a move: the skill `/pack`, the record of the tree's roles with its
format and its check, and a section of the mechanism's doc with the part's moments and parts.

`/pack` acts at the four moments the whole names. It recommends and the person decides: it
starts and ends no session, hands out no work and writes nothing in another role's area, because
the person stays the one judge between areas. A role is its name, the map of what it owns and its
brief; the record names no session, since sessions are lost and records stay. A session taking a
role reads its brief and its own area first, and the install's instruction offers the default
session, never requiring it. The method's glossary defines a role.

The first run is this tree's: its roles record, written from the roles the person named
([q-dyc63i](../questions/q-dyc63i-which-standing-roles-does-this-tree-have-where-every-session-develops-the-harness-itself.md)), and a read of which sessions the person actually runs.

Every instruction's text — the skill's and each rule's — is shown to the person before it lands:
the part changes the way of working.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A fresh session given this morning's collision — two sessions editing the same two
      mechanism docs — and running `/pack` names the overlapping area and a way to separate
      it, untold; judged by someone who did not build it.
- [ ] This tree holds its roles record — the roles the person named, each with its name, its map
      and its brief — and the record's check refuses a role that lacks any of the three.
- [ ] Run over this tree, `/pack` names the sessions the person actually runs and proposes a new
      role only where a collision repeated; the cut it proposes is the person's to judge.
- [ ] A session that takes a role reads its brief and its own area first; what it starts with is
      counted beside a free session's in the same tree, and both numbers are reported.
- [ ] The install's instruction offers the default session, and declining it leaves nothing
      behind; judged on a fresh reader given only what a session would read.
- [ ] The session mechanism's declaration holds the part, and `mechanisms.py --check` passes.
- [ ] The skill's text and each rule's were shown to the person before they landed.
- [ ] `/verify` passes against this ticket and the governing docs.
