# Standing sessions — how this tree built the way of working

A record of what happened, a step per entry, for
[q-ja5mp4](../questions/q-ja5mp4-does-the-method-have-one-session-direct-the-others-an-orchestrator-and-what-does-it-owe-the-sessions-it.md).
The way of working is built first in this tree and offered to every tree, so each step also
names what a fresh tree would need to take it by itself; that sketch is
[q-wit7by](../questions/q-wit7by-what-does-a-fresh-tree-under-the-harness-need-to-set-up-standing-sessions-by-itself.md)'s
material, never a rule until it lands in a skill or a rules file.

## 1. Evidence from a tree already working so — 2026-11-15

The person asked this session to question game-delta's orchestrator about how it works with its
sessions, and to have it survey the sessions it serves. Two reads came back: the orchestrator's
own report with all eight sessions' answers (game-delta f24efe9), and an outside read by a
subagent of this tree ([the audit](game-delta-orchestration-audit-2026-11-15.md)). They agree: nine
sessions cut by the game's domains move fast; the shared checkout is the root of most collisions;
the reply's form is the main excess; the orchestrator directs no work, and its duties that need no
judgement — ids, the nightly sweep — lapsed.

<straw-dog question="q-wit7by">
A fresh tree needs: a way to ask the sessions it already runs how the work feels to them, the
answers kept in their own words, and a read of the tree's records by someone other than the
session that keeps the way of working.
</straw-dog>

## 2. The shape chosen — 2026-11-15

Four options were put to the person; the person took the fourth: **a keeper of the harness, one
area among the others.** It directs no work. It is the one writer of the rules, the mechanisms,
the rule-failure register and the updates from core. Its arbitration is finding where areas
collide and bringing each collision to the person as a decision. Merging into main is a second
such area, the lander. What needs no judgement is mechanism: ids, lines kept apart by a worktree
per writing session, landing through the lander, a notice of changed rules, the scheduled sweep.
The keeper does not raise the coordinator; the first call that finds none does.

<straw-dog question="q-wit7by">
A fresh tree needs: the keeper's area named in its roster from the second standing session on;
every other session registering a rule failure and handing the amendment to the keeper instead of
landing it.
</straw-dog>

## 3. The first keeper — 2026-11-15

The person asked this session to be the keeper, and it accepted. The session's title was set to
name its role, as game-delta's sessions name theirs. Three questions were opened under q-ja5mp4:
how a project cuts its work into standing sessions and changes the cut
([q-jz3mlm](../questions/q-jz3mlm-how-does-a-project-cut-its-work-into-standing-sessions-and-how-does-the-cut-change-as-the-project-grows.md)),
how a session takes a role on and hands it on so it outlives a chat
([q-mvd4pi](../questions/q-mvd4pi-how-does-a-session-take-on-a-standing-role-learn-it-and-hand-it-on-so-the-role-outlives-any-one-chat.md)),
and what a fresh tree needs ([q-wit7by](../questions/q-wit7by-what-does-a-fresh-tree-under-the-harness-need-to-set-up-standing-sessions-by-itself.md)).
The one other session running here, on the store's coordinator, was told what the keeper owns.

<straw-dog question="q-mvd4pi">
A fresh tree needs: a session taking a role names it in its title and its row, and the role's
brief lives where a successor finds it without this chat.
</straw-dog>

## 4. The first traffic to the keeper — 2026-11-15

Within the hour the keeper was asked from two sides. game-delta's orchestrator, told by the
person to turn to the keeper freely, asked what core does with its commit and doc gates, which
sit in core's files against R7, and how core sees an architecture document five sessions write.
The keeper answered with its view and left both decisions to the person in game-delta's session:
move the project's gates out of core's files now; take worktrees and landing through one lander
rather than build the gate further; split the architecture by area, the index keeping the
contracts between areas. The coordinator's session here asked whether its rule texts collided
with the keeper's before committing them — none were in flight — and handed over, at the person's
word, a sweep list of fifteen places contradicting decisions of
[q-j5s7gp](../questions/q-j5s7gp-how-is-a-recorded-rule-failure-replayed-as-a-test-against-the-amended-rule.md),
left by a session that ended 2026-11-10 and so belong to nobody.

<straw-dog question="q-wit7by">
A fresh tree needs: a session that lands a rule text asks the keeper first; a recipient's keeper
turns to core's keeper with what its tree changed in core's files or would offer to core; drift
left by a session that ended comes to the keeper.
</straw-dog>

## 5. A mechanism's change carried to the recipients — 2026-11-15

The coordinator's session, on the person's word, carried the store's migration to ids that never
change to every tree carrying the harness. game-delta's orchestrator got the steps by message,
naming its sender as the coordinator's session and the keeper's earlier advice as coming first;
an uncommitted HARNESS-MIGRATION.md went to the root of each tree with no session running —
ai-game-1, xuanxue-workshop, codename_japaka, game-gamma, game_beta, research-harness, studies,
jobs; the three clones of this tree get it by a pull. The keeper asked beforehand only to be told,
and was. The ticket's next stage waits until all nine trees report migrated; that tally is the
ticket's, held by the coordinator's session.

<straw-dog question="q-wit7by">
A fresh tree needs: a change a mechanism ships to the recipients goes from the mechanism's own
session, naming itself, and the keeper is told where it went; a recipient that runs a keeper
receives it through its keeper.
</straw-dog>

## 6. A recipient takes the way of working in order — 2026-11-15

The person accepted in game-delta the whole order its orchestrator drew from the keeper's advice:
the project's own gates and its S5 rewordings out of core's files; core updated; the store's ids
migrated; a worktree per writing session; the architecture cut by area. Step one was built and
went to a judge: the gates moved to `tools/gates/` behind their own `gates.sh`, called from the
host's settings by a command of their own so an update leaves them be; the S5 wording became a
local rule that names S5 as the one it overrides; core's files went back to the installed ref.
For step two the keeper advised updating from the public repository at a pinned commit, without
`--overwrite` first, letting the update's refusal name any file still edited, and checking after.
The orchestrator offered core a change to `/discover`'s description that its person had accepted
there; the keeper took it as a proposal to bring to this tree's person.

<straw-dog question="q-wit7by">
A fresh tree needs: an update of core pinned to a commit and run without `--overwrite` first; a
project's own mechanism kept outside core's files and hooked by a command of its own; a change a
recipient makes to a core skill offered back to core's keeper instead of kept in place.
</straw-dog>

## 7. Traffic both ways — 2026-11-15

The person accepted `/discover`'s new use case here, and core took it (5a63b969): the first change
a recipient sent up through its keeper. game-delta then did its steps one to three: its gates out
of core's files (65b78ed), core updated without `--overwrite` (b0bcb05), the store's ids migrated
in a commit of their own (d5ac4a8). On the way its keeper met a core bug — under
`core.autocrlf=true` an untouched AGENTS.md read as an edit in core — which core fixed with a test
(8531f359), and two gaps in the migration, which the keeper here passed to the session holding the
migration rather than mending them. It declined to mark a maintenance sweep that never ran: the
tree has no schedule for one.

<straw-dog question="q-wit7by">
A fresh tree needs: a bug a recipient meets in core goes to core's keeper with how to reproduce it;
core's keeper routes it to the session that holds that work, or mends it with a test where nobody
does; the scheduled sweep set up at install, not given to a session's hands.
</straw-dog>

## 8. The sweep's findings reach the keeper — 2026-11-15

The nightly sweep, its session running with no person, sent the keeper what the keeper's own
decisions contradict and what the q-j5s7gp subtree the keeper took over still holds, writing
nothing in either itself. The coordinator's session sent a wording fix to a rules file, found by
the same sweep, for the keeper to land, and repaired the rest of what fell in its own unit; the
two sessions told each other which paragraphs of the two mechanism docs both were editing, and
each stages only its own hunks. The keeper checked each finding against its text before acting:
the mechanical ones went to a subagent, the role texts were wrapped as straw dogs and their
tension recorded in the open question of what a role is, and what changes the rules — who lands
an amendment, who repairs a rule file, whom the sweep's leftovers go to, who lands on main — went
to the person as one decision.

<straw-dog question="q-wit7by">
A fresh tree needs: the sweep sends what falls in a session's area to that session and the rest to
the keeper, writing nothing in another's area; a session holding a mechanism sends a rule change to
the keeper rather than landing it; two sessions editing one file say which parts each holds.
</straw-dog>
