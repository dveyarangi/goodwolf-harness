## Families

Notation: [H]/[M]/[L] = high / medium / low confidence in the claim as stated. (E) = rests on evidence I can point to (a corpus figure, a standard's text, a tool's documented limit, a study); (P) = rests on argument or on the family's own stated principles without a measurement. Where a figure comes from a secondary citation rather than the primary, I say so.

### 1. Issue tracking with sub-issues and blocking links (Jira, GitHub, Bugzilla, Linear)

**Mapping.** Matter = issue; single-parent decomposition = epic/story/sub-task or GitHub parent/sub-issue; cross edge = "blocks / is blocked by" (Jira link type, Bugzilla `depends on`); settlement kinds = resolution field (Bugzilla: FIXED, INVALID, WONTFIX, DUPLICATE, WORKSFORME, INCOMPLETE); "currently worked" = assignee's in-progress item.

1. **Depth and breadth.** Jira's native hierarchy is exactly three levels (epic → story/task → sub-task); sub-tasks cannot have sub-tasks; levels above epic need Advanced Roadmaps/Premium [H](E) ([Atlassian community](https://community.atlassian.com/forums/Jira-questions/Issue-hierarchy-epics-features-stories-tasks-sub-tasks-how-to/qaq-p/2139946), [Titanapps summary](https://titanapps.io/blog/jira-issue-hierarchy)). GitHub sub-issues: up to 100 sub-issues per parent and up to eight levels of nesting [H](E) ([GitHub docs](https://docs.github.com/en/enterprise-cloud@latest/issues/tracking-your-work-with-issues/using-issues/adding-sub-issues), [InfoQ on the rollout](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/04/github-subissues-journey)). These are product limits, not observed distributions; the family's own lore is that breadth grows (epics with dozens of children) while depth stays at 2–3 because each level is a different *kind* of thing (initiative/epic/story/sub-task), not a recursive one [M](P). Bugzilla has no hierarchy at all — only the dependency graph plus "meta bugs" used as ad-hoc parents [H](E) ([Mozilla bug 24496 on resolving FIXED with unresolved dependencies](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=24496)).
2. **Single vs multi-parent; the second relation.** Jira/GitHub: strictly one parent; Bugzilla: no parent, `depends on`/`blocks` carries both "part of" and "cannot proceed until" in a single relation, which is the documented source of confusion (meta bugs vs true blockers) [M](E, Mozilla practice pages). Jira keeps the two relations separate: parent link vs "blocks" issue link; the "blocks" graph is a DAG in intent but not enforced [H](E). A matter serving two parents is handled by duplication or by a link ("relates to"), never by a second parent [H](P, from the schema). Duplicates are the family's dominant settlement-by-merge: roughly 53% of closed Stack Overflow posts are closed as duplicates [M](E) ([IEEE study](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8816736/)); bug-report duplicate rates of ~20–30% in Eclipse/Mozilla are the commonly cited figures (Bettenburg et al. 2008) [M](E, from memory of the paper).
3. **Insertion.** GitHub: "change parent" on a sub-issue is a UI operation [H](E, docs). Jira: inserting a level between epic and story means converting issue types and re-establishing epic links; a KB article documents the resulting inconsistency ("epic has sub-tasks under it along with child issues") [M](E) ([Atlassian KB](https://support.atlassian.com/jira/kb/jira-epic-issues-have-subtasks-under-them-along-with-child-issues/)). Insertion is cheap when the type ladder allows it and blocked when it doesn't — because the levels are typed, a newcomer "between" two typed levels has no type to be.
4. **Per-message reader.** The family has no per-message reader; triage is per-issue, and the excerpt a triager sees is the issue plus its linked issues one hop away. The one measurement of "how far a message lands" in this family is duplicate-detection: ranking candidates by text similarity with recency windows — studies use the whole repository, not a neighbourhood [M](E, [MSR 2016](https://clones.usask.ca/pubfiles/articles/AhasanuzzamanDuplicateMSR2016.pdf)). No figure on working-set size.
5. **Ladder position.** The described shape (typed settlement, blocking as a separate relation, unlimited depth) is past Jira's native form and roughly GitHub's 2025 form; the family's known failure is *hierarchy drift*: sub-issues that are really blockers, blockers that are really parts, and orphaned children after a re-type [M](P). Bugzilla's history — a request to split WONTFIX into sub-values ([bug 522873](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=522873)) — shows settlement kinds proliferating under use [M](E).

### 2. Threaded conversation and conversation disentanglement (email/Usenet/Reddit trees, Slack threads, IRC disentanglement corpora)

**Mapping.** Matter = message (or a thread's root); single-parent decomposition = reply-to; per-message reader = the disentangler/annotator who, for each incoming line, decides which earlier line it answers or whether it starts a new thread; settlement = accepted answer / "resolved" marker / closed-as-duplicate. No cross edge.

1. **Depth and breadth.** Reddit discussion trees: mean depth 3.38 (σ 3.32), mean width 6.24 (σ 29.9), mean size 14.3 (σ 59.7); strong negative correlation between normalised depth and width — trees are deep-and-narrow or shallow-and-wide, rarely both; root bias (the root's degree is larger and more broadly distributed than any comment's) [H](E) ([Deconstructing the structure of online conversations on Reddit](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/364025579_Deconstructing_the_structure_of_online_conversations_on_Reddit); [CSCW 2024 follow-up](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3686913)). Depth is what stays bounded; breadth is what grows, and the family attributes it to attention concentrating on the root (E). Slack tried arbitrary-depth replies, found threads "quickly became extremely complex," and shipped one level of replies, calling the constraint "an instant success" [H](E) ([Slack Design](https://slack.design/articles/threads-in-slack-a-long-design-journey-part-2-of-2/)). Zulip replaces trees with flat named topics and argues that is what makes history browsable [H](E) ([Zulip](https://zulip.com/help/introduction-to-topics)).
2. **Single vs multi-parent.** Email/Reddit: single parent by construction. The IRC disentanglement corpus (77,563 messages) allows a message to link to one or more earlier messages, i.e. a DAG [H](E) ([Kummerfeld et al. 2019](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1374/)). Molweni's guideline: each utterance gets "at least one parent" [H](E) ([Molweni](https://aclanthology.org/2020.coling-main.238.pdf)). The family never models "cannot be taken up until"; ordering is time.
3. **Insertion.** The JWZ threading algorithm's dummy containers are the exact operation: when a message references a parent not yet seen, a placeholder node is created so siblings group correctly; when the real parent arrives it fills the placeholder; empty placeholders with one child are pruned [H](E) ([jwz.org](https://www.jwz.org/doc/threading.html)). Re-parenting is invisible and automatic because parentage is declared by the child (In-Reply-To/References), not chosen by a reader. In Slack, a message posted in the wrong place simply cannot be moved into a thread (the family's known failure: "people not using threads") [M](E, practitioner sources).
4. **Per-message reader.** This is the family's core measured problem. In the IRC corpus: 88.3% of consecutive within-conversation messages are ≤8 messages apart, 99.4% ≤100 apart; 94.9% within 2 minutes; three or fewer conversations run concurrently 46.4% of the time, ten or fewer 97.3%; annotators were first given 100 messages of context, later extended to 1,000 lines with a check of every unlinked message against the extra context; Cohen's κ for links 0.71–0.74 [H](E) ([ar5iv 1810.11118](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.11118)). What goes wrong with too small an excerpt: heuristic (time-window) methods produced conversations "spanning days" while human annotations "rarely contain links beyond an hour" — i.e. a too-large window over-links to stale matters, a too-small one strands messages as new roots (E, same source). Molweni notes long-distance links (≥4 utterances) are the hard case and are "common in multiparty dialogs" [H](E).
5. **Ladder position.** Arbitrary-depth reply trees are the family's beginner form (Usenet, early Slack); the mature forms are either depth-1 threads (Slack) or flat topics (Zulip) — the family walked *down* the depth ladder for legibility [H](E). The shape described (unbounded depth, re-parenting by a reader) is what Slack found unworkable for humans; the disentanglement corpora show it is tractable for an annotator at κ≈0.72 given ~100 lines of context. Both facts stand; the family does not reconcile them.

### 3. SDRT-style discourse attachment and the Right Frontier (Asher & Lascarides; STAC, Molweni, Annodis)

**Mapping.** Matter = discourse unit or complex segment; decomposition = subordinating relations (Elaboration, Explanation) which put a unit "under" another; cross edge ≈ coordinating relations (Narration, Continuation) — a weak fit; per-message reader = the parser/annotator attaching each new unit; the bounded excerpt = the Right Frontier (the last node plus the nodes that dominate it). No settlement states.

1. **Depth and breadth.** The Annodis corpus: ~100 doubly annotated French documents, ~6,837 EDUs, 6,402 relations; the mean number of nodes open on the right frontier at any moment is 16.43% of all nodes in the graph [H](E) ([Afantenos & Asher 2010](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1006.5880)). Multiparty chat: Molweni averages 8.82 utterances per dialog (max 14), 3.51 speakers [H](E). No depth figure is published as such; the RF size is the family's proxy for "how much of the structure is live," and it shrinks proportionally as the graph grows (P, from the definition).
2. **Single vs multi-parent.** SDRT structures are DAGs; a unit may attach to more than one node [H](E). RST, the sibling theory, insists on trees and adjacency; Afantenos & Asher found 20.38% of non-local attachments violate RST's adjacency assumption [H](E). The two relations are merged: subordination *is* the hierarchy, and what may be attached next is derived from it (the RF), not stored separately.
3. **Insertion.** Late discovery that a unit sits between two others is handled by complex segments (grouping) rather than re-parenting; the family treats structure as built incrementally and mostly monotone — an attachment, once made, is not revisited [M](P). What goes wrong: the RFC is violated ~5% of the time in the corpus, and the authors attribute violations to click errors or "structural misconceptions" [H](E).
4. **Per-message reader.** The theory's answer is precise: the excerpt is the chain of dominating ancestors of the last node plus the last node — nothing else is available for attachment. Empirically the constraint held 95% (lenient) / 88.91% (strict) of the time with five naive annotators not told the rule [H](E). About 40% of attachments are non-local (distance ≥2), so "attach to the previous unit" is a poor baseline [H](E). Psycholinguistic work reports discourse expectations sensitive to the RFC [M](E) ([Discourse Processes 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0163853X.2026.2658004)).
5. **Ladder position.** RF-constrained attachment is the mature form for monologue; for multiparty chat the family reports that long-distance links and DAG structure are the open problem (Molweni; [Structural Characterization for Dialogue Disentanglement](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.08018)) [M](E). The described shape — reader sees "a bounded excerpt" — is the RF idea; the family's evidence says the right excerpt is *ancestors of the current node*, not "all open matters" and not siblings.

### 4. Questions Under Discussion (Roberts; Büring's d-trees; QUD-tree annotation)

**Mapping.** Matter = question under discussion; decomposition = sub-question as strategy for answering a super-question (Roberts' "strategy of inquiry"); settled = answered and popped; cross edge: none; per-message reader = the QUD annotator/hearer who, for each utterance, decides whether it answers the current QUD, opens a sub-question, or resolves and pops. "Currently worked" = top of the QUD stack.

1. **Depth and breadth.** No published depth/fan-out distribution for QUD trees that I can point to [L](P). De Kuthy, Reiter & Riester report "reasonable" inter-annotator agreement for the tree structure and "good" agreement for information structure on English and German interview data [H](E) ([LREC 2018](https://www.ims.uni-stuttgart.de/documents/team/arndt/doc/DeKuthyReiterRiester2018.pdf)). The theory bounds breadth by the requirement that sub-questions be a strategy for the parent (Roberts), and bounds depth only by the stack discipline (P).
2. **Single vs multi-parent.** Trees by construction (Büring's d-trees; Riester's QUD trees) [H](E). A move that bears on two questions is the family's known trouble: it is handled by reconstructing a common super-question, i.e. by inserting a parent [M](P). Ordering constraints beyond the stack are not modelled.
3. **Insertion.** Insertion is the family's *normal* operation: most QUDs are implicit and are reconstructed by the annotator, so a question "between" two existing ones is routinely inferred and placed [H](E, De Kuthy et al.; [Riester, Constructing QUD Trees](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Constructing-QUD-Trees-Riester/8afdbca4475b0cd1a164fae9ad747bfb34808b03)). What goes wrong: agreement on structure is only "reasonable" — insertion choices are where annotators diverge (E).
4. **Per-message reader.** Roberts' model says the excerpt is the QUD stack: the current question and its unanswered super-questions [H](P, canonical). The annotation tool (TreeAnno) shows the growing tree; the annotation guideline is a principle of maximal informativity (choose the QUD the utterance answers most fully) rather than a window [M](E). No figure on how far a message lands.
5. **Ladder position.** The stack-plus-tree with implicit insertion is the mature form; the known weakness is that the same discourse admits several defensible trees, and the family accepts that as intrinsic rather than fixable (E, agreement figures).

### 5. IBIS / gIBIS / Dialogue Mapping (design rationale)

**Mapping.** Matter = Issue; decomposition = sub-issues that "question" or "are suggested by" a node; settlement = a Position taken plus arguments; cross edges = gIBIS link types *generalizes/specializes*, *replaces*, *questions*; "replaces" is the family's "superseded by a newer matter"; the per-message reader = the facilitator mapping a live meeting.

1. **Depth and breadth.** The NCR field trial with indented-text IBIS captured more than 2,300 requirements and design decisions over roughly 15 months [H](E) ([Conklin & Burgess Yakemovic 1991](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1207/s15327051hci0603%25264_6)). Dialogue Mapping guidance: the root question should be as broad as possible so the first layer is wide; the grammar is kept small so the facilitator can keep up [M](E, [Conklin demonstration paper](https://www.publicsphereproject.org/events/diac08/proceedings/33.Dialogue_Mapping.Conklin.pdf), [Conklin HT01 retrospective](http://www.cognexus.org/conklin-ht01.pdf)). No published depth distribution; practitioner lore is wide-and-shallow maps because deep chains of sub-issues lose the room [M](P).
2. **Single vs multi-parent.** gIBIS is a graph: issues can respond to and be suggested by several nodes; Dialogue Mapping in practice draws mostly a tree with occasional cross-links [M](E/P). The second relation is absent; ordering is the facilitator's choice.
3. **Insertion.** The family's costliest known problem is not insertion but *capture*: recording rationale disrupts design (Grudin-type objection; Buckingham Shum & Hammond 1994 "what use at what cost") [M](E, from memory of the literature). Inserting an issue between two existing ones in a live map is cheap in Compendium (drag a link), but the retrospective records that maps captured in the moment are rarely restructured afterwards — insertion happens live or not at all [M](P).
4. **Per-message reader.** The facilitator's excerpt is the visible map, typically one root question's neighbourhood projected on a screen; the practice's stated skill is "capture just enough context and move on" [M](E). No figure on working-set size.
5. **Ladder position.** The described shape is IBIS with typed settlements added; the family's documented failure modes are the capture cost and the "maps nobody re-reads" problem — the structure serves the meeting, not later navigation [M](E, HT01).

### 6. Goal-oriented requirements and assurance cases (KAOS, i*/GRL, GSN)

**Mapping.** Matter = goal (or GSN goal/strategy); decomposition = AND/OR refinement; settlement = satisfied/denied labels, or GSN "solution" (evidence) attached; postponed-with-default = GSN *undeveloped* element; cross edges = i* dependencies / GSN "in context of"; the second relation is not an ordering.

1. **Depth and breadth.** Industrial GSN cases reported by Diemert & Joyce: automotive 149, 257, 484, 513 nodes; rail 40 and 95; industrial control 14 (secondary citation via later assurance-case papers) [M](E) ([Eliminative Argumentation](https://www.criticalsystemslabs.com/resources-hub/2020SysConDiemert/2020SysConDiemert.pdf)); the CERN LHC machine-protection case is described as the only public assurance case over 100 nodes [M](E) ([Viger et al. 2026](https://incose.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/sys.70010)). Goal-model reasoning papers report their largest test model at 351 nodes / 510 edges [M](E) ([SHORTer reasoning](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.05568)). The family states that KAOS models for large systems become "incomplete and/or complex" and hard to change [M](E, [KAOS complexity metrics](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262221604_A_Framework_to_Evaluate_Complexity_and_Completeness_of_KAOS_Goal_Models)). Breadth grows; depth in published examples sits around 4–7 levels [L](P, from examples, no survey figure).
2. **Single vs multi-parent.** Goal graphs are DAGs: a sub-goal may contribute to several parents (edges 510 > nodes 351 above) [H](E). GSN handles a shared sub-argument by *away goals* and modules rather than a second parent [H](E, GSN standard). Cost the family names: propagation semantics become ambiguous under multi-parent AND/OR (satisfaction analysis needs conflict handling) [M](P).
3. **Insertion.** GSN has a dedicated node type for exactly this — the *Strategy* — inserted between a goal and its sub-goals to state how the decomposition works; adding one later is a normal edit [H](E). Kelly's change-impact work treats re-parenting as a traced change with review [M](E, from memory). What goes wrong: hidden re-use — the same sub-goal reached by two paths gets edited in one and not the other (P).
4. **Per-message reader.** None; review is whole-graph and top-down. Practitioner studies report confidence coming from tracing a branch root-to-leaf, i.e. the ancestor chain [M](E) ([How do practitioners gain confidence](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.03657)).
5. **Ladder position.** The described shape (single parent, typed settlement, undeveloped-as-default) is GSN's mature form minus multi-parent; the family's known failure is scale — hundreds of nodes reviewed as one graph — and the lack of an incremental reader is exactly what the LLM-review papers now try to add (E, 2024–2026 arXiv titles).

### 7. Feature models (software product lines)

**Mapping.** Matter = feature; single-parent decomposition = the feature tree (mandatory/optional/or/alternative groups); cross edges = cross-tree constraints (requires/excludes); "settled" ≈ selected/deselected in a configuration; "currently worked" ≈ the feature being configured. The mapping of *requires* to "cannot be taken up until" is loose: it constrains selection, not order.

1. **Depth and breadth.** Knüppel et al. evaluated 127 real-world models: an automotive line with up to 18,616 features and 1,369 cross-tree constraints, plus Kconfig-derived models including Linux [H](E) ([FSE 2017](https://wwwiti.cs.uni-magdeburg.de/iti_db/publikationen/ps/auto/KnueppelTMMS:FSE17.pdf)). Linux 2.6.9 x86-64 translates to 7,400 Boolean variables; across 5,709 models, systems software shows 7.11 require-arcs per node vs 2.78 exclude-arcs, with hub-like degree distributions [H](E) ([network analysis, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.16577)). Depth is not reported in these sources; SPLOT's academic models are small with depth as a listed metric ([Bagheri & Gasevic 2011](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/344310672_Classifying_Feature_Models_Maintainability_based_on_Machine_Learning_Algorithms)) [L](E-weak). Family's finding: the tree stays shallow and breadth plus cross-tree constraints carry the semantics; simple requires/excludes are insufficient for real models [H](E, Knüppel).
2. **Single vs multi-parent.** Strict tree; a feature serving two groups is a known expressive gap, worked around by a cross-tree constraint or by duplicating the feature [H](P, standard textbook point). The second relation is kept separate (constraints) and is unordered.
3. **Insertion.** Thüm, Batory & Kästner classify every edit as refactoring, specialization, generalization or arbitrary, decidable by SAT [H](E) (ICSE 2009); inserting an abstract feature between two is a refactoring (product set unchanged) and tooling (FeatureIDE) makes it cheap [M](E). What goes wrong: an inserted *concrete* feature or a moved group changes the configuration space silently unless checked (E, the paper's motivation).
4. **Per-message reader.** None in the family; configuration is interactive whole-model reasoning. No figure.
5. **Ladder position.** Tree-plus-constraints is the mature form; the family's own evidence says the tree is the minor part at scale and that "attributed"/complex constraints take over (E, Knüppel). The described shape is where feature models began.

### 8. Work Breakdown Structure and schedule network (PMI Practice Standard; MIL-STD-881)

**Mapping.** Matter = WBS element/work package; single-parent decomposition = WBS with the 100% rule; cross edge = precedence relation in the schedule network (FS/SS/FF/SF), kept in a separate artefact; postponed-with-default = planning package under rolling-wave planning; settled = work package complete/baselined.

1. **Depth and breadth.** PMI's Practice Standard describes a typical WBS as around four levels, with the number of levels chosen for manageability [M](E) ([PMI standard](https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/practice-standard-work-breakdown-structures-8063), [Practice Standard v2 PDF](https://eva.fing.edu.uy/pluginfile.php/248446/mod_label/intro/WBS%20Practice%20Standard%20v2.pdf)). MIL-STD-881 fixes the top three levels per system type and leaves the contractor to extend below [M](E, from memory of the standard). Work-package sizing heuristics (8/80 hours) bound depth from below [M](P, practitioner lore). Breadth is what grows; depth is governed by control-account granularity.
2. **Single vs multi-parent.** Strict tree; the 100% rule forbids an element under two parents because the sums would double-count [H](E). A deliverable serving two parents is placed once and cross-referenced in the WBS dictionary [M](P). The "cannot start until" relation lives entirely outside the WBS, in the network diagram/schedule [H](E, PMBOK).
3. **Insertion.** Inserting a level re-codes everything below (1.2.3 → 1.2.1.3) and is a baseline change under change control; practice standards say the WBS is deliverable-oriented so that such restructuring is rare [M](E/P). What goes wrong: cost accounts and earned-value history keyed to old codes break (P).
4. **Per-message reader.** None; scope change requests are processed against the whole baseline.
5. **Ladder position.** The described shape is WBS plus a schedule network — the family's mature form — except that WBS elements do not carry typed settlements (a dropped element is removed by change control, not marked). The family's known failure is decomposition by activity or organisation instead of deliverable, which produces deep, ambiguous trees (E, standard's guidance).

### 9. Interactive theorem proving: goal stacks and hierarchical proofs (Coq/Rocq, Isabelle/Isar, Lean; Lamport)

**Mapping.** Matter = open goal; decomposition = a tactic replacing a goal by subgoals (single parent, tree); cross edge = shared existential variables — instantiating one in goal A constrains goal B — and Coq's *shelved* goals; settled kinds: closed (decided), `sorry`/`Admitted` (postponed with a default that the checker flags), `shelve` (postponed), `clear`/`give_up`; "currently worked" = the focused goal; the per-message reader = the proof state display.

1. **Depth and breadth.** No corpus figure for tactic-tree depth that I can point to [L](P). Lamport's method is hierarchical numbering with unbounded depth and short step lists per level, motivated by the claim that flat prose proofs hide errors [H](E) ([Lamport 2012](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/proof.pdf)). Isabelle's Isar documentation states that hierarchical structure keeps internal goals concise and lets sub-proofs be checked independently [H](E) ([From LCF to Isabelle/HOL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.02836)).
2. **Single vs multi-parent.** Tree by construction; a lemma serving two goals is factored out as a named lemma (a new root), never shared in place [H](P). The second relation is real and handled by shelving: evar-dependent goals are hidden and revisited via `Unshelve` [H](E) ([Rocq proof mode](https://rocq-prover.org/doc/V8.18.0/refman/proofs/writing-proofs/proof-mode.html), [issue 8770 "What are the shelves for?"](https://github.com/coq/coq/issues/8770)).
3. **Insertion.** `have`/`assert` inserts an intermediate matter cheaply; extracting a lemma re-parents a subtree. What goes wrong: scripts that address goals by position break when a tactic's subgoal order changes — the documented reason Coq has recommended bullets/focusing over positional selectors since 8.8, and Isar's reason for naming cases [H](E, Rocq docs) [M](P for the breakage mechanism). Also [issue 9171](https://github.com/coq/coq/issues/9171): `Unshelve` after `admit` is "counterintuitive" — postponed matters interact badly with defaulted ones (E).
4. **Per-message reader.** The excerpt is exactly one focused goal with its hypotheses plus a count of remaining goals; ancestors are not shown, siblings are hidden behind bullets [H](E). Known failure of too small an excerpt: "replaying a proof after changes in the statement usually doesn't display enough information to distinguish between branches of case analysis" (E, Rocq docs). No figure on working-set size.
5. **Ladder position.** Focused single-goal work with typed postponement is the mature form; the family's lesson is that position-based reference to sibling matters is the brittle part, fixed by naming (E).

### 10. Hierarchical Task Network planning and refinement acting (HTN, TIHTN, HDDL, RAE)

**Mapping.** Matter = task; decomposition = a method refining a compound task into a task network; cross edge = ordering constraints inside the task network, a separate relation from decomposition; settled = primitive task executed; insertion = TIHTN's "task insertion".

1. **Depth and breadth.** No survey of realised decomposition depth I can point to [L](P). The theory's result: unrestricted recursive decomposition makes plan existence undecidable; bounding depth or allowing task insertion restores decidability — TIHTN is EXPTIME-complete (Alford et al. 2015) [H](E) ([Geier & Bercher 2011](https://mlanthology.org/ijcai/2011/geier2011ijcai-decidability/), [structural complexity analysis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.14174)).
2. **Single vs multi-parent.** Each task occurrence has one parent method; the same task *type* recurs under many parents (P). Ordering constraints are first-class and separate: HDDL keeps decomposition and ordering as distinct constructs [H](E) ([HDDL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.05499)).
3. **Insertion.** Task insertion — adding tasks not produced by any decomposition — is the family's named operation; it changes the complexity class and is what makes "refining methods" learnable [H](E) ([Xiao et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.12949), [TIHTN with state constraints, IJCAI 2017](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2017/0623.pdf)). Inserting a *compound* task between an existing parent and child (re-parenting) is not the family's operation; it is a domain edit.
4. **Per-message reader.** Refinement acting (RAE) keeps a refinement stack per top-level task and reacts to each event by consulting the current stack, i.e. the ancestor chain of the current task [M](E, Ghallab/Nau/Traverso 2016; [HTN Acting addendum](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.02127)). No figures on stack size.
5. **Ladder position.** The described shape is HTN's task network with typed settlement added; the family's known failure is the search-space consequence of deep recursion, which it bounds by depth limits or insertion — both are search remedies, not recording remedies (E/P).

### 11. Hypothesis-driven issue trees (MECE; consulting problem structuring)

**Mapping.** Matter = question in the tree; single-parent decomposition = MECE branching (mutual exclusivity *is* single parentage); settled = branch tested and pruned; "currently worked" = the branch the hypothesis points at; no cross edge.

1. **Depth and breadth.** Practitioner guidance converges on 2–4 (some say 3–4) levels and 2–5 branches per node; six levels is called "analysis paralysis"; six or more branches at one level is called unwieldy and is to be fixed by adding depth, not width [M](E, practitioner sources: [Analyst Academy](https://www.theanalystacademy.com/issue-tree-and-logic-tree-framework/), [Crafting Cases](https://www.craftingcases.com/issue-tree-guide/)). No measured corpus; the family bounds depth by "stop when a leaf is answerable by one analysis" (P).
2. **Single vs multi-parent.** Forbidden by MECE; a question that serves two branches is treated as a sign the cut is wrong and the tree is re-cut [H](P, definitional).
3. **Insertion.** Re-cutting the tree is expected and cheap because the tree is disposable and short-lived; the family does not preserve history (P). What goes wrong: MECE violations after insertion (overlap) go unnoticed (P).
4. **Per-message reader.** None; the tree is drawn before evidence is gathered.
5. **Ladder position.** The shape described is deeper and longer-lived than this family finds workable; the family's own advice caps depth at 4 and treats the tree as a one-off (E, guidance).

### 12. Adjudication: issues for decision, threshold questions, and "need not reach"

**Mapping.** Matter = issue for decision; decomposition = sub-issues under a claim; cross edge = threshold ordering (jurisdiction before merits); settlement kinds = decided, moot (made irrelevant), "need not reach" (made irrelevant by a sibling's outcome), dismissed as duplicative, reserved (postponed), superseded on amendment.

1. **Depth and breadth.** No measured depth; English Commercial Court practice caps statements of case at 25 pages, never over 40 without permission, and requires an agreed List of Issues as the tool for scoping evidence [M](E) ([Commercial Court Guide](https://www.judiciary.uk/courts-and-tribunals/business-and-property-courts/commercial-court/litigating-in-the-commercial-court/commercial-court-guide/)). Judgments in practice run two to three levels of issues [L](P).
2. **Single vs multi-parent.** A factual issue commonly bears on several claims; courts handle it by deciding it once and cross-referencing, not by nesting it twice [M](P). The ordering relation is explicit and enforced: *Steel Co.* holds that Article III jurisdiction must be decided before the merits and rejects "hypothetical jurisdiction" [H](E) ([Steel Co. v. Citizens, 523 U.S. 83](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/96-643.ZO.html)); lower-court drift away from that rule is itself documented [M](E) ([Harvard JLPP note](https://journals.law.harvard.edu/jlpp/wp-content/uploads/sites/90/2021/01/Kulp-Jurisdictional-Avoidance.pdf)).
3. **Insertion.** A late-discovered issue is added by amendment and numbered "12A" rather than renumbering — insertion is allowed but renumbering is avoided because references in filed documents are frozen [M](P, practice). What goes wrong: issues argued under the wrong head are decided out of order, or not decided (mootness) (P).
4. **Per-message reader.** The judge assigning submissions to issues works from the List of Issues — all open matters, flat — not from a neighbourhood (P). No figures.
5. **Ladder position.** The described shape matches this family's mature practice in settlement vocabulary (moot, need-not-reach, reserved) and in a hard ordering relation; the family's known failure is deciding a lower matter when a threshold matter would have made it irrelevant (E, Steel Co.).

### 13. Outliner task hierarchies (Org-mode)

**Mapping.** Matter = headline with a TODO keyword; single-parent decomposition = outline nesting; settlement kinds = configurable done-states (DONE, CANCELLED, DEFERRED); cross edge = `ORDERED` property (a child is blocked until earlier siblings are done) and `org-enforce-todo-dependencies` (a parent cannot be DONE while children are TODO); org-depend adds arbitrary blocker links.

1. **Depth and breadth.** No measured figures [L](P).
2. **Single vs multi-parent.** Strict tree; the blocking relation is *derived from the tree* (sibling order, parent-child) rather than stored separately, with org-depend as the escape hatch [H](E) ([Org manual](https://orgmode.org/manual/TODO-dependencies.html), [org-depend](https://orgmode.org/worg/org-contrib/org-depend.html)).
3. **Insertion.** Promote/demote and subtree move are single keystrokes; insertion is invisible and unrecorded (E, manual). What goes wrong: derived blocking silently changes when a subtree moves under an `ORDERED` parent (P).
4. **Per-message reader.** None; narrowing to a subtree is the user's excerpt (E).
5. **Ladder position.** This is the family's whole ladder — a beginner's tool that stays a tree and derives ordering from it; it has nothing to say about a reader placing messages.

## What the tasking smuggled in

- **Tree first, DAG second.** The description takes single-parent decomposition as primary and cross edges as an addition. SDRT, IBIS and goal models start from a graph and never had a tree to add to; feature models, WBS and MECE trees start from a tree and treat any second relation as a foreign artefact. The tasking's order is one family's, not the field's.
- **Depth as the danger.** The questions ask whether depth stays bounded. Every family with measurements (Reddit, feature models, GSN, WBS) reports breadth as what grows; depth is bounded by the *typing of levels* (Jira, MIL-STD-881), by a stack discipline (QUD, proof mode), or by legibility (Slack). Nothing measured shows depth as the scaling problem.
- **One matter currently worked.** Proof mode and QUD have a single focus; the IRC corpus shows ≤3 concurrent conversations only 46.4% of the time and ≤10 97.3% of the time. The single-focus assumption is a design choice the conversation families do not share.
- **The reader sees a structural excerpt.** The disentanglement corpora bound the reader by *recency* (100–1,000 lines, time within an hour), not by structural neighbourhood; SDRT bounds by *ancestors of the last node*; adjudication bounds by *the flat list of all open issues*. The tasking assumes an excerpt shaped like a subtree.
- **Six settlement kinds.** The list (decided, dropped, merged, postponed-with-default, irrelevant, replaced) is Bugzilla's resolution set with GSN's "undeveloped" and gIBIS's "replaces" added. Most families have two states or fewer; adjudication has more and orders them.
- **Insertion is common.** No family measures how often re-parenting happens. QUD annotation and JWZ threading treat insertion as the normal case; feature models and WBS treat it as a controlled edit; Slack makes it impossible. "Expected to be common" is the tasking's forecast, not a finding.
- **Two relations, kept separate.** WBS/schedule, HDDL, Jira keep them separate; Bugzilla merges them and suffers for it; org-mode derives one from the other; SDRT derives availability from the hierarchy. The description's separation is one of at least four arrangements.
- **"Bears on nothing" as a category.** Only the disentanglement family has it explicitly (unlinked/system messages). Elsewhere every message is forced under something.

## Nothing to take

**Refused families.**
- Formal dialogue games (Hamblin, Mackenzie DC, Walton–Krabbe): their structure is commitment stores plus legal-move rules; embedded sub-dialogues exist but there is no decomposition tree, no re-parenting and no settlement typology. Mapping cannot be stated in two sentences.
- Abstract argumentation frameworks (Dung): an attack graph with extension semantics; no decomposition, no "currently worked," no insertion. Refused.
- Rhetorical Structure Theory as a separate family: a strict-tree, adjacency-bound sibling of family 3 whose adjacency assumption family 3 measured as violated ~20% of the time in non-local cases; nothing it adds beyond that figure.
- Zettelkasten / faceted classification / Dewey: link networks or classification lattices with no settlement and no worked matter. Refused.
- Kanban/Scrum backlogs and GTD: flat or two-level lists; already covered by family 1 where they have hierarchy.
- Decision trees, Bayesian networks, partial-order planning: no incremental reader placing messages; POP's ordering constraints are covered by family 10.

**Looks portable, is not.**
- Slack's "one level won" is a legibility finding about human UI for chat replies, not about placing matters; it cannot be read as "trees deeper than one fail."
- Reddit's mean depth 3.38 is a reply-chain statistic driven by attention dynamics (root bias, branching-process criticality); it says nothing about decomposition of matters.
- SDRT's 95% right-frontier compliance is from French newspaper/Wikipedia text with naive annotators; the multiparty chat corpora (STAC, Molweni) are DAGs where long-distance links are the open problem — the monologue figure does not transfer to conversation.
- GitHub's eight levels and Jira's three are product limits and pricing tiers, not observations of what people need.
- Consulting depth guidance (2–4 levels, 2–5 branches) is practitioner lore for a disposable, pre-evidence tree, unmeasured.
- HTN decidability results concern search over decompositions, not maintenance of a record; TIHTN's "insertion" is adding leaves, not re-parenting.
- Goal-model and GSN node counts are graph sizes; depth is not reported, so "hundreds of nodes" cannot be converted into a depth claim.
- The IRC disentanglement window figures (100–1,000 lines; ≤8 messages for 88.3%) are for a fast technical-support channel; a slower deliberation would shift them by an unknown amount.

**Did the pass return usable material?** Yes, on four points with evidence: (a) breadth, not depth, is what grows in every measured family; (b) the two families that actually run a per-message reader (disentanglement, SDRT) bound the excerpt by recency and by ancestor chain respectively, with ~40% of attachments non-local and human agreement around κ 0.72; (c) insertion is cheap where parentage is declared by the child or the level is untyped (JWZ, QUD, org-mode, proof `have`) and expensive where levels are typed or codes are frozen (Jira, WBS, filed pleadings); (d) the ordering relation is enforced hard in exactly one family (adjudication, *Steel Co.*) and derived rather than stored in two (org-mode, SDRT). On depth figures for decomposition-of-matters specifically, the pass returned nothing measured beyond product limits and practitioner caps.
