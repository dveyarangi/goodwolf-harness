## Families

Notation: [H]/[M]/[L] = confidence; (E) = evidence exists that can be pointed to (a text, a tool behaviour, a documented practice); (P) = principle/argument only. Sources consulted online are linked where they bear on a claim; everything else is from knowledge.

### 1. Erotetic logic and the philosophy of questions (Collingwood; Belnap & Steel; Hintikka; Wiśniewski)

One sentence: a question is a set of possible direct answers plus a presupposition, and inquiry is a chain of inferences whose premises and conclusions include questions.

1. **Unit and relations.** Belnap & Steel (1976): a question *is* its set of direct answers; its presupposition is what every direct answer entails (roughly the disjunction of the direct answers) [H]. Relations: *containment* (Q1 contains Q2 if every direct answer to Q1 entails one to Q2), *equivalence*, and *presupposition* — a question is only well-posed once its presupposition is settled [H]. Collingwood (*Autobiography* 1939, *Essay on Metaphysics* 1940): every proposition is an answer to a question; every question rests on a presupposition; a presupposition is *relative* if it is itself the answer to a prior question, *absolute* if it stands to all questions only as a presupposition, never as an answer; a question whose presupposition is not in fact made "does not arise" ([SEP entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/collingwood/); [Douglas](https://medium.com/genus-specious/part-2-collingwoods-revolution-in-logic-817bcdef1920)) [H]. That gives a dependence order (question→presupposition→prior question) terminating in roots that are *not* questions — your "some questions can only be asked once another is settled" is Collingwood's relative presupposition [H]. Hintikka's interrogative model of inquiry: a *principal* question and *operational* questions put to an oracle/Nature, in a game whose moves are deductions and questions; a question may be asked only when its presupposition has been established in the play [H]. Wiśniewski's Inferential Erotetic Logic: *erotetic implication* (a question plus premises implies another question whose every answer helps answer the first) and *evocation* (premises give rise to a question); *erotetic search scenarios* are trees with the principal question at the root, direct answers as leaves, and auxiliary questions entering only if erotetically implied ([Synthese 2003](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1022983325118); [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/WINTLO-3)) [H]. The "serve" relation you name is, in IEL, a *validity* condition: Q2 serves Q1 iff every direct answer to Q2, with the premises, narrows Q1 [H]. **Closure:** a *direct* answer; *partial* answer = a proposition entailed by some but not all direct answers (eliminates alternatives without picking one) [H]. **Structure:** the e-scenario is a tree because it is a *plan of asking* (branch per possible answer); the space of questions under containment/equivalence is a *preorder*, not a tree [H]. Van Fraassen (*The Scientific Image*, why-questions): a question's identity = topic + contrast class + relevance relation — the same words are different questions under a different contrast class [H].
2. **Separation from bookkeeping.** Complete: the logic has no notion of who asked, when, or where the answer is filed; it also does not hold that a question is a work item — Hintikka's inquiry is epistemic, its "work" is the asking [H] (P). Collingwood explicitly denies that propositions can be assessed apart from the question they answer — the record of an answer without its question is meaningless to him [H].
3. **Failure modes.** *Non-arising*: a question with an unmade presupposition is nonsense (Collingwood) — and a question whose presupposition is *false* has no true direct answer (Belnap: "corrective" answers deny the presupposition — the erotetic form of "this was the wrong question") [H]. Bromberger's *p-predicament*: you know every answer you can think of is wrong (the question stays open because the answer-set is mis-drawn) [M]. Van Fraassen gives the machinery for *split* (one wording, two contrast classes) and *merge* (two wordings, one partition) [M]. IEL's *erotetic decomposition principle* is the formal account of splitting a question into auxiliary ones [M]. Nothing in this family maintains structures over time; e-scenarios are constructed per inquiry [H].
4. Claims about the logic: (E) textual. Claims that this helps in practice: (P) — Hintikka's model was applied in argumentation pedagogy; the IEL group applied e-scenarios to dialogue systems (small experiments) [M].

### 2. Formal semantics of discourse: Questions Under Discussion (Roberts, Ginzburg, Büring, van Kuppevelt, SDRT, inquisitive semantics)

One sentence: a discourse is a stack (or partial order) of open questions, each move is relevant iff it partially answers or sub-asks the top one, and sub-questions form a strategy of inquiry.

1. **Unit and relations.** Roberts (1996/2012): the QUD; a *strategy of inquiry* is a hierarchy of interdependent questions; *Q2 is a subquestion of Q1 iff a complete answer to Q2 contextually entails a partial answer to Q1*; a move is relevant iff it partially answers or is part of a strategy for the QUD; a root "Big Question" (*what is the way things are?*) ensures every question has a parent ([Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Question_under_discussion); [Riester et al.](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0163853X.2017.1316038)) [H]. Büring's *d-trees* draw this as a tree with questions at internal nodes and assertions at leaves [H]. Van Kuppevelt (1995): a subquestion is *induced* by an unsatisfactory answer — partial answers spawn children [H]. Ginzburg's KoS: QUD is a partially ordered set, not a stack; *MAX-QUD* is the current focus; crucially, *resolvedness is goal-relative* — whether a proposition resolves a question depends on the goals of the asker (Ginzburg 1995 "Resolving questions") [H]. SDRT (Asher & Lascarides): relations Q-Elab, QAP, Question–Answer pairs; the *right frontier constraint* — new material may attach only to the right frontier of the discourse graph — is a formal account of which questions are still open for attachment [H]. Inquisitive semantics (Ciardelli, Groenendijk, Roelofsen): a question is a downward-closed set of information states; questions and assertions live in one algebra; *Q1 entails Q2 iff every resolution of Q1 resolves Q2*; there is a *dependency* connective (Q1 → Q2: Q2 is determined given Q1) ([Synthese 2015 on IEL meets inquisitive](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-013-0355-4)) [H]. **Structure:** under entailment questions form a *lattice* (Heyting algebra), so subquestion is a partial order with meets/joins; the *tree* appears only when you record the *order in which they were raised* [H]. **Closure:** a complete answer (selects a cell of the partition); a *partial* answer eliminates ≥1 cell; Ginzburg adds *downdating without answering* (a question is dropped from QUD because the conversation moved on or it became irrelevant) [H].
2. **Separation.** The QUD is content-only; there is no bookkeeping. But Ginzburg's goal-relativity is a direct statement that *closure is not a property of the answer* — it lives in the pairing (question, purpose) [H]. Nobody in this family equates a question with a work item; discourse "moves" are the work [H].
3. **Failure modes.** *Implicit QUDs*: most questions structuring a text are never uttered and must be accommodated — questions that "never arise" explicitly but are load-bearing (Roberts; Onea's *potential questions*) [H]. *Stale top*: SDRT's right frontier says attaching to a closed-off node is incoherent; the analogue of "child answered while the parent changed" is a non-relevant move [M]. *Merge*: two utterances answering the same partition are the same question; *split*: a question whose partition is too coarse is refined by sub-questions [H]. Maintenance is by the participants' running common ground; the models say nothing about durable records [H].
4. Structure claims (E) textual; predictive claims about coherence and prosody have experimental support in linguistics [M] (E); nothing here is evidence about *work*.

### 3. Issue-based design rationale (IBIS, gIBIS, PHI, QOC, DRL; Dialogue Mapping)

One sentence: a design deliberation is a hypertext of issues, positions and arguments, and the record of it is the rationale.

1. **Unit and relations.** Kunz & Rittel (1970) IBIS: *issue* (a question), *positions* (candidate answers), *arguments* (for/against positions); issue–issue relations: *generalizes/specializes*, *questions/is-suggested-by*, *replaces*, *similar-to*, *temporal successor* [H]. gIBIS (Conklin & Begeman 1988) fixed the link types: responds-to, supports, objects-to, specializes, generalizes, questions, is-suggested-by, replaces [H]. **PHI** (McCall, 1980s–1991) adds exactly your relation: an issue *serves* another if resolving it helps resolve the other; PHI represents *non-deliberated* issues too (IBIS only held controversial ones), removes pro/con typing, and *restricts the order in which issues can be raised* — a "quasi-hierarchy" ([Wikipedia: Design rationale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_rationale); [TAMU slides](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/furuta/436slides/ch26.pdf); [UT slides](https://users.ece.utexas.edu/~perry/education/382v-s06/L14.pdf)) [H]. QOC (MacLean, Young, Bellotti, Moran 1991): Questions, Options, Criteria, with *consequent questions* arising from an option — a DAG since a criterion or option is shared [H]. DRL (Lee 1991): decision problem, alternatives, goals, claims; *is-a-subdecision-of*, *is-a-subgoal-of*, *is-a-kind-of* [H]. **Closure:** in IBIS an issue is *resolved* by selecting a position (a decision); Conklin's Compendium adds a *decision* node type [H]. Partial: a position tentatively favoured; IBIS has no formal partial answer [M]. **Structure:** a graph; PHI's "quasi-hierarchy" means a DAG with a dominant tree and cross-links [H]. Rittel's wicked-problem theses (1973): *no stopping rule*, *every wicked problem is a symptom of another*, *the formulation of the problem is the problem* — i.e. the structure has no natural root and no natural leaf [H].
2. **Separation.** IBIS is a notation for content; the *hypertext tool* (gIBIS, Compendium) is the bookkeeping, and the separation is explicit in the literature (Lee & Lai 1991 "What's in design rationale?" compares representations as such) [H]. The family's key stance: the issue is *not* the work item — the artifact being designed is separate from the argumentation about it, and Fischer, Lemke, McCall & Morch ("Making Argumentation Serve Design", 1991/1996) argue rationale is used only when tied to *construction* (the JANUS system couples a construction kit to the argumentation and fires critics) ([1991](https://l3d.colorado.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/1991-Making-Argumentation-Serve-Design-HCIJ.pdf); [1996](https://l3d.colorado.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/1996-Making-Argumentation-Serve-Design.pdf)) [H].
3. **Failure modes** — this family has the best-documented failures. (a) *Capture cost*: Buckingham Shum & Hammond 1994 review evidence that semiformal argumentation costs designers real effort at the wrong moment and that structuring in the notation interferes with design thinking; benefits accrue to *later readers*, costs to *authors* — Grudin's asymmetry ([paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1071581984710299)) [H] (E). (b) *Fragmentation*: users split reasoning into nodes too early and lose the thread (Conklin's own reports on gIBIS) [M] (E). (c) *Orphan / dangling issues*: issues raised and never linked or resolved were the norm in gIBIS logs [M] (E). (d) *Reified categories*: forcing a thought to be Issue vs Position vs Argument at capture time is the classic complaint; Shipman & McCall's *incremental formalization* (capture informal text, formalize later) is the proposed remedy [H] (E). (e) *Stale rationale*: the design moves on, the argument graph is not revisited — Horner & Atwood 2006 catalogue the barriers [M]. Maintenance in practice: Dialogue Mapping (Conklin 2005) makes a facilitator maintain the map live in the meeting; the map is then a record, not a living structure [M].
4. Structure claims (E). Cost claims (E, empirical but from small HCI studies of the 1990s). Fischer's "serve design" claim (P plus a demo system).

### 4. Goal-oriented requirements and assurance cases (KAOS, i*, GQM, GSN/CAE)

One sentence: a system's purpose is a graph of goals refined by AND/OR into sub-goals until leaves can be assigned to agents or supported by evidence.

1. **Unit and relations.** KAOS (van Lamsweerde): *goal*, refined by *AND-refinement* (all children needed) and *OR-refinement* (alternatives); *obstacles* (what blocks a goal), *conflicts* between goals; leaf goals become *requirements* (assigned to software agents) or *expectations* (to environment agents); the goal graph is a *DAG* because a subgoal may contribute to several parents [H]. i*/Tropos (Yu): *softgoals* with +/−/++/−− contribution links; satisfaction is *satisficing*, judged not proved [H]. GQM (Basili & Weiss): *Goal → Question → Metric* — literally a question layer between purpose and measurement; a metric may answer several questions (DAG) [H]. GSN (Kelly & Weaver): *Goal, Strategy, Solution (evidence), Context, Assumption, Justification*; *SupportedBy* and *InContextOf* links; an *undeveloped* goal is drawn with a hollow diamond — an open question marked inside the structure itself; *away goals* reference goals held in another module [H]. CAE (Claims–Arguments–Evidence) is the same shape [H]. **Closure:** a goal is *satisfied* when its AND-children are satisfied or one OR-child is, bottoming out in evidence (GSN) or an operationalization (KAOS). Partial: softgoal satisficing degrees; GSN *assurance claim points* and *confidence arguments* attach a degree-of-belief to a link [M]. **Structure:** DAG for goals; GSN is nominally a tree per module but away-goals make it a DAG [H].
2. **Separation.** These notations *define* the structure and leave identifiers/statuses to tools (DOORS, Jama, ASCE). GSN standard v3 explicitly separates the *argument* from *evidence artefacts* and their configuration management [M]. On question ≠ work item: KAOS says a *requirement* is a leaf goal assigned to an agent — so the work item is a *leaf of the deliberation*, not the deliberation [H].
3. **Failure modes.** *Confirmation bias in assurance cases*: an argument built to support a claim under-represents counter-evidence — Leveson's critique; *dialectical GSN* adds counter-evidence nodes [H] (E, incident analyses). *Undeveloped leaves that stay undeveloped* is a recognised audit finding [M]. *Obstacles* in KAOS are the account of "the child was answered but the parent changed": an obstacle analysis is re-run when a parent goal changes [M]. *Goal conflict* (two parents demanding incompatible children) is first-class [H].
4. Structure (E, standards and textbooks). "Assurance cases improve safety" — contested; (P) with a body of critique [M].

### 5. Requirements traceability management (DOORS, Jama, ReqIF)

One sentence: every requirement is linked to what it satisfies and what verifies it, and link health is what the tool maintains.

1. **Unit:** *object* (requirement statement) with typed links *satisfies*, *derives-from*, *verifies*, *refines*; a *derived requirement* has no parent in the stakeholder set but is needed by the design [H]. **Structure:** DAG across modules [H]. **Closure:** a requirement is *verified* when a verification object links to it with a passing result — status is a link property, not an attribute of the text [H].
2. **Separation:** complete and tool-enforced — text objects, links, and attributes (status, owner, id) are three distinct data kinds; ReqIF serializes them separately [H]. A requirement is not the work item; the work item lives in a change-management tool linked by id [H].
3. **Failure modes — named and tooled.** *Orphan* (no upward link) and *childless/widow* (no downward link) are standard report columns [H] (E). **Suspect links**: when either end of a link changes after the link was made, the tool marks the link *suspect* until a human reviews and clears it — the exact tooling for "a child answered while its parent has changed" ([IBM docs](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/engineering-lifecycle-management-suite/doors/9.7.2?topic=data-suspect-links-changed-objects); [DOORS 9.6](https://www.ibm.com/docs/SSYQBZ_9.6.0/com.ibm.doors.requirements.doc/topics/c_aboutsuspectlinks.html)) [H] (E). *Requirements volatility* metrics track churn; *baselines* freeze a version so that "answered against which parent" is answerable [H].
4. (E) for tool behaviour; whether traceability pays off — mixed empirical literature (Gotel & Finkelstein's "traceability problem", 1994; later studies show it is maintained mainly where regulators demand it) [M].

### 6. AI problem reduction and interactive theorem proving (AND/OR graphs, HTN, Prolog, Coq/Lean/Isabelle)

One sentence: a goal is solved by reducing it to subgoals whose solutions compose, and the proof state is the live tree of what remains open.

1. **Unit and relations.** Nilsson's *AND/OR graphs* (problem reduction): AND nodes (all subproblems required), OR nodes (alternative reductions); a node is *solved* when its children are, propagated upward; *solution graph* = the chosen subtree [H]. Newell & Simon's GPS: means–ends analysis, subgoal to reduce a difference [H]. HTN planning: tasks decomposed by *methods* into subtasks with ordering constraints; primitive tasks bottom out [H]. Prolog/SLD resolution: a query is a goal; each clause application replaces a goal by subgoals; the SLD tree is the search space; a *proof tree* is the solution [H]. Proof assistants: the *goal stack*; a tactic splits a goal into subgoals; a lemma may be *admitted* (Coq `Admitted`, Lean `sorry`, Isabelle `sorry`) — the structure carries the open question as a typed hole, and the checker reports how many remain; `Qed` closes [H]. **Closure:** solved/proved. **Partial:** a conditional result — a theorem proved *assuming* an open lemma — is exactly "the child is answered while the parent stays open"; in complexity theory whole literatures are conditional on P≠NP or GRH [H]. **Structure:** AND/OR *graph* (a subproblem may be shared — memoization), tree in the naïve case [H].
2. **Separation:** the strongest case in any family — the proof term *is* the structure; names, files, modules are bookkeeping and the checker is indifferent to them; Mathlib's policy that a `sorry` may not be merged is a bookkeeping rule laid over a structural fact [H]. Goal = work item here, trivially and by construction; but note the goal is *decided by the checker*, not by a written answer [H].
3. **Failure modes.** *Cycles* (a goal reduced to itself — loop detection in tabling/Prolog) [H]. *Shared subgoal solved under different assumptions* — the AND/OR graph must track context; HTN handles with constraints [H]. *Stale proof*: a change to a definition breaks downstream proofs — detected by re-checking, the CI analogue of suspect links [H] (E). *Lemma-incorporation* (Lakatos, *Proofs and Refutations*): a counterexample does not kill the theorem; the hidden lemma is made an explicit hypothesis — a *question splits* and the parent is *reworded* [H].
4. All (E), as executable systems.

### 7. Work-tracking practice (Bugzilla, Jira, GitHub, Scrum/Kanban, WBS/CPM, RAID logs, GTD)

One sentence: a work item is a record with a lifecycle and typed links, and "done" is a state transition governed by a definition of done.

1. **Unit and relations.** Bugzilla (1998–): *bug* with `depends on / blocks` (DAG), `duplicate of` (merge), and a two-axis lifecycle — *status* (NEW, ASSIGNED, RESOLVED, VERIFIED, CLOSED, REOPENED) × *resolution* (FIXED, INVALID, WONTFIX, DUPLICATE, WORKSFORME, MOVED) [H]. Jira: fixed 3-level tree Epic → Story → Sub-task plus a free link graph (*blocks, relates, duplicates, clones*); *status* vs *resolution* is a notorious source of confusion (an issue "Done" with empty resolution) [H] (E). GitHub: `closed as completed` vs `closed as not planned`; sub-issues (2024–25) add a tree; task lists are informal children [H]. Scrum: *spike* — a time-boxed item whose output is knowledge/decision, explicitly *not* a story — the practice's own admission that a question and a work item are different kinds [H]; *acceptance criteria* are the questions whose yes closes a story; *Definition of Done* is a global closure predicate [H]. Story splitting (Lawrence's patterns, SPIDR) — a question that turns out to be several [H]. WBS: *100% rule* (children exhaust the parent; deliverable-oriented, not activity-oriented) — a strict tree by fiat; CPM/PDM precedence network (FS/SS/FF/SF) is a DAG — PM practice keeps *decomposition* (tree) and *dependence* (DAG) as two separate diagrams [H]. PMBOK *issue log*, *decision log*, *RAID* (Risks, Assumptions, Issues, Dependencies), "parking lot" — separate registers for open questions vs work [H]. GTD (Allen): *open loops* captured; *project* = multi-step outcome, *next action* = the atomic step; *Someday/Maybe* = deferred question; Allen insists the lists are bookkeeping and the thinking ("what's the outcome? what's the next action?") is separate [H]. **Closure:** state transition + resolution word; partial: "in review", "blocked", % complete, "Won't fix" (closed without answer) [H]. **Structure:** tree for containment, DAG for dependence, always both [H].
2. **Separation:** trackers are *all* bookkeeping and know it; Bugzilla's status/resolution split is the industry's canonical separation of *process state* from *outcome*. The practice's answer to "is a work item a question?": no — a bug is a *report*, a story is a *promise of value*, a spike is a *question*; trackers hold all three in one record type and the type field is the seam [H].
3. **Failure modes.** Duplicates and the merge operation; *zombie tickets* (never closed, never worked) and mass "stale bot" closure (GitHub `stale` action closes on inactivity — closure by expiry, without answer) [H] (E). *Epic drift*: children done, epic's goal changed — nothing flags it (Jira has no suspect-link analogue) [H]. *Orphan sub-tasks* when a parent is deleted/moved [M]. *Ticket ≠ intent*: Fowler-style critiques that the tracker becomes the design record and the design intent is lost [M]. Maintenance: *backlog grooming/refinement* as a ritual; *WIP limits* as a cap on open questions; *ageing reports* [H] (E).
4. Tool behaviour (E); "grooming works" (P, practitioner consensus, thin evidence).

### 8. Standards and language-design processes (Rust RFC, PEP, ADR, IETF/W3C/ISO comment disposition)

One sentence: a proposal is accepted with an explicit list of what it leaves unresolved, and those items are tracked to closure by a later, separately recorded act.

1. **Unit and relations.** Rust RFC template has an *Unresolved questions* section split into *before RFC merge / before stabilization / out of scope*; the tracking issue lists them "to ensure they are not forgotten"; the stabilization report must document their resolutions ([rustc dev guide](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/walkthrough.html); [stabilization template](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/stabilization-report-template.html); e.g. [tracking issue #64796](https://github.com/rust-lang/rust/issues/64796)) [H] (E). This is an *accepted decision with open children*: the parent closes (RFC merged) *before* its children — the reverse of "done when all questions answered" [H]. PEP statuses: Draft, Accepted, *Provisional* (accepted, may change — a partial closure), Final, Rejected, Withdrawn, Deferred, Superseded; `Replaces`/`Superseded-By` headers form a chain [H]. ADRs (Nygard 2011): Proposed / Accepted / Deprecated / *Superseded by* — decisions are immutable; change = new record pointing back; the structure is a *linked chain*, not a tree [H]. W3C issue states: raised / open / pending review / closed / *postponed*; ISO ballot comment dispositions: Accepted / Accepted in principle / Noted / Rejected — "accepted in principle" is a partial answer as a status word [H]. IETF: *Last Call*, *consensus* (Clark's "rough consensus and running code") closes; RFC 7282 defines consensus as "no sustained objection", not agreement — closure by absence of live objection [H].
2. **Separation:** partial. The *record* (RFC/PEP/ADR file) carries both the answer and the status header; the *process* (FCP, tracking issue) is elsewhere. ADR practice is the clearest statement that a decision record is a *write-once* document and status is metadata about it [H]. A proposal is not a work item: tracking issue (work) and RFC (question/answer) are different objects in Rust by design [H].
3. **Failure modes.** Unresolved questions that outlive the feature ("stabilized with open questions" is a known Rust grievance) [M] (E, threads). *Superseded chains* that a reader must walk to find the live answer (ADR critique) [M]. PEP *Deferred* as the parking state; *Withdrawn* = author closed without answer [H]. Maintenance: the FCP ritual forces a closure decision by a deadline [H].
4. (E) for process texts; (P) for whether it yields better decisions.

### 9. Parliamentary procedure (Robert's Rules; Westminster practice)

One sentence: the "question" is the motion pending before the assembly, and the rules are a complete machinery for raising, subordinating, splitting, postponing, deciding and reopening questions.

1. **Unit and relations.** The *main motion* is the question; only one main motion may be pending at a time; *subsidiary motions* (amend, refer/commit, postpone to a certain time, postpone indefinitely, previous question, lay on the table) apply to it and are ordered by *precedence* — a strict stack of pending questions [H]. *Amendment to the amendment* is the depth limit (two levels) — a rule against unbounded sub-questioning [H]. *Germaneness* = relevance: an amendment must relate to the pending question [H]. *Division of the question*: splitting one motion into separately decided parts [H]. *Substitute motion*: a competing formulation replacing the question [H]. *Refer to committee*: delegate a child to be worked elsewhere and reported back [H]. *Object to the consideration of the question*: the question should not arise at all [H]. *Postpone indefinitely*: kill without deciding on the merits [H]. *Reconsider*: reopening a decision is permitted only by one who voted on the prevailing side, only within the same session — a procedural guard against reopening an accepted decision without new grounds; *rescind/amend something previously adopted* needs a higher threshold [H]. **Closure:** the vote (the *previous question* forces it). **Partial:** an adopted amendment settles part; "postpone to a certain time" is a dated deferral [H]. **Structure:** a stack with bounded depth, plus committee references (children handled in other assemblies) [H].
2. **Separation:** explicit and old. *Minutes record what was done, not what was said* — the record holds decisions and dispositions, the deliberation is not recorded (RONR) [H]. The *motion* is not the *task*: adopting a motion creates an obligation someone else executes; the assembly tracks execution only via committee reports [H].
3. **Failure modes.** *Tabled and forgotten* (lay on the table dies at session end) [H]. *Unfinished business / orders of the day* carry open questions across sessions with a rule for what lapses [H]. *Dilatory motions* — questions raised to obstruct [H]. Maintenance is by the *agenda* (orders of the day) and the chair [H].
4. All (E) as codified rules; effectiveness (P, centuries of use).

### 10. Common-law adjudication (issues, preclusion, mootness, remand)

One sentence: litigation narrows a dispute to issues of fact and law, a court decides only the issues necessary to judgment, and what it actually decided binds later cases.

1. **Unit and relations.** Pleadings *join issue*; issues of fact vs issues of law; a *question of first impression* has no precedent; certiorari petitions state *Questions Presented*; *elements* of a claim form an AND-tree (all must be proved), *affirmative defenses* are competing branches [H]. *Certified question*: a court asks another court to answer a sub-question of law — a cross-tree delegation [H]. **Closure:** judgment; *issue preclusion (collateral estoppel)* closes an issue across cases only if it was *actually litigated and necessarily decided* — a closure test on the *relation to the parent*, not the answer alone [H]. *Ratio decidendi vs obiter dicta*: only what was necessary to the decision binds — an answer to a child that did not serve the parent has no force [H]. **Partial:** "we need not reach / we reserve the question"; *remand* sends a child back open; plurality opinions settle the outcome without settling the rule (Marks rule) [H]. **Structure:** per case a tree (claim → elements → facts); across cases a DAG of precedent [H]. *Ripeness* (too early to ask), *mootness* (no longer worth asking), *standing* (not yours to ask) — three doctrines about when a question may or may not arise [H].
2. **Separation:** docket numbers, case captions, reporter citations are bookkeeping; the *holding* is content; law schools teach extracting the question–holding pair from the opinion (IRAC/CREAC), i.e. reconstructing the question structure from the record [H]. A case is not a question: it is a *dispute*, closed by judgment even with questions unresolved [H].
3. **Failure modes.** *Dicta mistaken for holding* (child answer over-read) [H]. *Overruled parent*: a lower-court decision built on a precedent later overruled — the doctrine of *stare decisis* plus *prospective overruling* manages the stale-tree problem [H]. *Circuit splits*: the same question answered differently in two branches until a higher court merges [H]. *Advisory opinions* prohibited in US federal courts — you may not answer a question with no live parent [H].
4. All (E) as doctrine; whether narrow deciding produces better law (P, contested — minimalism debate).

### 11. Disputational traditions (scholastic *quaestio*; Talmud *sugya*, *teiku*; responsa)

One sentence: a body of learning is organised as questions each held with its objections and answer, and a question may be recorded as permanently unresolved.

1. **Unit and relations.** Aquinas's *Summa*: Parts → Questions → Articles, each article a question ("Utrum…"), with *videtur quod* (objections), *sed contra*, *respondeo* (answer), *ad primum…* (replies) — a fixed tree of questions with a fixed article schema; articles serve their question, questions serve their treatise [H]. The *quaestio disputata* and *quodlibetal* questions (asked from the floor, out of tree) [H]. Talmud: *kushya* (difficulty), *teyuvta* (refutation), *shinuya* (resolution); **teiku** — "let it stand": the redactors record a question as unresolved, and halakhic practice supplies a default (stricter for biblical matters, lenient for rabbinic) so that *action* proceeds while the *question* stays open ([Balashon](https://www.balashon.com/2006/07/teiku.html); [Sheilot](https://sheilot.com/en/articles/view/15339/); [Magid](https://ayinpress.org/column/teiku/)) [H]. Responsa (*she'elot u-teshuvot*, Islamic *istifta/fatwa*, Roman *responsa prudentium*): the *question–answer pair* is the unit of the corpus [H]. **Closure:** the *respondeo*; or *teiku* (closed as unresolvable, with a rule for acting anyway) [H]. **Partial:** a *distinction* (answering under one reading, not another) [H]. **Structure:** the Summa is a true tree by editorial fiat; the Talmud is a graph of cross-references over a linear text [H].
2. **Separation:** medieval page layout separates *text*, *gloss* and *commentary*; the Summa's numbering (I-II q.94 a.2) is pure bookkeeping over a content tree; the Talmud's *daf* pagination (Vilna edition) is bookkeeping unrelated to the sugya structure [H]. Question ≠ work: deliberation is the work [H].
3. **Failure modes.** *Teiku* is the explicit account of a question that never closes; the *default rule* is how the tradition keeps working with it open [H]. *Quodlibetal* questions are orphans by design [M]. *Stale parents*: later commentaries (Tosafot) re-open sugyot when a parent principle elsewhere conflicts — maintenance by perpetual commentary [M].
4. (E) textual.

### 12. Scientific method and research programmes (Popper, Lakatos, Laudan, open-problem lists, evidence-based medicine)

One sentence: science is the succession of problems, and a research programme is a hard core of settled answers with a heuristic that says which questions to ask next.

1. **Unit and relations.** Popper: P1 → TT → EE → P2 — every solution generates new problems; the *problem situation* is the unit [H]. Lakatos: *hard core* (not questioned), *protective belt* (auxiliary hypotheses adjusted), *positive heuristic* (the programme's own agenda of sub-questions), *problem shift* (progressive vs degenerating) [H]. Laudan (*Progress and Its Problems*): *empirical problems* (unsolved, solved, anomalous) and *conceptual problems*; progress = problem-solving effectiveness; a problem counts as solved *relative to a theory* [H]. Open-problem lists (Hilbert 1900; Millennium Problems; Erdős problems) — each with *open/solved/partial* and a bookkeeping layer (bounty) — and *conditional results* (theorems assuming RH) as answers to children while the parent is open [H]. Evidence-based medicine: a *PICO* question (Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome) is a *template for a well-formed question*; a systematic review answers one; *GRADE* rates certainty (high/moderate/low/very low) — a graded partial answer; *living systematic reviews* re-open the question on a schedule [H]. Preregistration separates *the question and its analysis plan* (fixed before data) from *the answer* [H]. **Closure:** in Laudan, relative to a theory — never absolute; in EBM, "sufficient certainty for the decision" [H]. **Structure:** Lakatos's programme is a *sequence* of theories; Laudan's problem set is unordered; problem–subproblem reductions in mathematics form a DAG [H].
2. **Separation:** journals, DOIs, registries (ClinicalTrials.gov, PROSPERO for review protocols) are bookkeeping; the *question* is registered separately from the *paper* that answers it — PROSPERO is literally a register of open review questions [H] (E). Question ≠ work item: a *study* is a work item; a *question* may take many studies [H].
3. **Failure modes.** *Degenerating programme*: children answered only to protect the core (Lakatos) [H]. *Anomalies* left unsolved indefinitely (Laudan) [H]. *File-drawer / publication bias*: questions answered negatively never get recorded — the answer exists, the record does not [H] (E). *Questions that never arise*: Kuhn's normal science does not ask outside the paradigm [H]. *Outdated reviews*: Shojania et al. 2007 found half of systematic reviews out of date within ~5.5 years — a measured tree-staleness rate [M] (E). Maintenance: living reviews, Cochrane update policy [H].
4. Philosophy (P); EBM staleness and bias (E).

### 13. Decision analysis and operations research (decision hierarchy, influence diagrams, VoI, set-based design, fault trees)

One sentence: a decision is a question whose answer we choose, an uncertainty is a question whose answer we learn, and the order in which they resolve determines what can be decided when.

1. **Unit and relations.** Howard/SDG *decision hierarchy*: decisions *taken as given*, decisions *to make now*, decisions *to defer* — a three-band sort of questions by whether they are open here [H]. *Influence diagrams*: decision nodes, chance nodes, value node; *informational arcs* into a decision node say what is known when it is made — the formal "can only be asked once another is settled"; the diagram is a DAG and *no-forgetting* orders the decisions [H]. *Decision trees* (chance and decision nodes) are the unrolled tree; *rollback* closes from the leaves [H]. *Expected value of information*: a question is worth asking only if some answer would change a decision — a criterion for which questions *should* arise [H]. Toyota/Ward *set-based concurrent engineering*: keep *sets* of alternatives open and narrow gradually — a partial answer is a *narrowed set*, and the *last responsible moment* (Lean software, Poppendieck) is when the set must collapse [H]. *Fault trees* (FTA): top event, AND/OR gates, basic events; a DAG because a basic event feeds several gates; *minimal cut sets* are the solution [H]. *Real options*: the value of keeping a question open [H].
2. **Separation:** decision analysis distinguishes the *frame*, the *model*, and the *record of the decision*; Howard's *clarity test* (a question is well-posed if a clairvoyant could answer it without asking for clarification) is the field's criterion for a well-formed question [H]. A decision ≠ a work item; the *implementation* is outside the analysis [H].
3. **Failure modes.** *Premature convergence* (point-based design; Ward's critique) vs *analysis paralysis* [H] (E, Toyota case studies, modest). *Wrong frame* (answering the wrong question well — Howard: "a good decision is not a good outcome") [H]. *Ignoring information order* — deciding a child before the parent's uncertainty resolves [H]. Fault trees go stale when the design changes; maintained by re-derivation, no incremental tooling in classic FTA [M].
4. Formal (E); Toyota claims (E, but anecdotal-heavy).

### 14. Intelligence requirements management (PIR → EEI → indicators; LTIOV; ACH)

One sentence: a commander's decisions generate prioritized questions, decomposed into collectable information items, each with a deadline after which its answer is worthless.

1. **Unit and relations.** *Commander's Critical Information Requirements* → *Priority Intelligence Requirements* (questions about the adversary/environment) → *Specific Information Requirements* / *Essential Elements of Information* → *indicators* and *collection tasks* — a tree from decision to observable; each PIR is tied to a *decision point* on the synchronization matrix [H]. **LTIOV** (latest time information is of value): every requirement carries an expiry — after it, the question is closed *unanswered and moot* [H]. *Analysis of Competing Hypotheses* (Heuer): hypotheses in columns, evidence in rows, eliminate by *inconsistency* — competition between answers, closure by elimination not confirmation [H]. **Closure:** answered, or expired, or the decision point passed [H]. **Structure:** tree from decisions to collection; a matrix for competition [H].
2. **Separation:** the *collection plan* (bookkeeping: who collects what, by when) is a separate document from the *PIR list* (the questions); doctrine (FM 2-0, JP 2-0) keeps them apart by design [H]. Question ≠ work item: collection *tasks* are work; PIRs are questions [H].
3. **Failure modes.** *Orphan requirements* — collection not tied to any decision (a doctrinal warning) [H]. *Stale PIRs* after the plan changes — doctrine mandates PIR review at each planning cycle [H]. *Answering the indicator, not the question* (collection-driven rather than requirement-driven analysis) [M]. Time is the maintenance mechanism: LTIOV prunes automatically [H].
4. Doctrine (E); efficacy (P).

### 15. Consulting problem-structuring (issue trees, MECE, hypothesis trees, Minto pyramid)

One sentence: a client's key question is decomposed into mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive sub-questions until each is answerable by analysis, and the write-up inverts the tree into answers.

1. **Unit and relations.** *Key question* at the root; *issue tree* of sub-questions (MECE: siblings do not overlap and jointly exhaust the parent); *hypothesis tree* = the same tree with each node phrased as a claim to test ("answer-first") [H]. Minto's *pyramid principle*: every statement raises a question in the reader's mind that the next level must answer — the *reader's* question tree is the document's structure; the tree is a strict tree by rule [H]. **Closure:** a sub-question is closed by an analysis; the parent by synthesis ("so-what") [H]. **Partial:** a hypothesis "largely confirmed"; the *day-one answer* revised weekly [H]. **Structure:** tree by discipline (MECE forbids DAG) [H].
2. **Separation:** workplan (who, which analysis, by when) is separate from the issue tree; the *storyline* is a third artefact [H]. A question ≠ a work item: an *analysis* is the work item and one analysis may inform several sub-questions (which MECE hides) [M].
3. **Failure modes.** *Boiling the ocean* (tree too wide), *hypothesis fixation* (child confirmed, parent unexamined), *non-MECE* overlaps (two children answer the same thing = merge), *orphan analyses* (work not on the tree) [H] (P, practitioner lore). Maintenance: weekly re-cut of the tree [H].
4. (P); no controlled evidence.

### 16. Education and heuristic pedagogy (essential questions, PBL driving questions, KWL, thesis RQ/SQ, Socratic aporia, Polya)

One sentence: learning is organised around questions that are chosen to be open, sub-questions the learner generates, and a record of what is known, wanted, and learned.

1. **Unit and relations.** Wiggins & McTighe *essential questions*: recurring, open, *not* meant to close — an open question kept open on purpose [H]. PBL (Buck Institute): a *driving question* and the students' *need-to-know list* — child questions generated by the learners, closed as they are researched [H]. *KWL* (Know / Want to know / Learned) — a per-learner question log [H]. Dissertation practice: RQ1..RQn with sub-questions SQ1.1…; contributions map to RQs [H]. Socratic *elenchus* ends in *aporia* — the question is made harder, not closed; Meno's paradox — you cannot seek what you do not know (questions that cannot arise) [H]. Polya (*How to Solve It*): *auxiliary problem* (serves the main one), *related problem*, *specialize/generalize*, *working backwards*, *inventor's paradox* (the more general question is easier) — a vocabulary of relations *between problems* [H]. **Closure:** the learner's articulated answer; for essential questions, never [H]. **Structure:** driving question → need-to-knows is a shallow tree; Polya's relations form a graph [H].
2. **Separation:** rubrics, grades, syllabi are bookkeeping; UbD explicitly separates *desired results* (questions) from *assessment evidence* from *learning plan* (work) — three layers [H].
3. **Failure modes.** *Pseudo-inquiry* (questions with a known answer posed as open); *need-to-knows never revisited* (PBL implementation reports) [M] (E, weak). *Auxiliary problem harder than the original* (Polya warns) [H].
4. UbD/PBL efficacy (E, mixed education research); Polya (P).

### 17. Knowledge management and Q&A communities (Zettelkasten, Stack Overflow, wikis)

One sentence: a body of knowledge is a network of notes or question–answer pairs where the identifier scheme and the meaning-links are deliberately different things.

1. **Unit and relations.** Luhmann's Zettelkasten: notes with *Folgezettel* ids (1, 1a, 1a1, 2…) — a branching id scheme that Luhmann himself said is *not* a content hierarchy but a fixed placement (*Stellordnung*) so that links can be made; the meaning is in the *links*, the tree is in the *ids* [H]. Stack Overflow: *question* with lifecycle — open; *closed* as duplicate / needs details or clarity / needs more focus (= should be split) / opinion-based / off-topic; *merged* (duplicate with answers folded in); *protected*, *locked*; the *accepted answer* (asker's closure) is distinct from the *top-voted* answer (community's closure) — two closure authorities on one question [H] (E). Wikis: "Open questions" sections; Wikipedia *citation needed* — an open question attached to a claim [H].
2. **Separation:** Zettelkasten is the cleanest deliberate separation of id-bookkeeping from structure in any family, and it is a *design choice* argued for in Luhmann's "Kommunikation mit Zettelkästen" [H]. SO separates *question text* (editable), *status* (close reason), and *identity* (numeric id with redirects on merge) [H]. A question is not a work item anywhere here [H].
3. **Failure modes.** SO's *unanswered* backlog and the *research on unresolved questions* on Stack Exchange ([arXiv 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.00964)) [M] (E). *Duplicate detection* as a first-class moderation task; *"too broad"* as the split signal; *accepted answer outdated* (the "accepted answer is wrong now" complaint — SO added *outdated answer* flags and the ability to unaccept) [H] (E). Zettelkasten: *orphan notes* (no links) as the recognised failure in the modern community [M].
4. SO behaviour (E); Zettelkasten efficacy (P, one famous user).

### 18. Lean/Toyota problem-solving (A3, 5 Whys, PDCA, hoshin catchball)

One sentence: a problem is worked on one sheet whose sections force a chain of questions from gap to root cause to countermeasure to follow-up, and the sheet is a thinking tool rather than a form.

1. **Unit:** the *problem* (gap between standard and actual); *5 Whys* is a chain (why → why…) that stops when the cause is actionable — a stopping rule by *actionability*, not truth [H]. A3 sections: background, current condition, goal, root-cause analysis, countermeasures, plan, follow-up — an ordered set of questions the author must answer; the *follow-up* section keeps the problem open past implementation until the effect is checked (PDCA's Check) [H]. Hoshin kanri: goals cascade with *catchball* (negotiated refinement up and down) — a tree with bidirectional editing of parents [H]. **Closure:** verified effect, not delivered countermeasure. **Structure:** chain (5 Whys), tree (hoshin), single sheet (A3) [H].
2. **Separation:** Shook (*Managing to Learn*) insists the A3 is a *process* and the paper is incidental; the same sections on a form filled mechanically is the named anti-pattern [H].
3. **Failure modes.** *Single-cause 5 Whys* (a tree pruned to a chain — the known critique) [H]. *Countermeasure without check* (question closed at Do, not at Check) [H]. Maintenance: *standard work* updated to hold the answer; the A3 archived [H].
4. (E) as documented practice; efficacy (E, case-study grade).

### Refused

- **Dung-style abstract argumentation frameworks**: the unit is the argument and the relation is attack; there is no question node, so "what closes a question" cannot be stated — refused.
- **Bloom's taxonomy / question classification schemes**: they sort questions by cognitive demand and have no relations between questions or lifecycle — refused.
- **Journalism's 5W1H**: a checklist of question forms, no structure or closure — refused.
- **Zeigarnik effect / psychology of open loops**: an effect about memory for unfinished tasks, not an account of a question structure — refused.
- **TRIZ**: its unit is a contradiction and its method a matrix of principles; mapping it to questions needs two sentences (contradiction → question, principle → answer-family), so refused by your rule.
- **Design thinking's "How might we"**: a phrasing convention inside family 3/15, not a family — refused as a slice.

## What the tasking smuggled in

- **"Tree."** Every family that formalises the structure ends up with something other than a tree: a lattice under entailment (inquisitive semantics), a DAG under AND/OR sharing (KAOS, GSN, fault trees, AND/OR graphs), a stack with bounded depth (Robert's Rules, QUD), a chain (ADRs, 5 Whys, Lakatos), a preorder (Belnap containment, complexity reductions). The tree appears where a *discipline* imposes it (WBS 100% rule, MECE, Aquinas's editors, Minto) or where the structure records the *order of asking* rather than the relations of content (e-scenarios, d-trees). PM practice keeps two diagrams — decomposition (tree) and dependence (DAG) — and never merges them. "Or perhaps a more general structure" hedges this but still centres the tree.
- **"Serve."** This is PHI's word (McCall), and it comes with PHI's baggage: a *quasi-hierarchy*, a restriction on the *order* in which issues may be raised, and the inclusion of *non-deliberated* issues. Roberts's subquestion is defined by *entailment of a partial answer*, IEL's by *validity of erotetic implication*, KAOS's by *AND/OR refinement*, Collingwood's dependence by *presupposition*. These are at least four different relations ("helps answer", "answering it partially answers the parent", "all of these are needed", "cannot be asked until") that "serve" flattens into one. The tasking also lists "presupposition, dependence, competition, equivalence" as if they were siblings of serving; in the families, presupposition is a *precondition on asking*, competition is a relation between *answers* not questions, and equivalence is a relation that triggers *merge*.
- **"Question" as the unit** (versus issue, goal, decision, hypothesis, problem, motion, requirement, claim). Each alternative unit carries a different closure semantics: a *goal* is satisfied (KAOS), a *claim* is supported by evidence (GSN), a *hypothesis* is eliminated (ACH) or corroborated, a *motion* is adopted or lost, a *decision* is taken and then only superseded (ADR), an *issue* is resolved by choosing a position (IBIS), a *requirement* is verified, a *problem* is solved relative to a theory (Laudan). "Question" imports the erotetic picture — closure by a *true answer* — which is the one picture that fits design work least (Rittel: no stopping rule; Ginzburg: resolvedness is goal-relative).
- **"Closed by a written answer."** Families close questions in at least nine other ways: *downdating without answer* (Ginzburg), *mootness/ripeness/standing* (law), *teiku* with a default rule (Talmud), *postpone indefinitely / object to consideration* (Robert's), *duplicate / won't fix / not planned* (trackers), *expiry* (LTIOV, stale bots), *supersession* (ADR/PEP), *elimination* (ACH, Popper), *satisficing* (i*). And several families close *by an act, not a document*: a vote, a judgment, a checker's `Qed`, a verified effect (PDCA Check). Where a document does close, several families make the *decision immutable* and put change in a *new* record (ADR, PEP, RFC) — the tasking's "written answer" says nothing about mutability.
- **"Partially answered (settled in part, leaning one way, still open on something)."** This runs together three distinct things the families keep apart: a *partial answer* proper (eliminates some alternatives — Belnap, Roberts), a *conditional answer* (settled given an open parent — conditional theorems, Rust "accepted with unresolved questions"), and a *provisional/graded* answer (a credence or certainty rating — GRADE, PEP Provisional, "accepted in principle"). "Leaning one way" is a credence, not a partial answer.
- **"Work is done when its questions are answered."** Fischer et al. and the spike/story split say the opposite ordering: construction and deliberation are different kinds, and argumentation matters only when it *serves* construction. Law closes a case by judgment with questions expressly unreached. Rust *merges the parent* with children open. PDCA closes on *checked effect*, not on answered questions. GTD closes a *project* on its *outcome*, not on the exhaustion of its actions. The equation of "body of work" with "long deliberation about it" in the first sentence is the same smuggling — IBIS vs PHI split on exactly whether non-deliberated issues belong in the structure.
- **"Each open or closed."** Binary status is the exception. Trackers have status × resolution; W3C has *postponed*; PEP has *Deferred/Provisional/Withdrawn*; DOORS has *suspect*; GSN has *undeveloped*; Robert's has *tabled / referred / postponed to a time*; the Talmud has *teiku*; intelligence has *expired*. Several of these are states *about the question's relation to others*, not about the question.
- **"Two things to separate."** Most families that separate at all separate into *three*: content/relations, process-state, and record/identity. Robert's Rules: motion / precedence-state / minutes. Bugzilla: report / status / resolution + id. Zettelkasten: links / (no process) / Folgezettel ids. DOORS: object text / link health / attributes. UbD: desired results / evidence / plan. Decision analysis: frame / model / decision record. Bundling "status words" with "identifiers, folders, naming rules" under one "bookkeeping" heading presupposes that process-state and identity belong together; the families mostly put process-state closer to the structure (suspect links, postponed, undeveloped are structural facts) and identity apart from both.
- **"A child answered while its parent has changed"** presupposes that a question keeps its identity across rewording. Van Fraassen (contrast class), Lakatos (lemma-incorporation), and Rittel ("the formulation is the problem") all say the reworded question is a *different* question; DOORS resolves this operationally (any change → suspect, human clears) rather than by an identity criterion; ADRs resolve it by forbidding change (supersede instead).
- **"Orphan questions"** presupposes a rooted structure in which every question ought to have a parent. Roberts supplies a Big Question root by stipulation; Collingwood's roots are *not questions* (absolute presuppositions); Rittel says there is no root; quodlibetal questions and SO questions are parentless by design; GTD's *Someday/Maybe* is a deliberate orphanage.
- **"Documents that answer."** The answer as a *document* is one family's convention (ADR, RFC, responsa, Summa); in others the answer is an artefact (JANUS: the design itself), a proof term, a vote, a verified metric, a judgment. The phrase "naming rules for the documents that answer" already decides that answers are files.
- **"Deliberation" and "work" as the same structure, and "tracking method" as the only other thing present.** Several families have a *third* structure the tasking does not mention: the *argument* (positions and arguments in IBIS; evidence in GSN; objections and replies in the quaestio; pro/con in Kialo-style maps). Whether the argument lives with the question, with the answer, or in its own layer is a decision the families make differently, and the tasking's framing has no slot for it.

## Nothing to take

Material in the families that looks portable and is not, plus the boundary of this report:

- **Truth-conditional answerhood** (families 1, 2) presupposes a fixed partition of possible worlds. A design question's alternatives are invented during deliberation (Rittel, QOC's consequent questions), so "partial answer = eliminates cells" has no cells to eliminate until someone draws them; the definitions transfer as metaphor only.
- **Parliamentary precedence** (family 9) presupposes a synchronous assembly with one pending main motion; it says nothing about many open questions worked asynchronously, which is the ordinary condition of a body of work.
- **Issue preclusion and ratio/dicta** (family 10) presuppose adversarial parties and a hierarchy of courts; the *test* ("actually litigated and necessarily decided") is portable as a phrase, its force is not.
- **Stack Overflow's two closure authorities** (asker vs crowd, family 17) presuppose a crowd; a project with one deciding human has only one.
- **Proof-assistant closure** (family 6) presupposes a checker that decides; where a human decides, `Qed` degenerates into "someone wrote Accepted", i.e. back to family 8.
- **DOORS suspect links** (family 5) presuppose that any edit to an end of a link is a candidate invalidation; in prose records where wording changes constantly this floods, and the tool's own literature says teams disable it — the mechanism transfers, the threshold does not.
- **PHI's "serve"** (family 3) came with an evaluated tool lineage (gIBIS, JANUS, Compendium) whose main empirical finding is the capture-cost asymmetry; the word can be borrowed without the finding, but the finding is what the lineage learned.
- **Efficacy claims** in families 7, 13, 15, 16, 18 are practitioner lore or case-study grade; nothing above is controlled evidence that any structuring of open questions improves outcomes. The only quantitative staleness figures found are EBM review half-life (family 12) and Stack Exchange unanswered-rate studies (family 17), both about their own domains.
- No recommendation, ordering, or verdict is offered here; the families are material about what the shape is an instance of, not an argument for any one of them.
