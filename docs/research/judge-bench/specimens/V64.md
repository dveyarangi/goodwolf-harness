## Question 6: a "how it stays in order" section

> **How it stays in order.**
> - **One home per rule.** A rule is written once, in its owner's rules file, and installed as a marked block into every skill that reads it. A block that drifts from its source, or that nothing owns, fails the check. A project's own answers and overrides go in one local file, installed last, each override naming the rule it replaces.
> - **Provisional is marked.** Text that a piece of open work will change is wrapped as a *straw dog* — `<straw-dog until="…" ticket="…">` — with the condition that retires it and the work that will. A script lists every one, so nothing provisional passes for settled.
> - **Rules that fail get amended.** When a rule was present and the agent didn't follow it, the case is registered: the rules in play, why it didn't fire, the rewording. The next occurrence grades the amendment — it holds, or the entry is struck.
> - **Every mechanism declares itself.** A part of the method that other work relies on states its parts, the moments a person acts on it, what it produces and who reads that. A script checks the declaration is true, and that core cites nothing only one project has.
> - **The checks travel.** An install ends by running them inside the receiving project, and the project can keep them in its own verification.

Each bullet points into core: the rules files and installed blocks, straw dogs and *Self-improvement* in AGENTS.md, the mechanism shape, and the harness's gate.
