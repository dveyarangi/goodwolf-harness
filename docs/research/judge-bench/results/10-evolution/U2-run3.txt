PART: The draft makes an "answer" the endpoint of the relation and fingerprints "the answer's text", but in this store an answer is not text or an entity: the entry's `answer` part is only a pointer line (a link to an ADR, doc, ticket or code, plus who and when), and `lean` is a single mutable line; the draft never examines what the script would actually hash or compare.
CONTRACT CHANGE: Once examined, what the relation promises changes. If the fingerprint covers only the store's answer/lean line, it will not fire when the decision it points to is edited, and it will fire on every routine lean rewrite (conclude rewrites leans each session). If it covers the linked document, the mechanism reaches across the store's boundary into docs, ADRs, tickets and code, with their own owners and homes. Either way the relation can no longer be a plain list of ids "like depends on". It becomes id-plus-fingerprint pairs, so the entry format, the move/rename handling, the check and the install all change. The earlier event-driven design (fires when B closes, is re-closed or is marked suspect) may be what survives, which would remove the fingerprint and the "script finds changes" claim.
REASONING: The draft commits to a shape: an `assumes` relation whose subjects are answers (or the lean of an open question), which records a fingerprint of the answer assumed so that a script, not the agent's memory, finds the change, with the agent still judging whether the assumption broke. The change of endpoints from questions to answers is the part the person queried, and the draft examined it. The draft's own recommendation, the fingerprint, rests on an unexamined premise. In the tree, the questions skill's record format shows `answer` as a link and attribution, "the answer is never the deliberation itself", and the argument lives in the body. A `close` points to a record elsewhere. The lean is one overwritten line. I also checked the draft's remark that the check already complains about a closed entry whose dependency is open again (questions.py around line 640). That holds, so it is not a problem. I considered whether the fingerprint choice is only local detail to settle later. It is not, because the choice decides what a stored relation is made of, what triggers it, and whether it crosses into other owners' records. The draft also puts the fingerprint choice to the person as a decision with a recommendation, which leaves its premise (that answers have text) unexamined.
