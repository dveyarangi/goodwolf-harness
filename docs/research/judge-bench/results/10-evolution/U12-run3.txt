PART: The draft makes the `## Decisions` list of a question both the holding place for decisions that have "nowhere to land" and a gate that `close` refuses to pass while any row lacks a `→`; it leaves unexamined how a row that by definition cannot land ever leaves, and how a closed child's answer pointer (`q-parent#decisions`) and `due` stay true once the row moves up or lands.
CONTRACT CHANGE: What the list promises changes. Either it is a transient queue every row must leave (so "nowhere to land" must mean "a home must be created", and the root-blocked case is a deadlock), or it is a final home (so `close` cannot refuse on it). Which one it is decides what a closed answer may cite, when the straw dogs waiting on that question come due (today `due` treats an answer citing any record other than the owner's as landed, so a pointer to a parent question would count as landed unless `due` is changed), and whether rows need an identity that survives being moved.
REASONING: The draft commits to moving the guard from the ticket to `close`: refuse while a row lacks `→`, move the row to the nearest open ancestor that contains everyone who needs it, let the root close only when all is landed, and have the child's answer point at the list so that `due` treats it as not yet arrived. I checked `due` in `questions.py`: it calls an answer landed when none of its links cites the owner's record, so a link to another question's entry counts as landed today. The draft therefore silently changes `due`, and it never says how `due` learns that the row has since landed. The pointer is to the list, not the row, and the row is moved rather than copied, so the pointer in a closed answer (which closed entries do not get rewritten) goes stale. Dogs bound to that question would either never fire or fire too early. The "nowhere to land" premise is also unchecked: a row defined as having no home can never gain a `→`, yet the root cannot close with such a row and cannot move it. The draft's claim that the tall-question inconvenience "almost disappears" is asserted, not checked. Several things pass the counter-test as local detail: the row syntax, the wording of the refusal, and the choice between a ticket and a question as the guard owner. This part does not pass it, because the answer-pointer and `due` contract and the root case reach straw dogs, closed records and the questions mechanism.
