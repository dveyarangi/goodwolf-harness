The first was an open child under a closed parent, a case the earlier exchange confirmed exists (q-0001.0010.0001): "nearest open ancestor" may then not exist. The second was the `due` semantics for a pointer answer. This is new, but it stays inside the questions mechanism and only delays a dog until the moved line lands at the ancestor's close, so it does not undo any accepted decision. The third was the claim that the ticket drops out of the picture. That is consistent with the ticket's own first criterion (`close` refuses a decision with no home) and with the person's earlier "tickets are outside the equation", so it was examined before. Whether "contains everyone who needs it" can be checked by the script is an implementation detail. The choice is put to the person with a recommendation, and its premises are consistent with the tree as I read it.
