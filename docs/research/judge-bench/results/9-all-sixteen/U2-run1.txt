CONTRACT CHANGE: Once examined, the relation cannot be answer-to-answer with a text fingerprint without either changing the store's unit (the open question; one entry per question) and decision 43 (the answer stays a pointer, never held), or reaching across the boundary into decision records in docs/ (whose text changes for reasons unrelated to the answer, firing falsely); a lean fingerprint also fires on every lean rewrite or reword, contradicting "same question means same answers, never same wording" and the draft's own "a reword fires nothing". The viable contract is likely still question-keyed (entry A rests on question B, with the firing events being the store's own close/re-close/suspect/lean calls), or answers must first be given an identity (for example a version or strike counter), which changes what depends on the store and who may write the relation.

I checked the questions mechanism and questions.py: the store's unit is the open question, "a closed question points to its answer and never holds it", an answer is a link to a record, a lean is one part that is replaced by the `lean` call, and there is no answer history or per-answer identity. Fingerprinting "the text of B's answer" therefore has nothing in the store to hash except a link string or a lean line (which changes whenever the agent rewrites it, so the script would report constant false breaks), or it hashes a decision doc outside the store. Moving the subject from question to answer is the person's own correction, but whether answers exist as addressable things is the premise that decides if the mechanism is a store-level script feature at all. The choice put to the person (date vs fingerprint) leaves this premise unexamined, and it is not local detail: it changes what the relation stores, who detects the change and which records depend on it.
