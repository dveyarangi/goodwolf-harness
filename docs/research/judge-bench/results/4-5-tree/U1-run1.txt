PART: The proposed rule writes the assumes link only for "a position that is still live" and leaves out premises "nobody expects to move" (ground), yet the draft's own argument is that when a link is written nobody knows what change will come, so what counts as ground is not knowable at write time and ground can later move.
CONTRACT CHANGE: The link's promise, that a change in an assumed answer finds every answer resting on it, would hold only for premises someone expected to move. A change in a premise written off as ground would find nothing, silently. Either every premise is written (so the link's cost and noise change and "ground" becomes a derived or read-time notion), or ground is a recorded state that can be revoked when the project goes liquid again (a new record type and a trigger). The soil/liquid gradient the draft names as the user's observation would then be a property of the stored links, not of whether a link is written at all.
REASONING: The draft commits to one non-blocking assumes link, with consequences (answer suspect vs question reworded or moot) judged at the change, and with depends on cut back to ordering later. The draft's new justification for collapsing the two kinds is that the consequence is unknowable when the link is written, yet it admits the same unknowability into the write criterion ("a premise nobody expects to move is ground and is not written"). That contradiction goes to the link's coverage guarantee and to what the script can mechanically find, which the record's earlier design relies on (pinning, printing the dependents at each touch of B's answer). I considered the fate of depends on and its existing re-examination role, but the draft explicitly defers that, so it is settleable later. Dropping the script-derived pinning from the earlier draft is a smaller detail. The ground criterion is the part that fails the counter-test.
