PART: The draft makes answers the endpoints of the relation and fingerprints the answer text, but it never checks what an answer is in the store: a closed question holds only a link to the record that decided it, and a lean is a store field, so an answer's text is not an addressable thing the store owns.
CONTRACT CHANGE: What the relation points at, what is fingerprinted and who holds it changes. It could point at a question plus the state of its decision link, at the linked record's content (which reaches from the store, which is core, into docs/, which core may not depend on), or at an answer history the store does not have yet. That changes the boundary between the store and the decision records, what counts as "the answer changed" (any edit to a linked doc versus a new closing), and what the check can report on its own.
REASONING: The draft commits to a shape: `assumes` is a relation whose subjects are answers or leans, which records a fingerprint of the answer it rested on, so that a script finds the change and the agent judges whether the assumption broke. I considered several parts. The depends-on comparison is fine; I confirmed in questions.py that the check does complain about a closed question depending on an open one. The choice of date versus fingerprint is put to the person, but its premise is wrong, since the store keeps no answer text. The store's answer part is checked by `_answer_problems`, which has a comment "a closed question points to its answer and never holds it". A decided answer must be a link to a doc, ADR or code, and merged, superseded and deferred answers are an id or a condition. A fingerprint of "the answer text" would therefore have to read the linked records. That crosses the store-to-docs boundary and the core and instance rule, and a fingerprint of a whole doc would change on unrelated edits. A decision under a different question may also be linked, so several questions can share one answer. Moving the endpoints from questions to answers rests on answers being first-class objects with identity, and in this store they are not. Settling this later would rewrite the relation's shape, the script and the check, so it is load-bearing rather than local detail.
