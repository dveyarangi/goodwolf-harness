PART: The draft makes the filename position number the sole carrier of queue priority, without examining that a position is nested under its parent's id (depth means "is a child of") and that the format forbids renumbering, so position cannot express a cross-parent global order without reparenting or renaming tickets.
CONTRACT CHANGE: The proposed carrier cannot be "position only" as stated. Either priority gets a separate key (a header field or an ordering record), which reopens the ticket format and the 01-0011.0040 derived-index design, or ordering is allowed to rename and reparent tickets. That changes what a ticket id means, what citations point at, and what the pacer owns.
REASONING: The draft commits to a shape: the pacer's rules file installs ordering duties into /maintain, /ticket, /recall and /align, and the position number is the one carrier, with the table derived from it. I checked TICKET-FORMAT.md. Position is "global, changes when priority changes", but a child appends its position to the parent's id ("01-0010.0110"), depth means only "is a child of", and the format says "Never renumber anything else". The queue's tickets live under several parents (01-0010.*, 01-0011.*, 01-0012, 01-0014 and so on), so sorting by filename keeps every 01-0010.* ticket before 01-0011.* and 01-0012 whatever their priority. Putting .0110 after 01-0011.0040, or ahead of 01-0012, would mean moving it to another parent, which falsifies its lineage, or renumbering files that every record cites by relative link. The draft says the format "already declares" position as priority and leaves it there. That claim rests on a premise the format itself contradicts, and it was not examined, so the "derive the table from position" step and the "renumber positions" duty may not be implementable. Secondary: 01-0020's scope (turn and step pacing, resume state) does not name queue ordering, although the mechanism doc's "not yet" row binds it there, so the ownership claim is thinner than "already declared". The judgment-versus-field caveat was examined and deferred, so it passes. The carrier and key question is load-bearing because it crosses the ticket format, the derived index and the id and citation scheme, and settling it later would change the shape.
