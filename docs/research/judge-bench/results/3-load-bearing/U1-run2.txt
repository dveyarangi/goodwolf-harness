PART: The rule that a premise "nobody expects to move is ground and is not written" is adopted without examining who decides what is ground, when, and what happens when a ground premise does move.
CONTRACT CHANGE: The question's own promise is that a change in assumed answers finds the dependent answer. With ground unwritten, that promise holds only for premises someone foresaw moving, so an unforeseen shift (the case the draft itself says cannot be known at write time) reaches no one. Examining it forces a choice: write the link for every premise, add a way to detect or promote ground on a move, or narrow the promise to foreseen moves. It also changes what depends on remains for, since blocking is dropped and ordering is the only thing left.
REASONING: The draft commits to one non-blocking "assumes" link, with consequences judged at the change (untouched, answer suspect, or question reworded/moot), a "ground" exemption, and depends on reduced to ordering, though that last step is deferred. The earlier exchanges examined the depends-versus-assumes split and the draft handles it well, so that is not the gap. The draft's central argument is that nobody knows at write time which change will come. The ground exemption uses the same unknowable, expectation of movement, as its filter, which undercuts the argument. Because the exemption decides which answers the mechanism can ever find, it sets the mechanism's promise and boundary, and it cannot be settled locally afterwards. Candidates that pass the counter-test: the exact judging labels, the later fate of depends on (the draft defers it), and the wording of the block. Those can be settled locally without changing the contract.
