PART: Folding the mechanism-shape files and the mechanism's own doc/rules/skill into one `output` row gives that row a single stamp, so re-reading in one direction (doc against shape) re-stamps the whole row and silently clears the other direction (records against the doc) that never got re-read.
CONTRACT CHANGE: The `output` row would either have to split into two stamped pairs (shape-to-doc and doc-to-records), which is the third kind of row the draft says it avoids, or its re-check outcome would have to record which direction was verified. Either way, what "output is current" promises and what a stamp means change.
REASONING: The draft commits to a shape: two rows per mechanism (`parts` and `output`), with the shape files merged into `output` to avoid a third row kind, plus three re-check outcomes and a manual migration. Candidates: the `parts` scope, which is the Install-adds table without Relies-on, and the entry file leaving the doc surface. Both are put to the person as decisions with their stated reasons, so they count as examined. The `parts` scope also holds up locally, because it only picks which files go into a fingerprint. The `repaired` label and the manual `rules` migration are local detail that can be settled later without changing the contract. The merged `output` row is different. The draft says the clock "names which file moved" and the maintainer infers the direction, but the draft never says what a stamp covers. A stamp fingerprints the whole file set, so one pass in one direction hides the unfinished other direction. That is the very kind of silent staleness the ticket exists to remove, and it reaches the clock's semantics, the label format and the maintain procedure. It looks unexamined in the draft and is not a local detail. Confidence is only medium, because the truncated RFC may already have settled it.
