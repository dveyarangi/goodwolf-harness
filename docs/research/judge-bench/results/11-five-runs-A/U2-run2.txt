REASONING: The draft commits to a relation `assumes` whose ends are answers (or the lean of an open question), non-blocking, born with the answer that writes it and dying with it, and pinned to a fingerprint of the target answer so a script rather than the agent's memory finds a stale one. Its claim of absence about depends on holds: the tree's check (_unmarked_suspects in questions.py) already reports a closed entry whose dependency is open again and which is not suspect, so depends on does carry a little assumption, as the draft says. Its claim that a lean has no date also holds, since a lean is a bare value in the entry. I considered three candidate parts: (1) that answers are not addressable entities in the store, so "relation between answers" is stored on the question entry; (2) that a fingerprint of a lean fires on every lean rewrite, which departs from the earlier "fires only on close, reclose or suspect"; (3) that re-declaring on a new answer could silently drop a needed link, and that `clear` must re-pin. Each of these is a firing rule, a storage detail or a lifetime detail inside the mechanism. Each can be settled at /align or in the RFC without changing what the draft commits to: the relation's meaning, its direction, its non-blocking nature and its place beside part of and depends on. The draft itself defers the form to /align and puts the pinning choice to the person with a stated premise, so it does not close off the contract.
