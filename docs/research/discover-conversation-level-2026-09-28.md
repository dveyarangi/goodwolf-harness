Run on the shape as it stood after the align's turn: the dynamic level of a conversation about
work against the fixed levels of a method's document kinds; steering; the down/up asymmetry; drift
against warranted descent; writing the left height down; an external observer. Described in no

### Families

**1. Computational discourse structure and dialogue management** (Grosz & Sidner 1986; Roberts / Ginzburg "questions under discussion"; Larsson & Traum's information-state update, GoDiS/TrindiKit). *Mapping:* the conversation's current level is the top of an attentional stack (QUD stack / focus stack) whose entries are subordinate to purposes held in a separate, more slowly changing plan. Grosz & Sidner split discourse into linguistic, *intentional* (a hierarchy of discourse-segment purposes related by dominance and satisfaction-precedence) and *attentional* (a stack of focus spaces pushed/popped as talk moves) structures. QUD: relevance means addressing the maximal question under discussion; sub-questions form "strategies of inquiry" under a super-question. GoDiS keeps a fixed *plan*, a dynamic *agenda* and a dynamic *QUD*; an utterance answering a question not yet raised triggers *accommodation* — the system retrieves the question from the plan and pushes it. So: two axes coupled by accommodation — fixed plan/intentions, dynamic attention (H, E for the models existing; M for empirical adequacy). Detection is by relevance-to-QUD failure: a move addressing no open question is accommodated or flagged irrelevant (H, E — implemented systems). Grosz & Sidner name the drift/warranted split directly: a *subdialogue* is dominated by the parent purpose (clarification, correction) and the stack resumes when it pops; a *true interruption* or *digression* pushes a focus space with no dominance link and the parent may not be resumed; *semantic returns* ("anyway", "as I was saying") are pop markers (H, E from corpora, small). Asymmetry: the stack model predicts down-danger only as stack overflow/forgetting; Walker's *cache* model argued the stack overstates recoverability — humans lose popped-to contexts unless re-mentioned (M, E). Record: the stack *is* the record, nothing extra written — which is why unfinished parents are lost when the stack is corrupted. Observer: dialogue managers already are an external state-tracker separate from the generator (H, P).

**2. Conversation analysis and institutional talk** (Sacks, Schegloff, Jefferson; Goffman "footing"; Suchman & Jordan 1990). *Mapping:* the level is a participants' accomplishment displayed in sequence — an inserted question-answer pair inside an unfinished pair, a stepwise topic shift, a change of footing — never a hidden variable. Units: adjacency pairs, *insertion sequences* (a second Q–A nested inside a first pair before its answer is due), *side sequences* (Jefferson: repair episodes with an explicit return), *stepwise topic transition* (topic changes by small linked steps so nobody can say where it changed), *misplacement markers* ("by the way", "before I forget") flagging the speaker's own shift; Goffman's *footing*, continually shifted and marked prosodically. Axes: CA recognises only the sequence, but institutional-talk research (Drew & Heritage) treats the agenda, form or survey instrument as a fixed structure talk is held to; Suchman & Jordan showed standardised survey questions colliding with conversational relevance, interviewers covertly departing from the script (H, E). Detection: participants detect it themselves and mark it; a first-pair-part with no second is *noticeably absent* and accountable — the hole is not silently forgotten, it is heard (H, E). This directly disputes the premise that the answering process cannot monitor itself: talk is full of self-monitoring tokens. Drift vs warranted: insertion sequences are by definition warranted; stepwise transition is how unwarranted drift happens without being sanctionable — drift is *achieved smoothly*, so a detector looking for a jump will miss it (H, E). Asymmetry: none in the tasking's sense; the danger named is *conditional relevance left unmet*. Record: none — return markers do the work. Observer: the analyst, post hoc, with transcripts; CA rejects real-time coding schemas as losing what participants orient to (H, P).

**3. Rhetoric and formal dialectic** (stasis theory; Hamblin; Walton & Krabbe 1995; McBurney & Parsons deliberation dialogues). *Mapping:* the level is the *stasis* — the point on a fixed ladder of questions at which a dispute actually stands — and a shift is a dialectical shift from one embedded dialogue type to another. Stasis (Hermagoras, Cicero): conjecture (did it happen / does it exist), definition (what is it), quality (is it good, justified), policy (what should be done) — ordered; a dispute must be located at one stasis or the parties talk past each other. Walton & Krabbe: dialogue *types* (persuasion, inquiry, negotiation, deliberation, information-seeking, eristic) each with goals and rules; a *dialectical shift* is *licit* if the embedded dialogue serves the original's goal, *illicit* if it hijacks it — many classical fallacies are illicit shifts. Hamblin's *commitment stores* record each party's standing commitments turn by turn. McBurney & Parsons: a fixed stage sequence for deliberation (open, inform, propose, consider, revise, recommend, confirm, close) with a dynamic move set. Axes: two — type/stage fixed, embedding dynamic (H, E the theories exist; M that they describe real talk). Detection: a move not legal in the current dialogue type signals a shift (H, P). Drift vs warranted = illicit vs licit, criterion stated: does the sub-dialogue's closure advance the parent's goal (H, P). Asymmetry: stasis has a strong *down*-rule — you cannot argue policy while definition is contested, the lower stasis is settled first; the ordering runs from fact upward to policy, so their "bottom" is the tasking's "top" — the ladder's orientation is a choice (H, P). Record: the commitment store is exactly a robust written state of the deliberation so far, including what is conceded but not concluded (H, P). Observer: the referee of a regulated dialogue applies the rules (M, P).

**4. Facilitation and parliamentary procedure** (Doyle & Straus "Interaction Method"; Kaner; Schwarz; Robert's Rules of Order). *Mapping:* the level of the meeting is "the question before the house" atop a ranked stack of pending motions, while a facilitator neutral on content watches process and a recorder keeps group memory. Robert's Rules: a fixed precedence of motions; an amendment is a descent below a main motion and must be disposed of before the main question resumes; *division of the question* splits a motion found to contain two things; *lay on the table* parks the question with a rule that it dies if not taken up by the next session; *postpone to a certain time* schedules resumption; *point of order* and *appeal from the chair* are meta-level moves anyone can make. Doyle & Straus: separate *content* from *process*; the facilitator owns process only; the *recorder* writes *group memory* in the room's view; a *parking lot* holds displaced items. Kaner: divergent → groan zone → convergent, the facilitator naming which zone the group is in. Axes: two — agenda fixed, the live stack dynamic (H, E for codified rules; M for facilitation efficacy). Detection: the chair rules a remark *out of order* when it does not address the pending question — a per-utterance relevance judgement by a designated non-participant (H, P/E). Asymmetry: procedure guards *down* — amendments to amendments limited to two degrees, tabled items expire, the hole prevented by a clock, not by writing; going up (reconsider, rescind) is deliberately made *harder* — reconsideration needs a member who voted with the prevailing side and is time-limited — so the codified danger is *both* directions, with up rationed to protect settled decisions (H, E). Drift vs warranted: *division of the question* is the warranted case; *bikeshedding* / Parkinson's law of triviality the named drift (M, P). Record: minutes record only what was decided plus pending business; Doyle & Straus's group memory records half-formed states explicitly (H, P). Observer: the facilitator role is the family's central invention; Schwarz insists the observer stay content-neutral because content involvement destroys the ability to see process (H, P; outcome evidence weak).

**5. Negotiation** (Fisher & Ury; Raiffa; Susskind; framework-agreement practice). *Mapping:* the level is whether the parties are talking interests, options, criteria or wording, with a written single text carrying the half-agreed state between rounds. *Interests* below *positions*; "go to the balcony" (Ury) — step out to observe the process. Framework agreement → protocols/annexes is the fixed document ladder; "agreement in principle" then details. *Single-text procedure* (Camp David): a mediator's one draft criticised and revised in rounds. Axes: two, the fixed one often agreed at the outset as the *process agreement* (M, E anecdotal). Asymmetry *inverted*: the danger named is *reopening* — going up to renegotiate a principle after details were traded on it — hence "nothing is agreed until everything is agreed" (WTO single undertaking), which makes reopening free but settling hard; and "nibbling" — dropping to detail to extract concessions after the principle is set (M, P). Drift vs warranted: a return to interests is warranted when positions deadlock; positional bargaining is the drift (H, P). Record: the single text is a deliberately half-formed written state designed for resumption, with bracketed language marking open points — the brackets are the "which way it leans / what is still open" notation (H, E — treaty drafting practice). Observer: mediator; "the balcony" is self-observation, which the family admits is hard under pressure (M, P).

**6. Tutoring and metacognition in problem solving** (Wood, Bruner & Ross scaffolding; Schoenfeld 1985/1992; Sinclair & Coulthard exchange structure). *Mapping:* the level is the degree of specificity at which the more capable party currently intervenes, and the learner's dive into execution without returning to planning is the documented failure. Wood's *contingent instruction*: five levels of help from general verbal prompt to demonstration; after learner success move up one level, after failure move down one (H, E — Wood, Wood & Middleton 1978 and replications). Schoenfeld coded problem-solving protocols into episodes — read, analyse, explore, plan, implement, verify — and drew timelines: novices spend nearly all time in explore/implement and never return; experts cycle up to analyse/plan (H, E — protocol studies). Sinclair & Coulthard's rank scale (lesson > transaction > exchange > move > act) with *boundary exchanges* ("Right. Now…") marking transitions (H, E). Axes: Wood and Schoenfeld one axis; Sinclair–Coulthard a fixed structure the live talk instantiates. Detection: Schoenfeld's fix was an outside observer asking at intervals — "What exactly are you doing? Why are you doing it? How does it help?" — later internalised by students, with improved control behaviour reported (M, E — small-n classroom studies). Asymmetry: strongly *down*: the "wild goose chase" into implementation is the named pathology, the metacognitive return upward is what expertise looks like (H, E). Drift vs warranted: Schoenfeld's criterion is whether the excursion produces information that changes the plan; Kapur's *productive failure* argues a descent that fails is warranted pedagogically (M, E, contested). Record: none formalised beyond the plan written before implementing (P). Observer: teacher, then internalised — the family's evidence that an external prompt can be handed over is its distinctive contribution (M, E).

**7. Heuristic of mathematical discovery** (Pólya; Lakatos "Proofs and Refutations"). *Mapping:* the level is the generality of the statement under attack, and a counterexample found in detail is classified by whether it refutes only a lemma or the conjecture itself. Pólya: understand, plan, carry out, look back; *specialise* (down), *generalise* / the *inventor's paradox* (going up can be easier than the specific problem), *vary the problem*. Lakatos: *local counterexample* (refutes a lemma) vs *global* (refutes the theorem); *lemma-incorporation* rewrites the theorem to include the hidden lemma as a condition; *monster-barring* refuses the counterexample by redefining the term; *hidden lemma* is precisely "a term at the higher level hid an assumption". Axes: one (H, E — the texts; M for descriptive adequacy). Drift vs warranted is the family's core distinction, with a test: does the detail-level finding propagate upward (global) or stay local? The response to a global counterexample is to go up and change the statement, not patch below (H, P). Asymmetry: the inventor's paradox is an *up-is-safer* claim; monster-barring the pathology of refusing to go up (H, P). Record: the evolving theorem statement with its accumulated conditions is the written state; each incorporation records why (P).

**8. Psychotherapy** (Motivational Interviewing; process vs content commentary — Yalom; rupture repair — Safran & Muran; psychoanalysis as counter-case). *Mapping:* the level is which of the method's processes the session is in, and dropping to planning or advice before the client's ambivalence is resolved is the named error. MI (Miller & Rollnick): engaging, focusing, evoking, planning — ordered but recursive; named traps: *premature focus trap*, *expert trap*, *righting reflex*. Yalom: *process commentary* — talk about what is happening between us, distinct from content. Axes: fixed process sequence vs live utterance codes — two, and the MITI coding instrument makes both explicit (H, E). Detection and observer: MITI has trained external coders classify each therapist utterance into a fixed schema from recordings; inter-rater reliability established; fidelity scores predict client change talk (H, E). Asymmetry: strongly *down*: premature planning produces *sustain talk* / resistance; the link between counselor confrontation and client resistance within-session is among the better-replicated findings (H/M, E — Miller, Benefield & Tonigan 1993 and sequential analyses). Drift vs warranted: following the client into detail is warranted when it is *change talk*; the counselor's own drop to advice is the drift (H, P). Psychoanalysis inverts the shape: free association treats drift as the material and the analyst's interpretation of the shift is the work — a family in which detecting the shift is the therapy, not a correction (H, P). Record: MI's *summary reflection* before transitioning between processes is a spoken write-down of the state — what is settled, which way the client leans, what is open — explicitly recommended before moving to planning (H, P).

**9. Design methods** (Rittel & Webber wicked problems; Schön reflective practice; Dorst & Cross co-evolution; IBIS and Conklin's Dialogue Mapping). *Mapping:* problem and solution are two co-evolving spaces and the level of talk is which space is being explored, with the issue map as an externally maintained record of the deliberation. Rittel: wicked problems have no definitive formulation; "the information needed to understand the problem depends on one's idea for solving it". Schön: *problem setting* vs *problem solving*, *reflection-in-action*, the situation "talks back", *frame experiments*. Dorst & Cross (2001): designers iterate between problem space and solution space, with a *creative bridge* when a partial solution reframes the problem (H, E — small-n protocol study). IBIS: issues, positions, arguments; Conklin's *dialogue mapping*: a facilitator builds the IBIS map live on a shared display as the group talks. Axes: one axis of two spaces; documents are snapshots of the map, not levels (H, P). The family *denies* the asymmetry: descent into solution detail is how the higher-level problem is discovered, so the tasking's "drift" is this family's method — warranted only if the return trip is made (Schön's reflection) (H, P; M, E). Detection: the mapper notices when a contribution has no issue to attach to and *asks* for the issue — mapping forces classification of each utterance to a node type and its parent, exactly the tasking's classifier, done by a human in the room (H, P; E from practice reports). Record: an issue with positions and arguments but no decision is the written half-formed state; groups lose the ability to resume unless the map exists (M, E — case reports). Observer: the mapper does not contribute content (H, P).

**10. Cognitive engineering and high-reliability operations** (Rasmussen's abstraction hierarchy; plan continuation bias; Crew Resource Management; mission command). *Mapping:* the level is the position in a means-ends hierarchy where each level is the *how* of the one above and the *why* of the one below, and the operating pathology is fixation at a low level while the higher-level goal has become invalid. Rasmussen (1985): functional purpose → abstract function → generalised function → physical function → physical form; troubleshooters move *up* the hierarchy when stuck and down to act (H, E — electronics-technician studies; M for generality). Aviation: *plan continuation bias* / "get-there-itis" — continuing a descent as conditions invalidate the plan (H, E — accident analyses, Orasanu). CRM: *pilot flying / pilot monitoring* role split with mandatory *callouts* on a fixed schema ("sink rate", "unstable, go around") and *challenge-and-response* checklists (H, E — adoption; simulator studies). Mission command: *commander's intent* stated in writing so subordinates can regenerate lower-level plans when the situation changes; understand the intent "two levels up" (H, P). Axes: *one* — the artifact hierarchy and the reasoner's traversal are the same ladder; the fixed documents (orders at each echelon) are the hierarchy instantiated. Detection: the monitoring pilot's callouts are exactly the tasking's fixed-schema external observer; the schema is fixed because a free-text observer under load fails, and because the flying pilot is *known* to be unable to monitor themselves while acting — the cognitive-tunneling literature supports the premise (H, E — Wickens, Dismukes). Asymmetry: strongly *down*: fixation on execution is the killer; the up-move (go-around, abort) is made cheap and non-blaming by rule ("any crew member may call it, no justification required") — a *designed* asymmetry (H, E). Drift vs warranted: a descent is warranted when it is a *step of the plan*; unwarranted when the plan's premise has expired — the check is against the higher-level premise, not the detail's content (H, P). Record: the intent statement; in ATC/ops handovers, a structured brief of the current state exists for resumption by another head (H, P).

**11. Cognitive architectures and hierarchical planning** (Newell & Simon GPS; Soar impasses and subgoaling; HTN planning; least-commitment). *Mapping:* the level is the depth of the goal stack, and descent is permitted only when the level above hits an impasse. GPS: means-ends analysis. Soar: an *impasse* (tie, conflict, no-change) *automatically* creates a subgoal; the subgoal's result is *chunked* into a rule so the parent continues; the goal stack holds every suspended level's state. HTN: a fixed method library decomposes tasks; least-commitment planning leaves choices open until forced. Axes: two — the method library fixed, the task network dynamic (H, E — implemented systems). Soar answers by construction: every descent is warranted because only an impasse causes one, and every descent is *recorded* because the parent state stays on the stack; drift is impossible by architecture (H, P). Known failure: the impasse types are fixed, so descents needed for reasons outside the type set never happen, and deep stacks are not garbage-collected (M, P). Least-commitment planning is the family's contribution on *up*: leave the higher-level decision partial rather than fix it and redo (H, P).

**12. Control theory and cybernetics** (cascade/supervisory control; state observers; Powers's Perceptual Control Theory; Argyris's double-loop learning). *Mapping:* each level of talk is a control loop whose output is the setpoint of the loop below, and the external judge is a state observer estimating a variable the controller cannot read directly. Cascade control: the outer loop sets the inner's reference; the inner loop must be much faster. Luenberger observer / Kalman filter reconstruct unobservable state. Powers (PCT): a hierarchy of perceptual control systems; conflict at one level is resolved by *reorganisation* at a higher one. Argyris & Schön: *single-loop* vs *double-loop*; *Model I* defensive routines prevent the up-move. Axes: one hierarchy; documents are setpoints (H, E engineering; M for PCT and Argyris). The family says almost nothing about drift and cannot distinguish it from warranted descent; its contributions: (a) the separation principle — controller and observer are designed separately and the observer needs a model of the plant, not just its output, so a classifier with no model of the task will be a poor observer (H, P); (b) Ashby's requisite variety — a fixed-schema observer has less variety than the talk and will alias distinct shifts (H, P); (c) time-scale separation — higher levels should change more slowly, a design principle, not a danger direction (H, P); (d) Argyris: the *up* move is the one humans avoid, and organisations need an outside interventionist to see the governing variables (M, E — case studies). Record: the setpoint; nothing half-formed is representable — a limitation of the mapping.

**13. Philosophy and psychology of action** (Anscombe; Bratman; Collingwood; Vallacher & Wegner). *Mapping:* one action falls under a chain of descriptions linked by "why?", and the level of talk is which description in that chain is being asked about. Anscombe's *A–D order*: each "why?" ascends, each "how?" descends, and there is a description under which the agent stops answering "why" — the intention. Bratman: intentions are *partial plans* filled in top-down over time; norms of *means-end coherence* and *consistency*; the *non-reconsideration default* — a settled intention is not reopened without a trigger, because reconsidering is costly and destabilising (H, P). Collingwood's logic of question and answer: a question *arises* only when its presupposition is settled; so a lower question asked before the higher is settled "does not arise" — it is malformed, not merely premature (H, P). Vallacher & Wegner *action identification theory*: people hold an action under a prepotent identity; when the action cannot be maintained at that level they *drop* to a lower identity; mastery brings *emergence* back upward (H, E — experimental programme). Axes: one. On asymmetry the family is diagnostic rather than normative: a drop in level is a *symptom* of trouble at the higher level, so detecting descent is detecting a failure that already happened (H, E). Bratman supplies the up-side asymmetry: reopening is rationed by default and needs a reason (H, P). Collingwood supplies a test for drift: is the lower question's presupposition an answer already given? If not, the descent is malformed (H, P). Record: Bratman's partial plan is a half-formed decision by definition — what is fixed, what is left open (H, P).

**14. Software engineering method** (Parnas & Clements 1986; Cockburn's use-case goal levels; V-model; XP spikes; ADRs/RFCs; pair programming; folk terms). *Mapping:* a fixed ladder of documents from purpose to code is maintained as if produced top-down, while the actual work moves up and down it with named moves for the excursions. Parnas & Clements "A Rational Design Process: How and Why to Fake It": the ideal document sequence is never followed, but the documents are written as if it were, and each is updated when an excursion changes it — the fixed axis is a deliberate fiction (H, P). Cockburn: use-case *goal levels* with an altitude metaphor — cloud, kite, *sea level*, fish, clam — and "ask why to go up, ask how to go down"; a step at one level is a use case at the level below; one axis, the artifact kind determined by altitude (H, P/E). V-model: the descending leg (requirements, architecture, design, code) paired with ascending verification levels — "what must be observably true" is *paired* with each level rather than stacked between them (H, P). XP *spike*: a time-boxed, throwaway dive into detail to answer a planning-level question — a named warranted descent with two safeguards, time-box and discard (H, P). ADR/RFC status *proposed* with an *open questions* section: the written half-formed decision (H, P). Pair programming *driver/navigator*: navigator watches strategy while driver types (H, E the practice exists; M/E modest quality gain — Hannay et al. 2009; no measurement of level-drift detection). Folk pathology names: *yak shaving* (a chain of descents each locally justified), *rabbit hole*, *bikeshedding*, *premature optimisation* — all down; the up-direction has names too — *architecture astronautics*, *analysis paralysis*, *gold-plating requirements* — so the field's lore is symmetric even if the tasking's is not (H, P). Drift vs warranted: the spike's test is that it was *asked for by an open question at the higher level* and is *time-boxed*; a descent nobody asked for is the drift (H, P). Record: Parnas's documents, the ADR, the ticket's open-questions section — the most explicit write-down discipline of all families and the most complaints that it is not kept (H, P). Observer: navigator, reviewer, "five whys" as an ascending questioner (M, P).

**15. Editing and technical writing** (Van Buren & Buehler, "The Levels of Edit", JPL 1980). *Mapping:* edits are ordered by scope and the rule is not to perform a lower-scope edit on text that a higher-scope edit may remove. Nine edit types in five levels from policy/format through substantive down to mechanical; developmental before line before copy before proof. Axes: one (H, E). The asymmetry and its rationale are stated identically to the tasking: polishing sentences that will be cut is waste, and it *anchors* the author to the current structure, making the structural edit harder — the down-move not only wastes but corrupts the up-move (H, P; anchoring is lore). Drift vs warranted: a copy-level problem that reveals a structural one is the warranted case, and editors are trained to escalate it as a *query* rather than fix it — the query is a written half-formed note in the margin (H, P). Observer: the editor is by role external to the author; that authors cannot see their own structure is the family's founding assumption (H, P).

**16. Law: appellate structure and trial management.** *Mapping:* questions of law sit above questions of fact and the procedure rations movement between them. Pleadings frame the issues; trial finds facts; appeal reviews law with a *standard of review* per question type; *remand* sends the matter down with instructions to redo the lower level; *bifurcation* decides liability before damages. Axes: two — the record on appeal (fixed) and the live argument; appellate courts refuse what is not in the record (H, E). Asymmetry *inverted and codified*: the *final judgment rule* forbids going up mid-stream except by certified *interlocutory* question, because piecemeal up-moves are disruptive; going down (remand) is routine (H, E). The hole is handled by *issue preservation/waiver*: what you did not raise below you cannot raise above — closed by forfeiture, not by return (H, E). Record: a court may *reserve* a question and decide it later — the written half-formed state (H, E). Drift vs warranted: a fact question that hides a legal one is *certified* upward — the warranted case has a procedure (H, E). Observer: the judge on relevance objections, per utterance, with fixed grounds (H, E).

### What the tasking smuggled in

- **Four levels with fixed names and order.** Several families order differently or pair rather than stack: stasis runs fact → definition → quality → policy, so its "bottom" is the tasking's "top"; the V-model pairs acceptance criteria with each level instead of inserting them as one; Schoenfeld's episodes and MI's processes are phases, not a purpose–means ladder. The count and ordering are the method's, presented as the shape's.
- **"Level" as a purpose–means ladder at all.** Anscombe, Rasmussen and Cockburn support that reading; CA, Goffman and psychoanalysis have no ladder — footing, sequence, association. The word "level" preselects the ladder families.
- **The asymmetry stated as fact.** Negotiation, parliamentary procedure, Bratman and law all ration the *up* move and treat reopening as the danger; design methods deny the asymmetry; Vallacher & Wegner make descent a symptom, not a cause. The families that agree (Schoenfeld, MI, aviation, editing) share a feature: a party who *acts* on the lower level under time pressure. That is a condition, not a universal.
- **Only descent needs sorting into drift vs warranted.** Up-drift has names in every field — analysis paralysis, architecture astronautics, process talk as avoidance, reopening. The tasking asked for a two-kind taxonomy of one direction and none of the other.
- **The one exemplar of warranted descent** (a term hid two things). Lakatos's local/global counterexample, Soar's three impasse types, the spike's "open question asked above" and law's certified question give other warrants — feasibility, cost, tie, missing operator — that a definition-based test would miss.
- **Writing down as the remedy for the hole.** Robert's Rules uses a clock (tabled items expire); law uses forfeiture; CA relies on conditional relevance being heard; Soar keeps the parent on a stack with no authored note. Writing is one of at least four mechanisms, and it presupposes the state is articulable at the moment of descent — Schön and Dorst & Cross say it often is not until after.
- **"The answering process cannot monitor its own output."** True of one kind of generator; CA shows human talk self-marks its shifts constantly, and Schoenfeld shows an external prompt can be internalised. The premise fixes the solution before the families were consulted.
- **A per-message classifier with a fixed schema.** Most families detect shifts by *sequence* — a return marker, a noticeably absent answer, a move illegal in the current dialogue type — not by classifying single messages against a declared level. The schema-fixed observer is CRM's design, adopted for load on the monitor, a specific condition. Ashby's point stands: a fixed schema aliases shifts it has no name for.
- **Two axes posed as open, then presupposed.** Rasmussen and Cockburn answer "one axis" only because their artifacts are themselves a means-ends hierarchy; Grosz & Sidner and GoDiS answer "two, coupled by accommodation". The answer depends on what the documents are for, which the tasking did not say.
- **Vocabulary**: "steering", "hole", "robust", "resume", "come back down" — a navigation-and-stack metaphor. The stack is one contested model of discourse (Walker's cache model; CA's refusal of hidden variables), imported as the neutral description.
- **Role asymmetry**: the person rises or drops; the assistant answers. Who initiates shifts and who is steered is assumed, whereas facilitation, CRM and pair programming make the shift-caller a designated role independent of who is acting.

### Nothing to take

Refused: Bloom's taxonomy (levels of cognitive objective, no shifts, no documents); Endsley's situation-awareness levels (awareness quality, not purpose and means); Bateson's levels of learning / Dilts's "neurological levels" (Dilts's the closest surface match to the four levels and no evidence base — refused rather than lend it credibility); De Bono's Six Thinking Hats (modes, not levels); speech-act theory (force is orthogonal to why/how); Habermas's validity claims (dimensions of every utterance, not positions); Meadows's leverage points (an ordering of a system's intervention points, not of talk about it); Kahneman's System 1/2 and Boyd's OODA (mode and cycle models with no altitude). Not transferable even from accepted families: control theory's *inner loop faster* is about time-scales, not settling order; pair programming's measured benefits do not measure drift detection; Grosz & Sidner's stack has thin support for *human* recovery of popped contexts; Doyle & Straus's and Conklin's efficacy claims are practitioner reports — take the role design, not the efficacy; MI's premature-focus evidence is within-session therapy talk, transferred by analogy only.
