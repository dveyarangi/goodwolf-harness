## What it builds

One call, one session rule, two principles. The window, which reaches a session with every
message, already names the unit each other running session holds; one rule of the session
mechanism in the entry file stops any step before such a unit and brings the person two ways to
act. One of them, the takeover, is a call of the session script that moves the unit and leaves its
mark in the holder's row, which the holder's window then shows. The entry file names the agent's
bias, and that the one who built a thing does not judge it — a judge is a subagent. The session
doc, the ticket format and the hosts edge say what the method relies on and what is the host's.

## Challenges, decided 2026-10-25

1. **Taking over a held unit** — *the user*: the taking session writes the holder's row, taking the
   unit into its own and leaving in the holder's the mark of who took it. While the holder runs,
   the stop puts the takeover to the person; once said, it is acted on without a second ask.
2. **The judge** — *the user*: a judge is a subagent the one who built the work starts, never
   another session working on a running session's unit. The holder closes on the verdict. No later
   session judges what a subagent cannot see: a subagent reads the entry file and runs the wake
   itself, the text a new chat receives, and the host's delivery of it is the hosts edge's live
   check.
3. **The judge is a meta-rule, not a ticket's** — *the user*: the one who built a thing does not
   judge it; whenever that applies, a judge is needed, a ticket's criterion being one case. Its
   question is q-0037.0015's, and the rule waits on the pass mechanism of 01-0030.
4. **The stop is read off the window** — *the user*: the window reaches the session with every
   message, so one rule read beside it stops every step, free conversation included, where rules
   installed into four skills would stop only those steps.

## Taken at `/plan`

1. **The takeover.** `sessions.py --take <q-id> --session <tag>`, by the person's word: the unit
   leaves the holder's row, which keeps `taken <q-id> by <tag>` in its place, and enters this
   session's. Refused, writing nothing: a question no running session holds — `--hold` takes it —
   and one that is no unit. The same word lets go a unit this session held, which the call names
   and which is then nobody's *(the user, 2026-10-25, after a cold case)*. A write by the holder
   between the taker's read and its write is the later one; two rows holding one unit are the
   check's finding, as at a hold.
2. **A unit an ended row still holds.** `--hold` of a unit an ended row names writes that row's
   mark as a takeover does, without the person's word, the holder having ended; the chat,
   reopened, finds the mark in place of its unit rather than holding it beside its new holder.
3. **The mark in the row's life.** The holder's window shows `taken: <q-id> by <tag>`, data only.
   The holder's next hold, release or end clears it — a release that finds only the mark clears it
   too. Every rebuild keeps it; a rename rewrites its id; the check reports a mark naming no entry,
   as it reports a position. A row never carries both `holds` and `taken`. A mark naming a tag no
   row holds is no finding: the taker may have ended since.
4. **S9, the stop** — the session rules file, target the entry file, no `when`, so a tree with
   debug off keeps it: a unit the window shows another running session holding is not finished,
   closed, verified, committed or continued without the person's word; the reply says whose it is
   and where the two touch, and brings two ways with a recommendation — take it over, telling the
   holder where the host offers a message, or cut a sub-task through the ticket step and hold it
   here. The person's word to take it over is acted on without a second ask. A unit the window
   shows taken from this session is no longer its own: work on it stops, and the reply says so.
   Worded through `/skill-up`.
5. **S7 amended** — `/plan` and `/implement`: a refusal naming another running session stops the
   step, and a hold at the step's start lets go the unit the session held, named as nobody's
   *(decision 15, amended at implementation, v119)*. Where the window does not reach the agent, as
   in Cursor, that refusal is the only stop; the session doc and the hosts edge say so. A takeover
   lets go the taker's own unit the same way *(decision 14)*.
6. **S5 amended** — `/commit`: work on a unit this session took over is its own, the holder's
   uncommitted changes to it included; the rest of another session's changes stay out.
7. **S4 stays as it is**, under `debug=on`, for a collision that bears no work.
8. **The judge, a principle in the entry file's general rules**, bound as a straw dog to
   q-0037.0015: the one who built a thing does not judge it; a judge is a subagent the builder
   starts, given what is to be judged and where its evidence stands, never the builder's
   conclusion, since a subagent handed a conclusion returns it confirmed. Worded through
   `/skill-up`.
9. **TICKET-FORMAT** keeps only what a criterion names — its judge as a role, someone who did not
   build it — and loses *a later session included* and *accepted from any session; the holder of
   the unit closes on it and asks nobody*, instructions in a format file, which declares a format
   and holds none (register entry 58, 2026-10-21); the principle and the entry file's *Autonomy*
   say the rest. Through `/mechanism`, the ticket mechanism's.
10. **The bias, in the entry file's general rules**, authored there: the agent's default is to do
    as much as it can itself; work another session holds is not done without the person's word.
    Worded through `/skill-up`. The entry file moved the contract at v115, v117 and v119, each with
    its findings line.
11. **The session doc.** *Sessions see each other* drops *they cannot call one another*: a host may
    let one session message another, which the method uses to tell a holder its unit was taken
    and never relies on. *A unit held* gains the takeover's mark and its lifetime. *Moments* gains
    the takeover and the stop.
12. **The hosts edge.** The contract gains what a host may offer and the method uses where it is
    offered: one session's message to another, and a subagent. The Claude Code sidecar records the
    message delivered from one session to another on 2026-10-23; the other hosts' sidecars say not
    seen.

## Stages

1. **The takeover.** `--take` and the `taken` mark, tests first: the unit moves, the mark stands in
   the holder's row and its window, the holder's next hold, release or end clears it, a rename
   rewrites it, every rebuild keeps it; `--hold` from an ended row marks it; each refusal writes
   nothing.
2. **The rules.** S9 and S5 through `/skill-up`, installed; the injector's check green.
3. **The entry file.** The bias and the judge through `/skill-up`, the judge wrapped and bound to
   q-0037.0015; v115 to v119 and their findings lines. TICKET-FORMAT's sentences through `/mechanism`. Each
   shown before it lands.
4. **The records.** The session doc, the hosts edge and its sidecar; `edges.py --check` and
   `mechanisms.py --check` green.
5. **Live.** Two running sessions, one holding a ticket; the other, asked by the person to close
   it, stops with the two ways; told to take it over, it takes it, and the first one's window shows
   who took it and its reply says it stopped. Read by a subagent judge, from both rows and windows.
