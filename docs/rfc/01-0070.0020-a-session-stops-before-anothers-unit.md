## What it builds

Rules and one call. A session reads who holds what from its window, which 01-0070.0010 made carry
it; the steps that act on a unit — plan, implement, verify, close — stop before another running session's
unit and bring the person two ways to act. One of them, the takeover, is a call of the session
script that moves the unit and leaves its mark in the holder's row. A judge is a subagent the
holder starts. The entry file names the agent's bias. The session doc, the ticket format and the
hosts edge say what the method relies on and what is the host's.

## Challenges, decided 2026-10-25

1. **Taking over a held unit** — *the user, against the recommendation of no third write*: the
   session mechanism manages every session from any one of them, so the taking session writes the
   holder's row; it takes the unit into its own and leaves in the holder's the mark of who took
   it. While the holder runs, the stop puts the takeover to the person; once said, it is acted on
   without a second ask.
2. **The judge** — *the user*: a judge is a subagent the holder starts, never another session
   working on a running session's unit; a session judge is a ceremony that asks the person many
   acts to close one task. The holder closes on the subagent's verdict. A later session judges
   only what no subagent can see and closes on its own verdict. With it go the judge as a way to
   act, the question of who commits a judge's verdict, and the holder's release of a unit waiting
   on a judge, decided earlier the same day.

## Taken at `/plan`

1. **Where the stop is read.** The window's *other running sessions* lines name each one's held
   unit, so a session needs no call to know a unit is held. The rules read it there.
2. **The takeover.** `sessions.py --take <q-id> --session <tag>`, by the person's word: the unit
   leaves the holder's row, which keeps `taken <q-id> by <tag>` in its place, and enters this
   session's. The holder's window shows the mark; its next hold, release or end clears it — a
   release that finds only the mark clears it too, where today a release holding nothing writes
   nothing. Refused, writing nothing: a question no running session holds — `--hold` takes it —
   one that is no unit, and a second unit while this session holds one, as at a hold. A write by
   the holder between the taker's read and its write is the later one; two rows holding one unit
   are the check's finding, as at a hold. A message to the holder, where the host offers one,
   follows the mark.
3. **A unit an ended row still holds.** 01-0070.0010 lets `--hold` take a unit an ended row names,
   since an ended row holds nothing; but the ended row keeps its field, and when its chat reopens it
   holds the unit again beside its new holder. `--hold` of such a unit therefore writes the ended
   row's mark as a takeover does, without the person's word, the holder having ended: the reopened
   chat finds the mark in place of its unit.
4. **The mark in the row's life.** Every rebuild keeps the mark as it keeps `holds`; a rename
   rewrites the id in it; the check reports a mark naming no entry as it reports a position. A mark
   naming a tag no row holds is no finding: the taker may have ended since.
5. **S7 amended** — `/plan` and `/implement`: a refusal naming another running session stops the
   step with the two ways and a recommendation; nothing is done on the unit until the person names
   one; the person's word to take it over is the takeover, without a second ask.
6. **S8, new** — `/verify` and `/maintain`: a unit the window shows another running session
   holding is neither verified nor closed; the step stops with the two ways. A verdict written into
   another's criteria is finishing its work, which the stop is for.
7. **The two ways**, said in S7 and S8 alike: take the unit over, or cut a sub-task through the
   ticket step and hold it here. Said once in the rules file and installed into each target; no
   reply form is added.
8. **Not under `debug`.** Neither S7 nor S8 carries a `when`: a tree with debug off keeps the stop.
   S4's 🪢 row stays as it is, for a collision that bears no work.
9. **The judge.** The act is `/verify`'s, in its own body: a criterion naming a judge is judged by a
   subagent the session starts, given the criterion and where its evidence stands — never the
   session's own conclusion, since a subagent handed a conclusion returns it confirmed — and the
   session closes on its verdict; a criterion no subagent can see, such as what a host puts in a
   new chat, waits for a later session, which judges it and closes. TICKET-FORMAT's *Acceptance
   criteria* keeps only what a criterion names — its judge as a role, a subagent or a later
   session — since a format file declares a format and holds no instruction (register entry 58,
   2026-10-21). Its sentence *the holder of the unit closes on it and asks nobody*, an instruction
   landed there 2026-10-23, moves into `/verify` with the act. Both at the self-improvement dial,
   their text shown before they land. A host without subagents leaves every such criterion to a
   later session.
10. **The bias, in the entry file's general rules**, authored there: the agent's default is to do
    as much as it can itself; work another session holds is not done without the person's word.
    Worded through `/skill-up`. The entry file moving makes the contract v111, and the findings
    record gets its line.
11. **The session doc.** *Sessions see each other* drops *they cannot call one another*: the method
   reads one another through the rows, and a host may let one session message another, which the
   method uses to tell a holder its unit was taken and never relies on. *A unit held* gains the
   mark's lifetime. *Moments* gains the takeover and the stop at each step.
12. **The hosts edge.** The contract gains what a host may offer and the method uses where it is
    offered: one session's message to another, and a subagent. The Claude Code sidecar records the
    message delivered from one session to another on 2026-10-23; the other hosts' sidecars say not
    seen.

## Impact

- **Must change.** `sessions.py`: `--take`, the `taken` field in the row's form, read and shown
  in the window, cleared by hold, release and end, renamed and checked; `--hold` marking an ended
  row it takes from; the docstrings that still say a row is its own session's. `questions.py`: the
  window's line for a taken unit. Their tests. The session rules file — S7 amended, S8 new,
  `/verify` and `/maintain` in its anchor table — and its targets reinstalled. `/verify`'s body,
  the judge's act. TICKET-FORMAT's judge sentences. The entry file's general rules and its
  version; the findings record; the session doc; the hosts edge and its Claude Code sidecar.
- **Must verify.** The eight checks; the injector's check that local blocks stay last in
  `/verify` and `/maintain`; `/maintain`'s P1–P3, which close a pair — S8 sits beside them and narrows when they
  run, never what they do; the three live tickets whose criteria name a judge, read against the
  new sentence.
- **May simplify.** The judge-session handover the 2026-10-23 slip needed, never built.
- **Hidden risk.** A session whose window does not reach it — Cursor's, the message hook not
  reaching the agent — does not see a hold; there S7's refusal at plan and implement is the only
  stop and the close goes unguarded. The hosts edge says so. A subagent briefed with the holder's
  conclusion confirms it; the format's sentence forbids the conclusion in the brief, and nothing
  checks it.
- **Historical only.** Entries 64 and 65.
- **Probably unaffected.** The question store; the ticket's other rules.

## Hidden edges

The row's form gains a second optional field, `taken <q-id> by <tag>`, which stands where `holds`
would; a row never carries both. Starting the implementation puts the slice in work over L12's cap
again — ten were in work on 2026-10-25 — and the person's word of that day was for 01-0070.0010;
the start asks again.

## Stages

1. **The takeover.** `--take` and the `taken` mark, tests first: the unit moves, the mark stands in
   the holder's row and its window, the holder's next hold, release or end clears it, a rename
   rewrites it, every rebuild keeps it; `--hold` from an ended row marks it; each refusal writes
   nothing.
2. **The rules.** S7 amended, S8 written through `/skill-up`, installed; the injector's check
   green.
3. **The judge.** `/verify`'s act and TICKET-FORMAT's sentences, shown first; the tickets naming a
   judge read against them.
4. **The entry file.** The bias principle through `/skill-up`, v111, its findings line.
5. **The records.** The session doc, the hosts edge and its sidecar; `edges.py --check` and
   `mechanisms.py --check` green.
6. **Live.** Two running sessions, one holding a ticket; the other, asked by the person to close
   it, stops with the two ways; told to take it over, it takes it, and the first one's window shows
   who took it. Read by a subagent judge, from both rows and windows.
