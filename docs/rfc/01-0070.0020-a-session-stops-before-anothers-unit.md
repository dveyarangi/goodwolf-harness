## What it builds

Rules, not code. A session reads who holds what from its window, which 01-0070.0010 made carry
it; the steps that act on a unit — plan, implement, verify, close — read it there and stop before
another running session's unit, bringing the person three ways to act. The verdict a judge writes
is the handover; a message, where the host offers one, wakes the holder to act on it. The entry
file names the agent's bias. The session doc and the hosts edge say what the method relies on and
what is the host's.

## Challenges

1. **Taking over a held unit.** The first way — the holder releases it — needs the holder's row to
   lose the field, and only its own session writes it; a wake ending a silent row and a rename are
   the only writes to a row not its own. A takeover by the other session would be a third.
   **Recommended:** no third write. The holder releases itself: woken by a message where the host
   offers one, told by the person in its own chat where it does not — the person reaches every
   session the agent cannot — or its row ends by silence or its conclude, and the unit is free.
   *Waits on the person.*
2. **The judge and a record the holder is editing.** The judge writes its verdict into the unit's
   criteria; the holder may hold uncommitted edits in the same file, and a commit of the file
   carries them. **Recommended:** the judge writes and does not commit the unit's record; the
   holder commits it with its close, as happened on 2026-10-23. Where the host offers no message,
   the judge closes — the verdict written is the close — and commits it then. *Waits on the
   person.*
3. **A unit that waits only on a judge.** A slice landed and verified by its holder may keep one
   criterion a judge must read, as 01-0070.0010 does. Nothing says what its holder does then. Held,
   it makes every judge a judge of another's unit, and a holder holds one unit, so it cannot move
   on without letting it go; let go silently, as this session did on 2026-10-25 at the start of
   the next slice's plan, nothing says the unit now waits for a judge. **Recommended:** a holder
   whose unit, after its own `/verify`, waits only on a judge's criterion releases it, and the
   ticket's status says it waits on a judge; any session may then judge it and close on its own
   verdict, the unit being nobody's. The holder's release is S10 in `/verify`. *Decided
   2026-10-25, the user, who raised it: as recommended.*

## Taken at `/plan`, on the recommendations above

1. **Where the stop is read.** The window's *other running sessions* lines name each one's held
   unit, so a session needs no call to know a unit is held. The rules read it there.
2. **S7 amended** — `/plan` and `/implement`: a refusal naming another running session stops the
   step with the three ways and a recommendation; nothing is done on the unit until the person
   names one.
3. **S8, new** — `/maintain`: a unit the window shows another running session holding is not
   closed; the pass writes what it found into the unit's record and stops with the three ways.
4. **S9, new** — `/verify`: verifying a unit another running session holds, the session is its
   judge: it writes the verdict into the unit's criteria, closes and commits nothing of the unit's
   record, and wakes the holder with a message pointing at the record where the host offers one;
   where it does not, it closes on its own verdict.
5. **The three ways**, said in S7 and S8 alike: the holder releases the unit and this session
   takes it; this session judges it; a sub-task is cut through the ticket step and held here. Said
   once in the rules file and installed into each target; no reply form is added.
6. **Not under `debug`.** None of S7 to S9 carries a `when`: a tree with debug off keeps the stop.
   S4's 🪢 row stays as it is, for a collision that bears no work.
7. **The bias, in the entry file's general rules**, authored there: the agent's default is to do as
   much as it can itself; work another session holds is handed over, not done. Worded through
   `/skill-up` at implementation. The entry file moving makes the contract v111, and the
   findings record gets its line.
8. **The session doc.** *Sessions see each other* drops *they cannot call one another*: the method
   reads one another through the rows, and a host may let one session message another, which the
   method uses to wake a holder and never relies on. *Moments* gains the stop at each of the four
   steps and the judge's verdict.
9. **The hosts edge.** The contract gains what a host may offer and the method uses where it is
   offered: one session's message to another. The Claude Code sidecar records the message
   delivered from one session to another on 2026-10-23 and the holder acting on it; the other
   hosts' sidecars say not seen.

## Impact

- **Must change.** The session rules file — S7 amended, S8 and S9 new, `/maintain` and `/verify` in
  its anchor table — and its four targets reinstalled; the entry file's general rules and its
  version; the findings record; the session doc; the hosts edge and its Claude Code sidecar.
- **Must verify.** The eight checks; the injector's check that local blocks stay last in `/verify`
  and `/maintain`; `/maintain`'s P1–P3, which close a pair — S8 sits beside them and narrows when
  they run, never what they do.
- **May simplify.** Nothing.
- **Hidden risk.** A session whose window is stale — the host's message hook not reaching it, as in
  Cursor — does not see a hold; there S7's refusal at plan and implement is the only stop, and
  the close and the verdict go unguarded. The hosts edge says so.
- **Historical only.** Entries 64 and 65.
- **Probably unaffected.** The scripts; the ticket's format; the question store.

## Hidden edges

with the committed installer, and commits nothing of it.
