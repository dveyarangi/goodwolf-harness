# The kinds have names — implementation plan

**Authored:** 2026-10-08

Implements [01-0011.0110.0010 the-kinds-have-names](../tickets/01-0011.0110.0010-the-kinds-have-names.md),
first slice of [the spec](../spec/01-0011.0110-every-written-thing-has-a-source-of-truth.md). No
code: three prose files move, and the checks already in the verification set hold them.

## What governs it

- The spec's *Names and marks* and *The sort, and where it is declared*: the four kinds of
  record on two axes with expiry as the test; the five kinds of statement with a test each; roles
  reconciled with kinds; every principle and agentic classification named at its authored home.
- The entry file's *Core and instance*: the glossary is core and names no particular record of
  `docs/`; the kinds are stated with record *types* as examples, never a record.
- *Write for a capable model*: an entry states the term and its test; the examples that motivated
  it stay in the spec and the owning question's body.
- The mechanism shape's R7 and the installed blocks: Q1, Q5, P9, R7 and the local block in the
  entry file are not this slice's to touch; the names go on the authored bullets only.
- `/maintain`'s clock: the entry file is in every mechanism's rules-level surface, so this slice
  makes all five due at `rules`. That is the clock working, not a defect; the next maintenance
  pass re-checks and marks.

## Decisions this plan makes

1. **Roles stay and say their kind.** *Documentation*, *record*, *evidence*, *provenance* are
   not renamed across the fourteen files that use them. *Record* is the second axis — written
   under a declared format, its kind the format's — and says so; *doc* and *documentation* say
   they are of the kind *what must always hold*; *evidence* and *archived record* of *what
   happened*; *index* and *mark* of *what exists*; *ticket*, *RFC* and *spec* of *what it intends
   to become*; *provenance* is a part of a rule and of no kind. One sentence each. The spec's
   *either goes or stays as a declared axis* resolves to the latter for all four, since each role
   names something the kind does not — who writes it, under what, for whom.
2. **One entry per classification, its bins named inside it.** *Kind of record* holds the four
   bins, each bold so a reply cites it; *kind of statement* holds the five. A bin that already
   has an entry (*rule* for instruction, *principle*) keeps it; the three new ones — *invariant*,
   *definition*, *classification* — get entries. *Kind of turn* and *kind of moment* get entries
   that name the classification and point at its home, Q1 and the mechanism format, so a reply
   cites them by one name; *tier* already has one.
3. **Every bullet of the entry file's general section opens with its name**, instruction bullets
   included: the section is read as one list, and a reader tells principle from instruction by
   the glossary's test, not by which bullets carry names. The other sections' headings already
   serve as their names and are left. A bullet inside a straw dog keeps its wrapper; the name
   goes inside.
4. **The names.** Fixed here because the next slice cites them and a rename later costs every
   reply that did:
   - **one shape is not a class** — *Do not generalize from one shape…*
   - **the shape's context** — *Explore shape context…*
   - **the shape's structure** — *Explore shape structure…*
   - **method is mechanism work** — *Changing how the work is done…*
   - **an installed block is not yours** — *An `<installed>` block in a file…*
   - **a document informs** — *A document under `docs/` informs…*
   - **recency for evidence, longevity for principles** — unchanged words
   - **Occam** — *Clarity and simplicity first…*
   - **a capable reader** — *Write for a capable model…*
   - **tier is paid by every session** — *A skill's description is its tier-1 surface…*
5. **The mechanism docs and skills already lead their principles in bold**; this slice reads
   each and bolds a lead where one is missing, adding no sentence. The lead is the name.
6. **The whiteboard gets its glossary entry here**, not in `.0040`: it is a name the sort uses,
   and `.0040` builds on it.
7. **The rule that a principle must carry a name is `.0020`'s**, landing with the marks rule at
   tier 1; this slice does the naming and leaves no rule behind.

## Impact — 2026-10-08

**Impact.** `.agents/glossary.md` (new entries, six rewritten), `AGENTS.md` (ten bullet leads,
the version line), `docs/research/entry-contract-findings.md` (the v33 entry); a bold lead or
two in the mechanism docs. Every recipient receives the three on its next update. **Hidden
edges.** The rules-level fingerprint of all five mechanisms moves — expected, marked at the next
pass. The names become an interface the marks cite; a later rename is a rename across replies
and the marks rule, so the names are fixed above rather than left to implementation. A glossary
entry that cited a question or a ticket would be a leak; the entries cite only core files and
record types. **Leave alone.** The installed blocks and the local block in the entry file; the
skills' own bold leads where present; the project glossary under `docs/`. **Recommendation:**
proceed.

## Cheapest refutation

That a name at the home suffices to cite: a reply citing each general bullet by its name must
land a reader on one bullet. The names above were used in replies of 2026-10-07 before they
existed in the file, and each mapped to one bullet; the slice's second criterion is the same
test run against the file.

## Stages

1. **The glossary.** Add *Kind of record* with its four bins, the two axes and the expiry test;
   *Kind of statement* with its five bins and the test for each; *Invariant*, *Definition*,
   *Classification*, *Whiteboard*, *Kind of turn*, *Kind of moment*. Rewrite *Principle* to the
   user's definition — what to prefer when it is unclear what to do, written as prefer A over B,
   applied by judgement; keep its home sentence. Add one sentence of kind to *Doc*,
   *Documentation*, *Record*, *Evidence*, *Provenance*, *Index*, *Mark*, *Archived record*,
   *Ticket*, *RFC*, *Spec*.
2. **The entry file and its evidence.** The ten leads of decision 4; the version line to v33,
   2026-10-08; the findings record's v33 entry: names on the general bullets, the glossary's
   kinds, the occasion.
3. **The mechanism docs and skills.** Read each stated principle in the five docs and the skills
   that lead with one (`/mechanism`, `/discover`, `/align`); bold a lead where missing, change
   no words otherwise.
4. **The checks.** `inject_rules.py --check` clean (no installed block touched);
   `mechanisms.py --check` clean (no leak from the glossary); the suite green;
   `maintain.py --check` reporting five mechanisms due at `rules` — recorded as expected, not
   repaired here.

Every stage keeps the set green; stage 4 is the proof.
