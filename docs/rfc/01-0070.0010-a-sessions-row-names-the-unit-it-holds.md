# A session's row names the unit it holds — implementation plan

- **record of** what it intends to become

**Authored:** 2026-10-24

Implements [01-0070.0010 a-sessions-row-names-the-unit-it-holds](../tickets/01-0070.0010-a-sessions-row-names-the-unit-it-holds.md).

## What it builds

A session's row gains one field: the unit it holds. The session takes a unit when a ring step
begins work on it, lets it go when the person drops it, and holds nothing once its row ends or the
unit's question closes. The wake and the window show each running session's unit beside its
position; the session check reports one unit held by two running rows. No ticket file is read or
written by any of it.

- [The architecture](../architecture.md), the store's paragraph: a position is one session's; each

  drops it*.
- The parent question's decisions 1 and 8: the holder fact first; the mark in the row, never in

## Challenges

1. **What the row names.** The ticket says *the unit it holds, a ticket In progress*. Naming the
   ticket — its id or its path — makes the session mechanism read the ticket's format and its
   status, which decision 8 forbids: the format may be replaced. The store already joins a
   question to the record of the work that answers it, by its `owner` part, and the row already
   names questions. **Recommended:** the row names the question the unit answers; a unit is an
   open question with an owner; the holder of a ticket is read through the store's `owner`, never
   through the ticket. The wake and the window print the owner beside the id, so the person still
   reads the ticket. *Waits on the person.*
2. **When a session takes a unit.** The ticket says *when it begins the ring on it*. The ring's
   steps that work a unit are `/plan` and `/implement`; `/verify` may be a judge's and holds
   nothing; `/maintain`'s close ends the hold by closing the question. **Recommended:** a rule of
   the session mechanism, installed into `/plan` and `/implement`, takes the unit at the step's
   start; a refusal naming another running holder is what slice 2's stop will read, and until
   then the step stops and says whose the unit is. *Waits on the person.*
3. **The words.** The calls are *hold* and *release*, the decisions' own words — *the holder
   releases the unit* — and the row's field reads `holds <q-id>`. *Waits on the person.*

## Taken at `/plan`, on the recommendations above

1. **The field.** The row's line gains an optional last field, `holds <q-id>`. A row without it
   holds nothing, so every row written before reads as it did. The form is declared once, beside
   `/conclude`, and `ROW_FORM` carries it; the conclude's sentence on the form is that declaration.
2. **Reading.** A row holds its unit only while it runs and while the question is open. An ended
   row keeps the field, so a chat the host reopens and turns running again holds what it held —
   the same chat, the same work; a closed question holds nobody without any write.
3. **The calls.** `sessions.py --hold <q-id> --session <tag>` and `sessions.py --release --session
   <tag>`. `--hold` refuses, writing nothing, a question no entry holds; one that is closed; one
   with no `owner`, which is no unit; one another running session holds, naming that session; and
   a second unit while this session holds one, naming the release. Holding what it holds already
   writes nothing.
4. **The end.** `--end` writes the row ended without the field: a concluded session holds nothing
   and a reopened one takes its unit again at its next step.
5. **The rename.** `renamed()` rewrites the held id with the position and the recent ones.
6. **The read.** The wake's line for a session and the window's line for another running session
   end with `holding <q-id> (<owner>)` while they hold; the window's own header names this
   session's unit. Silent when nothing is held.
7. **The check.** A held id no entry holds is a diagnostic, as a position's is. A running row
   holding a closed question, or two running rows holding one question, is a finding, shown in
   each one's next window.
8. **The rule.** S7 in the session mechanism's rules file, targets `/plan` and `/implement`: at the
   step's start, hold the unit's question; a refusal naming another session stops the step, and
   the reply says whose the unit is. Worded through `/skill-up` at implementation. `/plan` has
   the heading *Installed from other mechanisms*; `/implement` has none, and its block anchors on
   its opening line, *Implement the work described by the relevant architecture decisions*, as
   the local block anchors on a line of its own there.
9. **The docs.** The session doc's *The row* gains the field and its lifetime; *Moments* gains *a
   session takes a unit, and lets it go*; *What it produces* says the wake and the window carry
   the holders. Q1's ring clause and L12 are untouched: whether the cap counts from the row is
   the pathfinder's, as the ticket says.

## Impact

- **Must change.** `sessions.py`: the form, the parser and writer, `--hold`, `--release`, `--end`,
  `renamed()`, `state()`, the check. `questions.py`: the window's lines for this and other
  sessions. The conclude skill's sentence on the row's form. The session doc. The session rules
  file, one rule, and its two targets reinstalled. `test_sessions.py`, and `test_questions.py`
  where the window's lines are asserted.
- **Must verify.** Every committed row reads unchanged under the new form; the wake's ending of a
  silent row and the seven-day removal carry the field untouched; a rename of a held question
  renames the field. The entry file does not change, so its contract's version stays.
- **May simplify.** q-0037.0019.0001's ring mark: a running row with a held unit is a session in
  the ring. Not taken here.
- **Hidden risk.** A session that holds and never releases keeps its unit for as long as its row
  runs; the row's three-hour silence bounds it. Two sessions racing to hold one question both read
  it free and both write: each writes its own file, so both rows hold it, and the check's finding
  is what shows it — no lock, as the architecture says.
- **Historical only.** Rows of ended sessions in other trees: they read as holding nothing.
- **Probably unaffected.** The question store's records; the ticket's format and its check; the
  hook wiring.

## Hidden edges

The hold is written by the session that holds, into its own row, so the one-writer rule stands.
The script rebuilds a row in ten places — the wake's ending, a sign of life, an arrival, a move, a
rename, the end — each by naming every field; a new field dropped by one of them would release a
unit unseen. Every rebuild carries the field except the end, and stage 1 tests each.
The finding of two holders surfaces in both windows, which is where slice 2's stop will read it.
A recipient's tree updated to this version has no `owner` parts on questions its own method does
not assign; there `--hold` refuses every question as no unit, and the rule tells the step to go on
holding nothing — the method's unit seam, not a failure.

## Leave alone

The stop on another's unit and the handover: 01-0070.0020. The cap's counting and Q1's ring
clause: the pathfinder's question. The ticket's format and its status words.

## Stages

Each stage keeps the suite green; tests first, red observed, per `/tdd`.

1. **The field.** Parse and write `holds`; rows without it read as before; `renamed()` and `--end`.
   Tests: a round trip with and without the field; every committed row read unchanged; the
   wake's ending, a sign of life, an arrival and a move each keep it; a rename carries it;
   `--end` drops it.
2. **The calls.** `--hold` and `--release` with their five refusals. Tests: each refusal writes
   nothing and names its reason; a second hold of the same id writes nothing.
3. **The read and the check.** The wake's and the window's lines; the diagnostic and the two
   findings. Tests: the lines while held and silent when not; a closed question and a doubly held
   one each a finding; an unknown id a diagnostic.
4. **The rule and the docs.** S7, the reinstall, the conclude's sentence, the session doc. The
   eight checks green.
5. **Live.** This session holds the slice's question at its `/implement`; a second session's wake
   and window show it; this session's conclude clears it. Read by a judge who did not build it.
