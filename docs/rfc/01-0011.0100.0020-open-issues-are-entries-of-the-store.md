# The tickets' open issues are entries of the store — implementation plan

**Authored:** 2026-10-03

Implements [01-0011.0100.0020 open-issues-are-entries-of-the-store](../tickets/01-0011.0100.0020-open-issues-are-entries-of-the-store.md).

## What governs it

- The ticket's decisions 1 to 5: an open issue is an unresolved question the ticket owns; an
  entry holds its own argument; a ticket names its question in `Answers`; the fold is drafted here
  and reviewed by family.
- Parent decisions 24 (the entry, amended by decision 3), 34 (the store's directories, amended
  2026-10-03: a closure that finishes a subtree moves it), 43 (a closed entry points to its
  answer), 50 (a closure follows its decision home), 52 (a request brings its question) and 30
  (the store is core's; tickets are a practice a project may swap).
- **The body is written by hand, the parts by the script** *(the user, 2026-10-03, at this plan)*.
  The argument is prose, written with the edit tools so its change is read as it happens; the
  script keeps the body byte for byte on every write. Refused: a call that writes the body, a new
  verb carrying multi-line prose through quotes or a file.

## The shapes

### An entry's body

The parts are the bullet list after the title; they end at the first blank line after a part.
Everything after that blank line is the body, kept verbatim and never parsed — a `- **x**` line in
an argument is not a part. A line that is neither a part nor a continuation, before the parts
end, is still reported as today. `questions.py` writes the parts and keeps the body: `Entry`
carries it, every event's rewrite emits it unchanged, and `open` writes none. The window, the
wake's read and `--tree` read the parts alone, so the body costs them nothing (parent decisions
26 and 27). The interference recheck that guards a write today guards the body with it: a hand
edit landing between a call's read and its write refuses the call, which is run again.

`owner` keeps its form and changes its meaning: the record of the work that answers the question.
The skill's table, `assign`'s help and its docstring say so.

### `close` moves the subtree it finishes

After a `close` is written, the call reads the store again and finds the largest wholly closed
subtree holding the closed entry that nothing open depends on — the same readiness the maintainer
already reports as *ready for done/*. If there is one, every entry of it moves to
`docs/questions/done/` in one invocation of the mover's own function, which repairs every link to
the moved files and re-aims theirs. The call prints what moved. A `close` that finishes nothing
moves nothing.

Failure: the closure is the decision and stands. If the mover refuses, the call reports the
refusal and exits non-zero after the close is written; `/maintain`'s sweep (Q4) moves the subtree
later. A closure inside an open subtree moves nothing until the last open entry of it closes; a
deferred or suspect entry counts as open, as it does for the maintainer. Ids never change, so a
session's position or recent list naming a moved entry still resolves.

The questions skill's interim straw dog — move by hand when `--check` reports a subtree ready —
is removed in the stage that builds this.

### `Answers` in the ticket header

`- **Answers:** [q-N](../questions/q-N-<slug>.md)` — the question the ticket's goal answers. Its
place in the shelf's order is just before `Outcome`: the question, then what answering it lands.
It is required on every live ticket. `tickets.py --check` reports a ticket whose `Answers` is
missing; one whose link is not exactly one entry of the store, live or in `done/`; and one whose
entry's `owner` does not resolve to the ticket itself. The script reads the entry through the
store's public reader, `read_store`; the dependency runs from the ticket practice to core's store,
never back (parent decision 30).

A ticket's open questions are the subtree under its `Answers` entry, drawn on request by
`questions.py --tree q-N`, which exists. Nothing renders them into the ticket.

### `Open issues` retired

The section leaves `TICKET-FORMAT` and the script's six named sections. A live ticket at either
stage holding a section titled `Open issues` is reported, as the guard that keeps the fold done.

### The texts

- **The questions skill**: *the only writer* becomes the only writer of the parts and of the
  sessions file, the body written by hand; the record format shows a body; `owner` reads as the
  record of the work that answers; *pruned* and *moot*'s longer reason goes to the entry's body
  rather than the owner's record.
- **The questions mechanism doc**: its row *holding the tickets' open issues as entries*, a straw
  dog bound to this slice, is replaced by the row for the body and the move at closure.
- **`TICKET-FORMAT`**: `Answers` in the header and in *The record*'s order; the `Open issues`
  bullet removed; *incepted … hosts chunks: … open questions* reads that an incepted ticket's open
  questions are entries under its `Answers`.
- **P7**, in `ticket.rules.md`, reinstalled into `/align` with overwrite: *Land a resolved decision
  in its durable home with its provenance and rewrite the ticket in place to what is now true;
  close the question it answers against that home, and repoint every entry closed against the
  decision when it lands there.*
- **`/ticket`**: *hold the decision tree in the ticket* becomes: the decision tree's open questions
  are entries under the ticket's `Answers`, each holding its own evidence and alternatives; a ticket
  is minted with its `Answers`, opening that question when the store holds none. Its two straw dogs
  bound to this slice are rewritten as standing text.
- **`/maintain`**: lines 30 and 76 speak of a ticket's *open issues*; they read the store instead.

## Stages

Each stage keeps the verification set green.

1. **The body.** Tests first in `test_questions.py`: a body survives `close`, `lean`, `assign`,
   `move`, `suspect` and a strike byte for byte; a bullet-shaped line in the body is not read as a
   part; a stray line among the parts is still reported; a hand edit between read and write
   refuses the call. Then the parser and the writer. The skill's format text and `assign`'s wording
   land here.
2. **The move at closure.** Tests: a `close` finishing a subtree leaves every entry of it in
   `done/` and a ticket's link to one of them resolving; a `close` under a still-open parent moves
   nothing; the last child's `close` moves the parent with it; a deferred child keeps the subtree
   live; a refused move leaves the closure written and says why. Then the call. The skill's straw
   dog is removed.
3. **The fold**, once [`.0018`](../tickets/01-0011.0100.0018-a-question-id-says-where-it-sits.md)
   has landed, so its entries are born with nested ids and the existing ones already carry theirs.
   The three tables below, each accepted by the user. For every ticket without an
   entry, `open` its question under its parent ticket's, `assign` it, and write `Answers` in its
   header; for every bullet, its destination — an entry opened, assigned and given its body by
   hand, a line in `What to build`, an existing entry's body, or removal; then the `Open issues`
   sections go. The live check stays green: `Answers` is a one-off field until stage 4 makes it
   required.
4. **The check.** Tests in `test_tickets.py`, with the fixture helper writing an `Answers` line and
   a store entry: a missing `Answers`; a link that resolves to no entry; a link to a file that is
   not an entry; an entry owned by another record; `Open issues` in an incepted and in a shaped
   ticket. Then `REQUIRED`, `ORDERED` and the named sections, and the live check passes over the
   folded tree.
5. **The texts**: `TICKET-FORMAT`, P7 reinstalled, `/ticket`, `/maintain`, the questions doc.

## The reconciliation

Run once, at the end of stage 3, and not shipped: a script in the session's scratchpad reads every
`Open issues` bullet from the commit before the fold and checks that its text, whitespace
normalised, sits at the destination its row names — an entry's body, a `What to build` line, or
this RFC's removal list. It is run twice: as is, passing; and with one argument cut from its
destination, failing on that bullet. Both results are recorded on the ticket's box. The guard that
stays is stage 4's: no live ticket holds the section.

## The fold

34 live tickets, 40 bullets. Each family is reviewed and corrected in place by the user before
stage 3 writes it. **Kinds** (decision 1): *question* — becomes an entry; *pointer* — a question
another record owns, entered under that owner; *finding* — a fact phrased as the question it
argues; *work* — undone work, to `What to build`; *constraint* — to `What to build` or the
criteria; *evidence* — to the body of the ticket's `Answers` entry; *removed* — a placeholder or a
resolution, with its reason. *New* in a cell means a question opened by the fold.

## Impact

### Impact

- **Must change:** `questions.py` (parse, `Entry`, `_with`, `_entry_text`, `close`), `tickets.py`
  (`REQUIRED`, `ORDERED`, `NAMED_SECTIONS`, the `Answers` check), `test_questions.py`,
  `test_tickets.py` and its `ticket()` fixture helper, which every test builds from; the questions
  skill and doc; `TICKET-FORMAT`, `ticket.rules.md` and its installed copy in `/align`; `/ticket`;
  `/maintain`'s two mentions; 34 live tickets and some 60 new entries.
- **Must verify:** `move_doc.py` called from a `close` keeps its own contract — one invocation,
  links repaired, nothing staged; the window and the wake read unchanged with bodies present;
  `straw_dogs.py`, `inject_rules.py --check` and `mechanisms.py --check` after the texts.
- **May simplify:** `/maintain`'s Q4 sweep stays, now catching what a `close` left behind rather
  than every finished subtree.

### Hidden edges

- **Recipients.** P5 runs `tickets.py --check` in every tree holding core. Once `Answers` is
  required, a recipient's existing tickets fail until each names an entry. frost_map's update is
  held for `.0195`; its release note owes this migration.
- **Every window grows.** Some 60 new open entries and about 15 roots: the root lines of the window
  and the wake grow by the new roots. Parent decision 26 measured the bound per root; this fold is
  its first real test.
- **A pointer that sits under another question.** q-0002 is owned by 01-0010 and part of q-0001;
  `--tree` from a ticket's `Answers` shows only what sits under it. An owned entry elsewhere is
  allowed and stays invisible from that render.
- **`.0030`'s straw dogs** will be entries too; they inherit the body shape this slice lands.
- **`.0040`'s queue index** derives the queue from ticket headers; `Answers` is one more field it
  may show.
- **Twins.** The new questions share words with old ones — .0168's with q-0017 — and the twin
  finding will fire; a person merges or dismisses.

### Leave alone

The tickets' *Decisions landed* sections — q-0023. `docs/pacer.md`'s own *Open issues*, a working
document and not a ticket. The window's format and the debug table. Archived tickets, which carry
no `Answers`.

### Recommendation

Proceed as staged. Narrowing considered: the move at closure could be its own slice, but it
touches the same write path as the body and is a few lines on the maintainer's existing readiness.
The `Answers` check introduces no general abstraction: one field, one link, one owner comparison.

## Limits and follow-ups

- The arguments of closed questions stay in the tickets' *Decisions landed* sections — q-0023.
- `docs/pacer.md` holds an *Open issues* section of its own; it is not a ticket and the guard does
  not read it. It folds with 01-0020's align.
- A recipient's migration to `Answers` is a release-note line for `.0185`/`.0195`.
