# An entry's name carries its whole question — implementation plan

**Authored:** 2026-10-02

Implements [01-0011.0100.0017 an-entry-name-carries-its-whole-question](../../tickets/done/01-0011.0100.0017-an-entry-name-carries-its-whole-question.md).

## The decision it follows

[Decision 55](../../tickets/01-0011.0100-the-open-questions-are-kept-by-a-mechanism.md) *(the user,
2026-10-02)*: a question is phrased short, and the skill says so, since its slug is made from its
words. The slug holds all of the words and is cut at a word boundary only past 160 characters. The
slug is for a person reading a listing; relevance, twins and the title search read the question
line inside the file. The entries already written are renamed with the mover.

No architecture changes. An entry's name is the store's record format, which
[the questions skill](../../../.agents/skills/questions/SKILL.md#records) declares; it fails the
load-bearing counter-test, since it can be changed locally.

## What the code does today

- `questions.py`'s `_slug` takes the question's leading words up to `SLUG_LENGTH = 40` characters.
  The writer names a new entry with it on `opens`.
- `--check`'s `_name_problems` accepts any slug made of the question's leading words in order, so
  `which-package-manager` passes for *Which package manager do we use?*. The test
  `test_may_shorten_the_question_to_its_leading_words` pins that allowance.
- The sixteen live entries carry 40-character slugs. Four records link them by filename:
  [rule-failures.md](../../rule-failures.md),
  [01-0017.0010 terms-defined-before-they-land](../../tickets/01-0017.0010-terms-defined-before-they-land.md),
  [01-0017.0020 practice-swaps-in-one-edit](../../tickets/01-0017.0020-practice-swaps-in-one-edit.md)
  and [01-0019 harness-amends-itself-by-explicit-meta-rules](../../tickets/01-0019-harness-amends-itself-by-explicit-meta-rules.md).

## Settled while planning

**The check holds the name to the whole question** *(settled and reported under decision 49)*.
Decision 55 says the slug holds every word. A check that still accepted a shortened slug would
let a hand-named or hand-renamed entry break the rule unnoticed, and would leave the second
acceptance criterion verified only once. So `_name_problems` compares the slug with
`_slug(question)` exactly, and its diagnostic names the expected filename. The name now follows the
question's wording: an entry whose question is reworded by hand fails the check until the mover
renames it. Reversible: it is one comparison.

## Stages

Each stage keeps the suite green. Between stages 1 and 2, `questions.py --check` on this tree
reports the sixteen live entries. That is the intended finding, and stage 2 clears it, so land the
two stages in one commit.

**1. The writer and the check, test-first.**

- Red:
  - A writer test: `opens` with a question whose words make a slug of about 150 characters
    creates an entry named with every word.
  - A writer test: a question past 160 characters creates an entry whose slug is cut at a word
    boundary at or below 160 characters, and whose words are the question's leading words. The
    test proves the cut happens at a boundary, not mid-word.
  - A check test: a slug that stops short of the whole question is reported, and the diagnostic
    names the full expected filename. This test replaces
    `test_may_shorten_the_question_to_its_leading_words`, whose allowance decision 55 removes.
- Green:
  - `SLUG_LENGTH = 160`.
  - `_name_problems` compares the slug with `_slug(read.question)`.
  - Rewrite the docstrings of `_slug` and `_name_problems` to say what each now does, dropping the
    shortened-slug example and the "forty characters" wording.
- Fixtures: `Store` and the other `place` calls that use shortened stems (`q-0001-which-store`,
  `q-0003-which-store-holds` and their kind) take their questions' full slugs. The id-mismatch
  test and the wrong-words test stay as they are.

**2. The live entries renamed.** One `move_doc.py` invocation over the sixteen pairs, each
destination `docs/questions/<id>-<_slug(question)>.md`. Read the pairs from stage 1's
`--check` diagnostics, which name each expected file; nothing new computes them. A
`--dry-run` taken while planning moved all sixteen and repaired citations in exactly the four
records the split's impact named. The longest new path is 121 characters under
`docs/questions/` (`q-0011`).

**3. The words.**

- [The questions skill](../../../.agents/skills/questions/SKILL.md#records): the entry line reads that
  the slug is every word of the question, cut at a word boundary past 160 characters. A sentence
  says to phrase a question short, since its name is made from its words.
- [The questions evidence](../../mechanisms/questions.evidence.md): its *Decided while building* line
  "up to forty characters" gains that decision 55 raised it to the whole question, cut past 160.

## Follow-ups and limits

- **The instruction to phrase a question short sits in the skill, but the agent usually opens a
  question from Q1's example line at tier 1, without reading the skill.** The decision places it
  in the skill, and a long question costs only a long name, so this slice does not move it. Whether
  it reaches the occasion is the reachability concern of
  [01-0018 reachability-coherent](../../tickets/01-0018-reachability-coherent.md). A sustained run of overlong names
  in the store is the evidence that would move it.
