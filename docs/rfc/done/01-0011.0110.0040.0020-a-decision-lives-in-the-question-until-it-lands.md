## What it changes

The [spec](../../spec/01-0011.0110-every-written-thing-has-a-source-of-truth.md) moves a task's
draft out of the ticket: everything bearing on the task is written in the body of the question it
stands on — the method glossary's *Whiteboard* — and the ticket, minted from it once the form is
seen, holds what is actual for the work and links the rest. This slice gives the body a decisions
section that `close` respects, narrows the ticket format, retargets `/align`'s three ticket rules
to the body, and states the ticket's contract.

## Decisions taken at this plan

*(the user, 2026-10-11)*

1. **A ticket carries `Minted: <date>`, required, and its shape is read from it.** A ticket minted
   before the day this slice lands is held to the required sections and the `/verify` box alone;
   one minted on or after it, to the narrowed format. Every live ticket gets the field once, dated
   by its file's first commit — 36 at the plan, the two minted on 2026-10-11 already in the narrowed
   shape, so the day the line falls on changes nothing for them. Omission is a diagnostic, so no new ticket escapes the narrowed
   check — the strictness `.0010`'s decision 1 chose for the same reason. A recipient's `tickets`
   gate reports its live tickets until they carry the field.
2. **An align's decisions are written in the question's file, never in the ticket**: a line each
   in the body's `## Decisions` list. A decision moved into its durable home gets `→ <link>` on its
   line. A question decided on the way closes with a link to that list. *Narrowed by 3 and 4: the
   list holds only what has nowhere to land, and the question, not the ticket, guards it.*
3. **A decision lands at once** — [q-0024.0009.0003.0002.0001](../../questions/done/q-0024.0009.0003.0002.0001-when-does-a-closing-question-land-its-decision-itself-and-when-does-it-hand-it-to-its-parent.md).
   Where the answer of the nearest open ancestor can change its form or home, it lands as a straw
   dog bound to that ancestor, which the ancestor's close brings due — the moment the ancestor
   forms from its children; otherwise it lands final. A decision with nowhere to land yet — its
   home is work not done — is a line in the `## Decisions` list of the lowest question holding
   everyone who needs it. The question that decided closes against where its decision landed.
   `suspect` is untouched: it is a parent moved for reasons outside its children. In the archive
   37 of 44 decided children closed under an open parent, so most landings are straw dogs; 102 of
   the tree's 150 are one line.
4. **The question guards its list.** `close` refuses while a line of the closing question's
   `## Decisions` has no `→`, naming the line: land it, or move it to the nearest open ancestor
   still holding everyone who needs it — moved, never copied. A root closes only when every line
   has landed. The closed question's answer cites the list its line moved to, and `due` reads an
   answer citing a question entry as not landed. The ticket guards nothing here.
5. **The landing rule is the questions mechanism's.** A new Q6, installed in `/align`, lands a
   decision as 3 and 4 say; the ticket keeps P7 as one clause — rewrite the ticket in place to what
   the landed decisions make true. P10 opens an align on its work, ticket or not; P11 sweeps the
   question's body, and the ticket where there is one. `/align`'s anchor becomes `### Land
   resolutions as they are made`, in both rules files that install there. The texts are those shown
   to the user on 2026-10-11, shown again before they are written.
6. **The contract is a section of the ticket format**, `## The contract`, before *The record*: a
   ticket states its outcome, the question it answers, what to build, its criteria with
   verification among them, its parent and its status, and is done when every criterion holds.
   The file is its default form; a board stands in where each item states all of it; no adapter,
   the scripts read the file form alone. `Minted`, `Type`, `Plan`, numbering and folders are the
   file form's, not the contract's. *The question it answers* is written as a straw dog bound to
   [q-0024.0009.0003.0002.0002](../../questions/q-0024.0009.0003.0002.0002-must-a-projects-own-board-link-each-of-its-items-to-the-question-it-answers-and-how.md),
   to be thought through *(the user)*.

## What need not exist

Weighed at the align and dropped: a guard on the ticket's close and on the archive (the question
guards its own list, decision 4); a rendered view of the descendants' decisions in the window and
`--tree` (nobody who reads a question's file would see it; decision 3 lands the decision where it
is read); a check on whether a landing should have been a straw dog (a judgement, the agent's); the
stage, *incepted* or *shaped* (decision 1 reads the shape from `Minted`); `assign`-before-closing
in the questions skill (`due` tells a list from a home itself).

## Shapes

**The list** is the body's `## Decisions` section, exact title, up to the next `## ` heading or the
end; lines inside a fence are not read. A decision is a top-level item — `- ` or `N. ` at column
0 — with its indented continuation; prose between items is no decision, as the two bodies that
already hold the section write it. A decision has landed when its item holds `→` followed by a
link that resolves to a file outside the question store, `docs/tickets/` and `docs/rfc/` —
a question entry is where a line waits, a ticket or an RFC is no home *(decision 2)*. The script
reads the list and never writes it: the body stays the agent's *(the user, 2026-10-03)*.

**`close`** reads the closing entry's list before it writes, of every kind, and refuses naming the
first unlanded item by its opening words, with the remedy: land it, or move it to the nearest open
ancestor holding everyone who needs it — for a `merged` or `superseded` close, to the question it
points to, which takes over what the closing one asked. **`--check`** reports a live closed entry whose list holds
an unlanded item, so a hand edit cannot do what `close` refuses; an archived entry is history and
is not read for it.

**A home is one predicate, read twice.** A link is a home when it resolves to a file outside the
question store, `docs/tickets/` and `docs/rfc/`, live or archived. The list reads it after `→`;
**`due`** reads a decided answer as landed only when every link it holds is one. That replaces
today's owner clause rather than sitting beside it: a closure under the old P7 cites its owner
ticket, which is no home, so it reads as before; a ticket that is not the owner, cited by an
answer, now reads as not landed too, which is what decisions 2 to 4 make of a ticket. The test
*a decision with no owner is due wherever it points* becomes *a decision citing a ticket is not
due, owner or not*.

The body stays hand-written and kept as found, as the architecture's *Question store* says; that
the script reads one part of it is the questions mechanism's, where that section sends
everything else, so the architecture gains nothing.

**The ticket** gains `Minted: YYYY-MM-DD` after `Type`, required, a real date. A constant in
`tickets.py`, the day this slice lands, divides the shapes: from that day the sections are the five
named ones and nothing else, and the criteria hold checkbox lines only; before it, the required
sections, the retired `Open issues` and the `/verify` box, as for every ticket. `shaped` and the
record's `stage` go; the record reports `minted`. The older shape is temporary: the constant and
its branch carry `# TODO q-0024.0009.0003.0002.0003`, deferred until no live ticket was minted
before the day, so the branch is found and removed then rather than leaned on.

## Stages

Each stage leaves the suite and the checks green; tests are written first, red, then made green.

1. **The list and `close`** (`questions.py`, `test_questions.py`). Tests: a close refused on an
   unlanded `- ` item and on an unlanded `N. ` item, naming it; a close passing when every item
   carries a resolving `→` outside the store, tickets and RFCs, or when the list is empty or
   absent; `→` to a question entry, a ticket, an RFC or a dead link is unlanded; a `## Decisions`
   heading inside a fence is not read; prose between items is not an item; `--check` reports a live
   closed entry holding an unlanded item and not an archived one; `reword` and `move` keep the list
   as written.
2. **`due`** (`questions.py`, `test_questions.py`). Tests: a decided answer citing a question's list
   is not due, live or archived; one citing a ticket or an RFC is not, owner or not; repointed to a
   home, it is; one citing a home and a ticket beside it is not.
3. **`Minted`** (`tickets.py`, `test_tickets.py`, the 34 live tickets). The fixture tree is rebuilt
   around `Minted`; tests: a ticket without `Minted`, or with a malformed date, is reported; one
   minted on or after the day holding a narrative section, or prose among its criteria, is
   reported; one minted before is not, and is still reported for no `/verify` box or a missing
   required section; `Minted` out of order is reported. Every live ticket gets the field in the same
   stage, by the edit tools, dated by `git log --follow --diff-filter=A` of each file — this
   ticket by its own. The check is green at the stage's end.
4. **The formats and docs** — mechanism work, under `/mechanism`, each text shown before it is
   written: `TICKET-FORMAT.md` (*The contract*, with *the question it answers* a straw dog bound to
   q-0024.0009.0003.0002.0002; *The record*, the header with `Minted`, the sections, the stage
   gone); `ticket.md` (*How it works*, the stage paragraph, the moment *reading how a shaped ticket
   reached its decisions*, the authority rows); `/questions` (*A raised question*, *Closing*,
   *Records* — the list and `→`); `questions.md` (the list among the entry's parts, the rules row);
   the method glossary (*Ticket*, *Ticket stage* gone, *Whiteboard*); `/ticket` (*Decision-bearing
   ticket lifecycle*, steps 3.1 and 5); `/discover`'s one line; `docs/process.md`.
5. **The rules** — `questions.rules.md` gains Q6 targeting `/align`; `ticket.rules.md` narrows P7,
   rewrites P10 and P11 and moves the anchor; `mechanism-shape.rules.md` moves R5's anchor;
   `/align`'s heading renamed; each mechanism re-installed with overwrite, `inject_rules.py

6. **The entry file** — the straw dog's sentence on where a decision forms, the contract's next

7. **The edge** — the recipient-facing line in
   [q-0018.0020.0002.0001](../../questions/q-0018.0020.0002.0001-how-does-an-edge-change-get-its-line-in-the-edge-record-when-it-is-made.md)'s
