## Where the plan reads an accepted line one way

Nine readings the person sees before the plan is accepted; the last two are theirs to decide,
since the whole lists them as open.

1. **"Its parts and what it produces are the role's zone."** A role's zone is its own
   mechanism's files — its directory, its skill's folder — and what that mechanism names under
   *What it produces, and who reads it*, in the bullet form the section already has: an output
   whose whole name is one code span is a mechanism of the tree, by its slug, or a path or path
   pattern in it. No field and no table is added to the format.
2. **"The name stands for the paths that mechanism declares."** A mechanism's name stands for
   its directory, the folder of each skill that claims it, and its parts; never for what that
   mechanism itself names as produced, and never for its records.
3. **Records of work are in no zone.** The whole says so of questions. The plan reads it of
   every record a unit of work writes — questions, tickets, plans, session records, rows, cases:
   who writes one is said by who holds the unit, and the default does not make them the
   keeper's. A record one role keeps — the product document, the journal — is named in that
   role's zone as a path.
4. **"Every role has a mechanism of its own."** Five local mechanisms, each a doc and one skill
   of the same name: `role-keeper`, `role-method`, `role-store`, `role-product`,
   `role-practice`. The prefix is needed: `product` and `practice` are already mechanisms those
   roles hold. Their state is `installed`, not `always on`: the whole has a role closed or two
   merged, and an always-on mechanism cannot be retired. A role is retired by the person's
   decision — its directory and skill removed, its row leaving the table.
5. **"A table in its local rules file, installed into the entry file."** The table,
   `| role | session |`, stands in the body of local rule L13, after the rule's opening words: a
   role by its mechanism's slug, a session by its name. The installer, the one reader of that
   file, finds it by its header row and holds it to its form. A shift is then what any change to
   a local rule is — the rule amended, installed with overwrite, the entry contract's version
   moved — and the version is how a running session learns the cut changed. The alternative, a
   table in the file's preamble like the settings' values, keeps a rule's body unread by any
   script; but the installer knows no entry file to render it into, and would have to be taught
   one.
6. **"What the table does not name is held by the session that holds the harness."** The local
   rule names that session outright. Nothing infers it, and nothing checks it.
7. **The invariant binds every mechanism.** The format cannot tell a role's mechanism from
   another, so the rule is general: what two mechanisms name, neither naming the other, is
   named by one — compared as files, so a path inside a mechanism another names is caught — or
   each of the two marks its bullet.
8. **To decide — how a session that holds several roles is named.** The plan takes one name, the
   one in its title, and a row of the table for each role it holds. The whole lists this as open
   ([q-dyc63i](../questions/q-dyc63i-which-standing-roles-does-this-tree-have-where-every-session-develops-the-harness-itself.md)).
   What a session's row then bears is the store's session's rule, which says "the role's name"
   today where the whole says "the name the table gives it"; that session is asked.

## The shape

**The format, first.** *Keeps:* *What carries a role*; *The cut*. The format shelf gains:

- under the doc's *What it produces*: an output whose whole name is one code span names a
  mechanism of the tree or a path or pattern in it, and a name that resolves to neither, a
  pattern that matches nothing included, is a diagnostic; its kind is *what must always hold*
  for a mechanism or an instruction's folder, and a named record's own kind. An output named in

- the invariant of reading 7, with its mark: each of the two bullets wrapped as a straw dog,

  sentence that the installer never reads a body gains this one exception.

The shape's doc gains the invariant in one sentence with its pointer to the shelf, and the rows

**The roles' mechanisms.** *Keeps:* *What carries a role*; *Two trees*. Each is
`.agents/mechanisms/role-<name>/role-<name>.md` and `.agents/skills/role-<name>/SKILL.md`,
`owner local`, `installed`, with no rules file and no script. The accepted whole is the align
that says what such a mechanism is; each role's own text is shown as it is written.

- The doc: what the role answers for, in its title line; its moments — taking the role, a change
  asked by another role's session, what the nightly pass leaves in its zone; its one part, the
  skill; what it holds, as named outputs, and who reads each — the seams with the other zones;
  what retires it; the grading. `/maintain` is handed nothing beyond the shape's own check: the
  mechanism has no records and no script.
- The skill is the brief: what the role answers for, what a successor reads first, what stands
  open in the zone and where. It restates neither the zone, which its doc holds, nor who holds
  the other roles, which the table holds: it points to both. It names mechanisms by their slugs
  and no skill of theirs (R8). Its description names its use cases alone: taking the role, and
  asking what the role answers for.

**Files several roles write.** *Keeps:* *The cut* — "a file several roles write is allowed,
marked … the person told what follows". The cut by mechanisms leaves every file with one role
on paper; what the sessions actually write is asked of them. Already seen: the entry file,
`harness`'s part, whose hand-written rules are the method role's work — marked in both zones,
and nothing follows while one session holds both roles; the hook and the mover, each one role's
part carrying a line another role's work asks for — one role's, the other asks. The register is
the method role's, and an entry any session writes there by the entry file's rule is a crossing

**The table and the local rule.** *Keeps:* *Who holds which*. L13's body becomes: its opening
words; the table; who holds what the table does not name; where a session reads its name; that
a mechanism's rules, skills and scripts are written and put to the person by the session whose
zone holds it, and a change in another's is asked of that session. The list of roles leaves it.
The rule says, in its body's words, which rules of the entry file it replaces for this tree —
the hand-written ones have no id for the grammar's `overrides` to cite — and it stays wrapped
as a straw dog.

**The entry contract** takes a new version with L13's block, and its findings an entry.

not markdown. Every instruction's text — each skill's, the local rule's, the format's — is

it. The keeper writes each stage's step into
[the journal](../research/standing-sessions-journal.md), from the other sessions' reports where

   the other, passing; a doc whose outputs are named in words, unchanged. Then the reader and

3. **The other three**, each by its session, asked with stage 2's two as the example and with

4. **The table.** The store's session is asked first for its rule's sentence on the row's name.
   Then the table's form in the installer, tests first: a role that is no mechanism; an empty

## Impact

**Impact.** Ten new files, all local; the format shelf, the shape's doc, its script and the
installer with their tests; the method's glossary; the local rules file and its block in the
entry file; the entry contract's version and its findings. Every session of this tree is handed
five more skill descriptions.

**Hidden edges.**

- These are the first mechanisms declared local here: the check's and the ship's rules for one
  had run on tests alone until the example.
- The installer reads one table inside a rule's body, where its contract was to read none.
- A brief wants to name the skills its role holds, and R8 forbids one skill naming another
  installed mechanism's; a brief names mechanisms by slug.
- A recipient whose own mechanism names an output by a whole code span that resolves to nothing,
  or that another of its mechanisms names too, is refused at its next update. Core's docs hold
  none; the recipients' keepers are told the ref.
- game-delta and fimonitor keep their roles as a list in their local rules. Nothing here touches
  that, and nothing reads it.
- Three of the five texts are other sessions' to write, and the table waits for all five.
- Core ships no default table: the default session is the parent's later slice, and until then
  who holds what a table does not name is each tree's own sentence.
- The front page may already be a part some mechanism declares; the check of stage 3 says so,
  and the bullet is then dropped or marked.
- A skill no mechanism names sits in a role's zone by its folder; when it is given a mechanism
  ([q-gbt7cy](../questions/q-gbt7cy-does-each-skill-state-only-what-it-owns.md)) the role's bullet
  is rewritten to name that.

**Leave alone.** The rows, the holds and the window; the settings' tables; the guard; the
recipients' local rules; a role's rights and skill set; the session mechanism's doc and rules,
whose one sentence is that session's to change.

**Recommendation.** Proceed, in the five stages, once the person has answered readings 8 and 9.
