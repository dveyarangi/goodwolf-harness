## A principle the person asks for: a rule closes its loop — 2026-11-16

The user, 2026-11-16, after the rule on messages was found to speak of the sender alone: "нам
надо добавить корневой принцип про всегдашнее закрытия лупа. если у правила больше одного
субьекта, оно должно прописывать поведение интеракции обоих субьектов - или убедиться что оба
субьекта получили полные правила интеракции. не знаю как это лучше сформулировать."

The case behind it: S10 as first landed told a session what to write and nothing of what the
receiver owes, and the person supplied the other half by hand the same day. Earlier cases of the
same shape in this tree: a unit taken over, where the taker is told and the holder's part was
added later; a hand-off whose conditions the receiver had to ask for.

A wording put to the person, not landed: *A rule closes its loop. A rule with more than one
party — one that asks and one that answers, one that hands over and one that takes — says what
each does, or names the rule that tells the other.* It is a rule about how rules are written,
and stands with the ones this question asks after.

The user, the same day, of that wording: "one that asks and one that answers - слишком
специфично. как насчет one that reads and one that writes, or one that produces and one that
consumes? - это тоже случаи того-же." The principle is wider than an exchange of messages: it
holds wherever one party produces what another relies on, which is the method's own word for a
seam. Reworded and put again: *A rule closes its loop. Where a rule has one party
produce what another relies on — a record and its reader, a hand-off and its taker, a question
and its answer — it says what each does, or names the rule that tells the other.*
