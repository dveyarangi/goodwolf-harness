Asked by the user at the align of 01-0010.0150.0020, 2026-10-25, the store, the session and
maintain taken as the first package: why does the store import the session? Read by a fresh
agent the same day, read-only.

**What `questions.py` takes from `sessions.py`.** At `--window`: the session seen, its holds, the
window memory that is also its sign of life, the rows, the other running sessions and their holds.
At `--wake`: registering and tending rows. At `--hook`: the tag, forgetting the window, being seen,
arriving. At every event: a tag not registered is refused, and the row is written after `at`; on a
rename the rows' ids are rewritten; `--check` checks the rows against the store; the path borrows
the session's `GONE_AFTER`. None of it touches the store's own data: entries, strikes, open, move,
close, `--tree` and `--seed` need nothing of the session. The reverse holds too: `sessions.py`,
whose docstring says the row stands without the store, imports from `questions.py` its refusal and
finding types, the id pattern its rows are written in, and the store's reader. Both test suites
import the other's.

**Essential and accidental.** Essential is one thing, a current position: `at` and the window's
path are defined from it. Registration, liveness, holds, other sessions, the wake, the hook's
session half, the rows' check and their renames are the session's duties, moved to it in its rules
on 2026-10-22 while the code stayed in `questions.py`. Two records disagree with the code:
`session.md` says the hook's session half is one call into the session script, where the hook makes
seven; `sessions.py` says the row stands without the store.

**The cut proposed.** Invert the dependency: the store exposes seams and imports nothing of the
session — the window takes the position and extra lines as arguments, `at` returns where it placed
the session and writes no row, a rename takes the extra records to rewrite, `--check` covers
entries, the window memory moves to the store whose record `questions.md` already says it is, the
path keeps its own constant; `sessions.py` takes the wake, the hook's half by an optional import,
the other sessions and holds, the row write, the row renames and their check. Without the session
mechanism a window draws from no position or from an explicit `--at q-N`, and `at` still strikes.
About 200 lines move; Q1's `--session` becomes optional and the session rule pointing at
`questions.py --wake` is amended; the hook files stay; the hook, wake and other-sessions tests move
to the session's suite.
