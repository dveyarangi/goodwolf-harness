## The sort by drift, 2026-10-07

Raised by the user inside the environment align, when "an agent reads the files a month later"
was seen to argue against an architecture doc as much as against a description of the
environment. A written thing is sorted by what it drifts against, not by its role:

1. **What it intends to become** — the future form. Drifts in itself, because it is refined as
   the work goes, and the work moves toward it. Spent once the thing reaches it: then what it
   settled lands in kind 2 and the record itself becomes kind 4. A ticket, a spec, an RFC, an
   open question. *(the user, 2026-10-07: first named purpose, then corrected — everything has
   a purpose, an evidence file included, so purpose is a property of every record and not a kind;
   the future form is one, and a landed one can move the invariants.)*
2. **What must always hold** — invariants, and the decisions with their reasons that make the
   thing what it is. Drifts from the doc into its derivatives: the code stops obeying, and the
   repair is to the derivative. Never spent by being reached.
3. **What exists** — a description for quick understanding, a description of the environment
   among them. Drifts from the derivatives into the doc: the code moves on, and the repair is to
   regenerate the doc from it.
4. **What happened** — history. Drifts by definition and is never repaired.

The test between 1 and 2, since both are written before the thing obeys them: a record of kind 1
expires when the thing matches it; one of kind 2 keeps governing after. The tree already runs the
handover from 1 to 2 — a resolved decision lands in its durable home before its ticket closes, and
a spec's surviving agreements get homes before it is archived — so the sort names a flow the
rules already hold apart.

**The sort is of a mechanism's records, never of its instruction** *(the user, 2026-10-07)*: an
instruction — a skill, a rule — is the mechanism itself, not something it writes. The one
exception is the mechanism shape, which produces instructions, so for it a skill is a record of
kind 2 and the invariants of that kind are `/skill-up`'s today.

**Proposed** *(the user, 2026-10-07)*: a clear classification of the kinds; no mechanism mixes
kinds in one record; every mechanism declares the kind of each of its outputs — beside the reader
its format already makes it name. The tree already holds one pair apart this way, a mechanism's
doc from its evidence, kind 2 from kind 4, and the ticket and the queue are the second shape that
forces the same cut: a ticket holds its outcome and criteria (1), its landed decisions (2, until
they land) and the story of what went wrong (4); the queue holds a candidate (1), a table derived
from the ticket headers (3) and a completed-step narrative (4).

**The whiteboard** *(the user, 2026-10-07)*: a working draft is needed in any case — a place
where everything bearing on a task is written down until enough has gathered to see its form,
and only then sorted by topic. It is not a fifth kind: it drifts against nothing, being the state
before the sort, and the kinds are what it is sorted into; maintenance does not hold it (the user,
2026-10-05). The ticket is, by idea, such a draft — unless the drafting moves to the question
store, and the ticket becomes the form a project gives one kind of interaction.

**Decided: the whiteboard is the body of the question the task stands on; the ticket is a record
of kind 1 alone** *(the user, 2026-10-07)*. A ticket is minted only once its form is seen, so it
cannot be the place where the form is found — that is the body of the lowest question containing
the task, which the placement rule already names. From that body a ticket is minted — outcome,
what to build, criteria — and is the form of one kind of interaction, agreed work carried into
code and closed. A decision lands from the body into its durable home; the story of what went
wrong goes to evidence or a session record, and the ticket links.

**Decided: core owns the contract of kind 1; the file form is its default realization, and a
project may substitute its own board** *(the user, 2026-10-07)*. What a unit of agreed work must
state — outcome, what to build, criteria, done when every criterion holds with verification among
them — is core's, since the delivery ring reads nothing else of a ticket. The file in
`docs/tickets/` is one realization; GitHub Issues, Linear or Jira is another, admitted when it
reads as the contract. That no recipient has asked is not the decisive fact: the adoption panel
of 2026-09-26 named tickets in the project's own tracker as a recurring reason across its
simulated readers, so the second shape is already in evidence.

**Decided: the queue is a record of kind 4 alone** *(the user, 2026-10-07)*: delivery status is
what has happened and where that leaves the work. Its table and counts are kind 3, rendered on
request as the derived-index ticket already says and never committed beside it; its order and
candidate are kind 1 and leave for a home of that kind; the rule by which it is ordered is kind 2
and leaves for the rules file of the mechanism that owns pacing.

**Decided: every document carries its kind** *(the user, 2026-10-07)*: a record's format
declares the kind, and each record says it, so a reader and a check know what it drifts against
and which way its repair runs.

Different citizens on different substrates. The method glossary's three kinds — documentation,
record, evidence — sort by role and cut across these: a mechanism's doc holds 2, a ticket's
outcome 1, an evidence file 4. `/maintain`'s *docs to their implementation, both ways* runs 2 and
3 together, which this sort takes apart: the two repairs go in opposite directions. The
maintenance method and each kind's home follow from the sort, which is why the environment align
(q-0018.0022) waits on it — whether the environment keeps a description record is a question
about kind 3.
