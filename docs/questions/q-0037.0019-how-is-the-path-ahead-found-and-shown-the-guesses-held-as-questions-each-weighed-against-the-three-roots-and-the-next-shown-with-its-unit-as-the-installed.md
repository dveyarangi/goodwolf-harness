Notation: each claim carries (confidence; *principle* = long-standing principle of the field, *recent* = evidence from roughly the last decade about situations of this kind). Direct evidence about one person steering with a proposing assistant is thin everywhere; where I say *recent* it is about adjacent situations unless stated.

#### 1. Goal–means trees built downward from the purpose
Benefits Dependency Networks (Cranfield, Ward & Daniel), Impact Mapping (Adzic), Opportunity Solution Trees (Torres), systems-engineering objective trees, Goldratt's Thinking Processes (current-reality → future-reality → prerequisite tree).

**Mapping.** The purpose statement is the top layer: drivers/pains, then objectives, then measurable outcomes or benefits. Each "answer" is a solution node; each candidate piece of work is an enabler whose value exists only through the chain of edges up to a pain. The question tree is the opportunity layer: things not yet shaped into solutions. "What next" = the enabler whose chain to a pain is complete and whose outcome has a measure; "readiness" = whether the chain's preconditions (the "business changes" or prerequisites) are in place; "leverage" = an enabler with many upward edges. A new idea must be placed as a node with a parent; one that cannot be placed is not a priority, it is an orphan. (mapping: high; *principle*)

**Where this situation sits.** On benefits-management's own ladder — no formal method → benefits identification → structuring (dependency network drawn) → realisation planning with owners and measures → review — the situation is at *identification*: pains and answers are written, the middle layer of measured outcomes and the edges from candidates to pains are not. The tree has a top and a bottom and no middle. (medium; *principle*)

**What the family knows about going wrong.** Solutions dressed as opportunities (the tree becomes a feature list with a purpose pinned on top) (high; *principle*, Torres is explicit). Trees that sprawl because every idea gets a node and nothing is pruned; Goldratt's trees in particular are known to become unmaintainable beyond a few dozen nodes (medium; *principle*). The missing measure in the middle: without an outcome metric the chain is a rationalisation, and anything can be argued to serve the purpose (high; *principle*). Root-cause fixation: one "core conflict" forced onto what is several problems (medium; *principle*). Dependency networks drawn once and never revisited become a funding artefact (high; *principle*, Ward's own finding).

#### 2. Flow economics and queue discipline
Reinertsen (*Principles of Product Development Flow*): cost of delay, WSJF, small batches, WIP limits; Kanban method (Anderson) with classes of service and explicit policies; Now/Next/Later roadmaps; RICE/ICE scoring as the degraded popular form.

**Mapping.** The question tree and candidate list are a queue; "guesses a lot and closes little" is unbounded WIP and an unbounded queue, which this family treats as the defect itself, not as a thing to be sorted. Value is cut by *time*: cost of delay per unit of duration; readiness is a precondition for entering the in-progress column; leverage and uncertainty are handled by classes of service (an "enabler" or "learning" class with its own cap) rather than by a score. A new idea enters the queue at the back by default and jumps only through an explicit expedite class with a hard cap (often one). The path is shown as columns with the Now band small and committed, Next and Later increasingly vague. (mapping: high; *principle*)

**Where this situation sits.** On the Kanban Maturity Model (levels 0–6): level 0–1, "oblivious / team-focused" — work is visible as items but there is no WIP limit, no explicit entry policy and no class of service; the first rung the family prescribes is simply to cap what is open. (medium; *principle*)

**What the family knows about going wrong.** Cost-of-delay numbers are invented and the ranking inherits the invention; WSJF divides two guesses (high; *principle*, acknowledged by proponents). The expedite class is abused until everything is urgent, which reproduces recency bias with a new name (high; *principle*). Large queues hide information about the system; a long backlog makes every estimate worse (medium; *principle*). Scoring frameworks like RICE degrade into post-hoc justification of what was already wanted (medium; *recent*, product-management practice literature). The family says nothing about whether the purpose is right; it sequences what is already accepted (high; *principle*).

#### 3. Decision analysis: value of information and real options
Stanford decision analysis (Howard, Raiffa), Hubbard's applied information economics, real options (Dixit & Pindyck; Trigeorgis), stage-gate as its institutional form.

**Mapping.** Each candidate is a decision under uncertainty with a prior on its worth. "Cannot start before a measure exists" is the case where expected value of information exceeds its cost: buy the measurement first. "Only unlocks other work" is option value: a piece whose payoff is the right, not the obligation, to do later pieces. "A guess whose worth is unknown until tried" is a pilot valued by the information it returns, not by its direct payoff. Value, readiness, leverage and uncertainty are therefore not four axes but one expected-value calculation with information and option terms; the family would say the four-way cut is a confusion. The path is shown as a decision tree with chance nodes, or as a staged portfolio with gates. (mapping: high; *principle*)

**Where this situation sits.** On the decision-quality chain (framing → alternatives → information → values → reasoning → commitment), the situation has alternatives in abundance and framing (the purpose statement) but no stated values function, no priors and no reasoning step; it is before the first quantitative rung. Hubbard's ladder — calibrated estimates → VOI on the handful of variables that matter → measure only those — has not been entered. (medium; *principle*)

**What the family knows about going wrong.** Precision theatre: models more exact than their inputs; the field's own remedy is calibration training and tornado sensitivity, which a lone person rarely does (high; *principle*). Analysis cost exceeding decision value for small pieces — decision analysis is for the few big decisions, not a hundred small ones (high; *principle*). Option hoarding: every piece justified as "enabling", options minted and never exercised; real-options literature knows option value is routinely overstated when exercise is discretionary and the holder is the same person who values it (medium; *principle*). VOI needs priors; with no priors the method cannot start, and the person here cannot hold even the earlier guesses (medium; inference, low).

#### 4. Cause prioritisation and theory of change
Importance–Tractability–Neglectedness (effective-altruism cause prioritisation, 80,000 Hours, Open Philanthropy); theory of change with explicit assumptions (Weiss); logframes and their adaptive successors (PDIA, "Doing Development Differently").

**Mapping.** The purpose statement with pains → aims → answers → degree built is close to a theory of change: pains are the problem, answers are interventions, "how far built" is outputs. The field adds the explicit *assumption* between each link, and demands an indicator per outcome. ITN gives three separable scores per candidate: importance (how much of a pain it relieves), tractability (readiness: can progress be made now), neglectedness (is anyone — including an earlier guess — already on it; a new idea duplicating a standing one scores low here, which is a direct anti-recency device). The path is the ToC diagram with assumptions marked tested/untested. (mapping: high; *principle*)

**Where this situation sits.** On the ladder logframe → theory of change with assumptions → adaptive management with learning loops, the situation has a logframe-like statement without assumptions or indicators; it is at the first rung. (medium; *principle*)

**What the family knows about going wrong.** Scale dominates: importance scores make speculative large-scale items beat tractable ones ("Pascal's mugging" in the field's own slang) (high; *principle*). Measurability bias: what has an indicator gets done (high; *principle*, Weiss). The "lockframe": a theory of change frozen into a reporting instrument so that learning cannot change it (high; *principle*, acknowledged by DFID/ODI reviews). Neglectedness is miscounted because the candidate's neighbours are not known — exactly the person's complaint that they cannot hold earlier guesses (medium; inference). ITN is designed for choosing among causes, not among tasks inside one cause, and loses resolution at small scale (medium; *principle*).

#### 5. Policy deployment with a bounded set: Hoshin Kanri and OKRs
Toyota's hoshin (X-matrix, catchball, 3–5 breakthrough objectives, PDCA review cadence); OKRs (Grove, Doerr) as the diluted form.

**Mapping.** Purpose → a small fixed number of breakthrough objectives for the period → annual objectives → quarterly initiatives; the X-matrix shows each initiative's correlation with each objective, so a candidate with no row is visibly outside the plan. Recency is defeated by two mechanisms: the number of objectives is fixed and small, and a new idea can displace one only at the review cadence, by catchball (argued, not declared). Readiness and leverage are not scored; they are argued in A3 form (background, current state, target, gap analysis, countermeasures, plan, follow-up). The path is the X-matrix plus the bowling chart of monthly results. (mapping: high; *principle*)

**Where this situation sits.** Hoshin's own deployment stages — ad hoc → objectives set but not cascaded → cascaded with catchball → review cadence with PDCA → self-correcting — the situation is ad hoc: there is a purpose, no bounded set of period objectives, no cadence at which a new idea may enter. (medium; *principle*)

**What the family knows about going wrong.** Cascade as command: objectives pushed down with no catchball become a to-do list; with one person the catchball partner is the assistant, which inverts the design (the proposer and the deployer are the same side) (medium; inference from *principle*). Too many objectives — hoshin practitioners say three to five and know that eight is already failure (high; *principle*). Goodhart effects on key results (high; *principle*). The year-end lurch and sandbagging (medium; *principle*, OKR practice literature).

#### 6. Operational design and mission command
Military operational art (end state, centre of gravity, lines of effort/operation, decisive points, backward planning, commander's critical information requirements), with design doctrine (US JP 5-0, Army Design Methodology).

**Mapping.** The purpose statement is the end state and the "troubles" are the problem frame; each answer is a line of effort; the pieces of work are objectives along lines; a decisive point is a piece whose completion enables others (leverage); conditions on a line are readiness. "Cannot start before a measure exists" is a CCIR: a named piece of information on which a decision waits, assigned to be collected. The path is the operational-design graphic: lines from current state to end state with decisive points marked and the phase line showing where one stands. Backward planning from the end state is the family's method of deriving next from purpose. A new idea is weighed by whether it sits on a line of effort or demands a new one — opening a new line is a commander's decision, not a staff suggestion. (mapping: high; *principle*)

**Where this situation sits.** Design doctrine's own sequence — understand the environment → define the problem → develop the operational approach → plan → assess and reframe — the situation has a problem definition and candidate approaches but no operational approach drawn (no lines, no decisive points, no phasing). It is before the operational approach. (medium; *principle*)

**What the family knows about going wrong.** End-state ambiguity, which the whole doctrine was revised to address after the 2000s wars (high; *recent* for the field, 2010s doctrine revisions). Decisive-point inflation: everything marked decisive (medium; *principle*). Design as a template filled in rather than thinking done (high; *principle*, critiques of Army Design Methodology). "A strategy of tactics": doing well what is near and calling it progress (high; *principle*). Reframing resisted because the graphic has been briefed (medium; *principle*).

#### 7. Appetite and betting; letting the backlog rot
Shape Up (Basecamp, 2019): no backlog; shaped pitches; a betting table at a fixed cadence; fixed time, variable scope; cool-down; hill charts.

**Mapping.** The growing tree of candidates is precisely the thing this family refuses to keep. Ideas live with whoever cares about them; before the betting table a few are *shaped* (problem, appetite, rough solution, rabbit holes, no-gos) and the table bets on at most a handful for one cycle. Recency is handled by cadence and by the burden of shaping: a new idea must wait for the next table and be shaped first, so being new confers nothing; "important ideas come back" is the stated policy on loss. Value is replaced by *appetite* — how much time this is worth — which inverts estimation. Readiness is shaping's job (rabbit holes found before betting). Uncertainty is shown on the hill chart (uphill = still figuring out, downhill = executing), which is the family's "where are we" display. (mapping: high; *principle*)

**Where this situation sits.** The family has no ladder; its own phases are raw idea → shaped pitch → bet → build → cool-down. Everything here is raw idea; nothing has been shaped, there is no table and no cycle length. (high; direct reading)

**What the family knows about going wrong.** Pitches shaped too big for the appetite (high; *principle*, stated in the book). Shaping as a one-person bottleneck — here the one person is also the only bettor and the only builder's supervisor (medium; inference). Hill charts are self-report and can sit at the top of the hill for weeks (medium; *recent*, practitioner reports). Deliberate loss of ideas is felt as negligence by people who keep records — the family answers that this feeling is the problem, which may not be acceptable to someone whose endeavour is partly the record (medium; inference). Six-week cycles are tuned for a team; a lone person with an assistant has no evidence base for cycle length (unknown).

#### 8. Argumentation structure for wicked problems: IBIS and design rationale
Rittel & Webber (wicked problems), Kunz & Rittel (IBIS), Conklin (dialogue mapping, Compendium), Buckingham Shum (design rationale capture).

**Mapping.** A tree of questions with positions and arguments is IBIS verbatim; the question store described here is an IBIS without the position and argument node types and without status marks. Rittel's thesis: for wicked problems the problem is understood through its solutions, so there is no deriving next from purpose — purpose and work co-evolve, and the honest method is to keep the argument explicit. "What next" is the issue whose resolution most reconfigures the others (the issue with the highest in-degree of "depends on" or "generalises"). A new idea is placed as a position under an existing issue or as a new issue; recency has no standing because placement is structural. (mapping: high; *principle*)

**Where this situation sits.** The family has no maturity ladder; the nearest is Buckingham Shum's stages of rationale capture — ad hoc notes → structured issues → issues with positions, arguments and status → rationale reused in later decisions. The situation is at structured issues: a hierarchy exists, positions and arguments are mostly not separate nodes, and status beyond open/closed is thin. (low; *principle*)

**What the family knows about going wrong.** The capture overhead: structuring argument costs more than it returns in the moment, and users stop (high; *principle*, the field's central known failure, Buckingham Shum). Dangling issues accumulate; the map grows faster than it resolves — which is this person's report in the field's own words (high; *principle*). The map substitutes for the decision; a well-structured argument feels like progress (medium; *principle*). The structure has no time axis, so it cannot show a path, only a position in an argument (high; *principle*).

#### 9. Research programmes, readiness levels and technology roadmapping
Lakatos (hard core, protective belt, progressive vs degenerating programmes); Hamming (open problems and open doors); NASA Technology Readiness Levels; DARPA's Heilmeier catechism; Cambridge roadmapping (Phaal, T-Plan).

**Mapping.** The endeavour is a research programme: the purpose statement is the hard core, the answers are the protective belt, the candidates are auxiliary hypotheses. Lakatos's criterion for what to do next: a programme is progressive while its additions predict something new that is then found; one that adds hypotheses only to absorb anomalies is degenerating — "guess a lot, close little" is the degenerating pattern named. TRLs separate maturity from worth: each answer has a readiness level 1–9, and the roadmap lays layers (purpose/market, answers/product, techniques/technology) over time with the links between layers drawn. The Heilmeier catechism is a gate for new ideas: what are you trying to do, how is it done today, what is new, who cares, what are the risks, how long, what are the midterm and final exams. Hamming's "open doors" is leverage: work that others can build on. (mapping: medium-high; *principle*)

**Where this situation sits.** Roadmapping's ladder (Phaal): no roadmap → fast-start workshop producing a first map → sustained updating → integrated with decision cycles. The situation has the layers' contents but no map and no time axis; it is before the first map. On Lakatos's gradient it shows degenerating signs — additions without tests — though the "how far built" field in the purpose statement is a step toward the progressive test. (medium; *principle*)

**What the family knows about going wrong.** TRL gaming and the valley of death between readiness 4 and 7 (high; *principle*). Roadmaps as wish lists with no delete step (high; *principle*). Lakatos's criterion is retrospective: it tells you a programme was degenerating, not that this next hypothesis is (high; *principle*, Feyerabend's objection). The Heilmeier catechism is designed to refuse, and in a one-person endeavour there is no one to be refused by (medium; inference).

#### 10. Personal horizons and the weekly review
Getting Things Done (Allen): capture → clarify → organise → reflect → engage; horizons of focus (purpose → vision → goals → areas → projects → next actions); someday/maybe list; the weekly review as the mechanism that "makes sense of the whole".

**Mapping.** This is the only family written for one person who cannot hold the whole in mind. "Something must make sense of the whole" is the weekly review by name; the someday/maybe list is where a tree of more-than-will-ever-be-done is meant to live, deliberately separate from the active projects list; a new idea is captured, not acted on, and judged at the review, which is the family's anti-recency device. The horizons give the derivation from purpose: a project without a goal above it and a next action below it is incomplete. Readiness is "has a next action"; leverage and uncertainty are not represented. (mapping: high; *principle*)

**Where this situation sits.** Allen's own stages — fundamentals (capture and clarify) → projects and priorities → focus and direction (horizons used) — the situation has capture, partial clarify, projects and priorities unseparated (active and someday mixed in one tree), and horizons written but not used to filter. Lower-middle rung. (medium; *principle*)

**What the family knows about going wrong.** The review is abandoned within weeks; the system then becomes a reproach (high; *principle*, acknowledged by Allen and by practitioner surveys). Next-action bias: small actionable things win over large uncertain ones because they have next actions (high; *principle*). Horizons are written once and never consulted (medium; *principle*). No concept of leverage or information value; the method is agnostic about worth (high; *principle*).

#### 11. Agenda setting and the attention bottleneck (descriptive)
Kingdon's multiple streams; Cohen, March & Olsen's garbage-can model; Jones & Baumgartner on attention and punctuated equilibrium; Simon on serial attention.

**Mapping.** The person's self-description is the garbage-can model: solutions (the assistant's proposals) looking for problems, participants with fluid attention, choice opportunities arising when a new idea arrives. "The newest is top priority" is the attention bottleneck: a single serial attention channel that reallocates abruptly rather than proportionally. Kingdon's streams say an item moves when problem, solution and the moment couple; recency is one such coupling and not a bias to be eliminated but an event to be gated. The family has little to say about readiness or leverage and nothing about showing a path. (mapping: high for the pathology; low for prescription; *principle*)

**Where this situation sits.** No ladder; the family describes rather than grades. Its one prescriptive observation: institutions that cope keep a formal agenda with a bounded number of items and a rule for entry, which is what families 2, 5 and 7 build. (medium; *principle*)

**What the family knows about going wrong.** It is a theory of going wrong; its known blind spot is that it offers description where practitioners want a mechanism (high; *principle*). Recent evidence adjacent to this situation: people working with a generative assistant produce more ideas and more similar ones, and anchor on the assistant's first proposal (medium; *recent*, 2023–2025 studies on LLM-assisted ideation and homogenisation; I have not seen a study of long-run steering).

#### 12. Probe portfolios in the complex domain (Cynefin)
Snowden & Boone; safe-to-fail probes with pre-agreed amplification and dampening; the distinction between complex and complicated work.

**Mapping.** "A guess whose worth is unknown until tried" is the complex domain by definition; the family's answer is not to value it but to run it as one of several parallel, cheap, safe-to-fail probes with the signals of success and failure named *before* starting, and to decide amplification or dampening on those signals. Work that "cannot start before a measure exists" is complicated-domain work (analyse then act) and should not be probed. Separating the two is the method; a single ranking across both is the error the family exists to name. The path is shown as the current domain per item and the probes in flight. (mapping: medium-high; *principle*)

**Where this situation sits.** No ladder; the family asks only whether the domain has been read. Here it has not: guesses and measurable pieces sit in one tree and are weighed in one way. (medium; direct reading)

**What the family knows about going wrong.** Domain misclassification, especially treating complex as complicated and buying analysis that cannot resolve it (high; *principle*). Probes without pre-stated amplify/dampen criteria become ordinary projects that never stop (high; *principle*). The vocabulary is used as decoration (medium; *recent*, practitioner critique).

### What the tasking smuggled in

- **Derivation.** The question assumes what-to-do-next can be *derived* from what the endeavour is for — a cascade model (families 1, 4, 5, 6 share it). Rittel (8), Cynefin (12), Kingdon (11) and emergent-strategy writing (Mintzberg) hold the opposite: purpose is partly found by doing, and the purpose statement is itself a hypothesis the work tests. The tasking treats the top of the tree as settled and the bottom as the problem.
- **The four-way cut.** Value, readiness, leverage, uncertainty are the tasking's categories. The fields cut otherwise: flow (2) cuts value by time and duration; decision analysis (3) collapses leverage and uncertainty into option value and value of information and would call the four-way cut a confusion; ITN (4) cuts importance/tractability/neglectedness; Shape Up (7) replaces value with appetite; Cynefin (12) cuts by domain before any valuation. Asking a family to "tell value from readiness, leverage and uncertainty" presupposes a cut most of them do not make.
- **A weighing mechanism preferred.** The framing — weigh each new idea against standing ones, show the path — points toward a scoring or pathfinding mechanism inside the tree. The strongest answers in several families are not weighing functions but *limits and cadences*: a cap on open work (2), three to five objectives (5), a betting table at fixed intervals (7), a weekly review (10), an agenda with an entry rule (11). The tasking does not mention cadence or limits at all.
- **The whole must be made sense of.** The tasking takes "far more than will ever be done" as a fact to manage. Families 7, 5 and 2 treat the size of the tree as the defect and prescribe letting most of it die; family 8 is the one that keeps everything and is the one whose central known failure is that it grows faster than it resolves.
- **Recency as a bias to remove.** Families 11 and 9 treat newness as information (new evidence, a coupling of streams) and locate the fault in the absence of a gate, not in the newness. "Without the newest winning by recency" pre-decides that.
- **Path as a view of the tree.** Every family that shows a path (6, 7, 9, 2) shows it on a *time* axis — lines of effort, hill charts, roadmap layers, Now/Next/Later. A tree of questions has no time axis; family 8 says so of itself. The tasking asks for a path from a structure that cannot carry one without a second axis being added.
- **One person.** The situation has two agents and the proposer of most ideas is the assistant. Agenda-setting (11) treats who proposes as the main variable; hoshin (5) relies on an adversarial catchball partner, which the arrangement lacks; the Heilmeier catechism (9) is a refusal device with no one to refuse. The tasking's "one person steers" hides that the steering problem is partly a proposer problem.

### Nothing to take

- **Multi-armed bandits / Bayesian optimisation.** Refused: the formalism needs repeated pulls of the same arm with a stationary reward; each piece here is done once and changes the reward of the others. Its one transferable idea — optimism toward untried arms, bounded by how often they have been tried — is already in family 3 as value of information.
- **Critical path / PERT / critical chain.** Refused: they need a fixed scope with estimated durations and a single deliverable; the tree is an open set with no end date. Theory of Constraints' bottleneck logic degenerates to "the person is the constraint", which is true and derives nothing.
- **Scrum and sprint backlogs.** Refused as a family: its mechanisms are team mechanisms (role separation, ceremonies, estimation by consensus); what remains for one person is the backlog, which is family 2 in degraded form.
- **Eisenhower urgent/important matrix.** Refused: its urgency axis is the recency trap given a name and a quadrant.
- **Weighted scoring (AHP, RICE as a standalone).** Refused as a family: it is a slice of family 2 or 3 and its known failure — numbers invented to ratify a preference — is already recorded there.
- **Zettelkasten and knowledge-graph methods.** Refused: they order knowledge, not action; they have no concept of next.
- **Direct evidence on long-run human–LLM steering of an endeavour.** None I can cite with confidence beyond the ideation-homogenisation and anchoring studies named under family 11; the question of how a person's agenda drifts over months when an assistant proposes most items is, as far as I know, unstudied.

## The outside view: the unit of work across fields, 2026-10-20

A second separate process, told not to read this repository and given nothing of the first, was
asked what a piece of work is an instance of across fields — software, research, learning a
subject, writing a long text, operating a service, deciding in a committee — what the pieces share
and where they differ in what *done* means, how a piece is broken down and ordered, what record
accompanies it, and how its steps run. Transmitted: one person with an assistant over many
sessions; a record per piece saying what it is for, what shows it done, what it depends on and what
came of it; pieces broken down when too big, their effect assessed before they start, placed in an
order, closed with evidence; the person's suspicion that the record and its handling are the same
across fields and only the format and the steps differ, held on one field's practice. Its output
follows as it came back; its headings are lowered one level.

### Families

Each family is a complete account of what the piece is. Confidence is given per claim as [level · kind], where kind is *principle* (long-standing in the field) or *recent* (evidence from the last decade or so, including evidence about assistant-driven work). Nothing below is harmonised; several families contradict each other on purpose.

#### 1. The work item of agile/lean software (story, issue, ticket)

**Mapping.** Piece = story or issue. "What it is for" = the story's *so that* clause. "What would show it is done" is two layers, not one: per-item acceptance criteria, and the team-wide Definition of Done (tests, review, docs) that applies to every item regardless of content. Dependencies = blocked-by links. Breaking down = story splitting, with a strong norm for vertical slices (each child ships something usable) over horizontal ones (layer by layer). Assessing effect = refinement (the "three amigos" conversation). Ordering = backlog prioritisation, ideally by cost of delay. Closure with evidence = merged change, green checks, demonstrated at review. Record while worked = ticket plus branch and review thread.

**Where this practice sits.** On the Kanban Maturity Model ladder (ad hoc → visualised → flow-managed → fit for purpose → optimising) a one-person practice with explicit done criteria and dependency links is at "flow-managed by form"; the upper rungs need measurement over many pieces and more than one worker, so the ladder gives out here [medium · principle].

**What the field knows about failure.** The card is a token for a conversation, not the specification (Jeffries' card–conversation–confirmation); treating the ticket as the spec is the classic failure [high · principle]. Horizontal splitting produces children that are each "done" and jointly ship nothing [high · principle]. "Done but not done" — acceptance met, Definition of Done skipped, debt accrues as "undone work" [high · principle]. Zombie backlogs: tickets that outlive their reason and are never deleted because deletion feels like loss [high · principle]. With a generating assistant: acceptance criteria written as tests get satisfied by code that is wrong — passes the check, misses the intent; this is now measured, not anecdotal [medium · recent]. Dependency links that are really one piece mis-split, so the "dependency" is an artifact of the cut [medium · principle].

#### 2. A job in a flow system (queueing, lean manufacturing, product-development flow)

**Mapping.** This family deliberately treats the piece's content as opaque. Piece = a job moving through a single-server queue. Done = the job exits the system. Breaking down = batch-size reduction. Ordering = a queue discipline re-evaluated at each pull (cost of delay, weighted shortest job first), never a fixed sequence drawn once. Dependencies = routing constraints. Assessing effect before start has no place here — flow thinking does not look inside the job. Record = the card plus cycle-time and age-in-state measurements.

**Where this practice sits.** One person working one piece at a time is the flow ideal (WIP = 1, serial); the family has nothing to teach about concurrency and everything to teach about batch size and what gets pulled next [high · principle].

**What the field knows about failure.** Large batches lengthen cycle time superlinearly; near-full utilisation blows queues up (Kingman's formula) — a worker who is always busy is a system that is always late [high · principle]. Multitasking is the dominant hidden cost (Goldratt; Weinberg's context-switch estimates) [high · principle]. Ordering by urgency instead of cost of delay; ordering fixed up front rather than at each pull [high · principle]. Student syndrome and Parkinson's law at the piece level — work expands to the slot, and starts at the slot's end [medium · principle]. Counting closed pieces as value invites Goodhart: throughput of tickets rises, value does not [high · principle]. Age-in-state of an open piece is the earliest leading signal of trouble, better than any estimate [medium · recent].

#### 3. The work package / product description in project management (WBS, PRINCE2, critical path)

**Mapping.** Piece = work package whose deliverable has a Product Description — purpose, composition, derivation (what it is built from), quality criteria, quality method, and who can judge it. That description is almost exactly "what it is for, what would show it is done, what it depends on." Dependencies = product flow diagram / activity network. Breaking down = WBS under the 100% rule (children exhaust the parent, nothing outside it). Assessing effect = impact analysis under change control. Ordering = critical path. Closure = product acceptance record. Record while worked = the work package plus checkpoint reports and a lessons log.

**Where this practice sits.** On P3M3 (awareness → repeatable → defined → managed → optimised), a practice with written product descriptions and acceptance evidence is "defined"; "managed" requires measurement across pieces that a single person rarely has [medium · principle]. The field would also note the practice is doing rolling-wave planning whether or not it calls it that.

**What the field knows about failure.** The planning fallacy: estimates by inside view are systematically optimistic; the remedy is reference-class forecasting from past pieces, not better decomposition [high · principle, with large recent datasets]. The 90%-done syndrome [high · principle]. Decomposing by discipline rather than by product means nothing integrated exists until the end — the reason PRINCE2 insists on product-based planning [high · principle]. Over-decomposition costs more than it informs; the WBS becomes the work [medium · principle]. Dependency networks drawn once and never refreshed [high · principle]. Buffers placed per piece get consumed; critical chain puts the buffer at the end [medium · principle]. Change control that forbids the plan from learning — the field's own answer is stage gates: "assess before start" is only possible with information that starting produces, so assessment is repeated at gates, not done once [high · principle].

#### 4. The study / experiment in research methodology

**Mapping.** Piece = a study. "What it is for" = the question or hypothesis. Done = the pre-specified analysis was run and its result recorded — including a null — never "the hypothesis held." This is the sharpest divergence from the ticket family: research has three terminal states (settled for, settled against, inconclusive or abandoned with reason) where the ticket has one [high · principle]. Dependencies = prior results, validated methods, materials. Breaking down = programme → questions → studies → pilot and main. Assessing effect before start = power analysis and value of information: which study, if run, changes the most downstream decisions. Ordering = Platt's strong inference: set up alternatives, run the experiment that excludes the most, repeat [high · principle]. Record while worked = the lab notebook: contemporaneous, dated, errors struck not erased, process not just outcome. Closure = write-up with data and deviations from plan.

**Where this practice sits.** On the open-science ladder (nothing pre-stated → preregistration → registered report → multi-site), a record that states done-criteria before work is preregistration-lite. On Lakatos's picture, the practice has a hard core (the long-lived aim) and a protective belt (the pieces); the field would ask which pieces are allowed to refute the core [medium · principle].

**What the field knows about failure.** Hypothesising after results are known; the garden of forking paths — many analyses, one reported [high · principle, confirmed by recent replication evidence]. The file drawer: inconclusive pieces never recorded, so the programme learns only from successes [high · principle]. Notebooks that record what was found and not how, which is the reproducibility crisis in miniature [high · recent]. Decomposing by deliverable rather than by question: the study produces an artifact and settles nothing [medium · principle]. The deepest structural point: a finding rewrites the decomposition tree, so the order of pieces cannot be fixed in advance — exploratory and confirmatory pieces must be labelled as such, not forced into one record [high · principle].

#### 5. The problem on a problem-oriented record (medicine, casework)

**Mapping.** Piece = a problem on the problem list (Weed's problem-oriented medical record). "What it is for" = the problem statement. Done = resolution criteria in the plan; but the field has a third state the ticket lacks — *chronic, monitored*: not closed, not active work, re-checked on a schedule [high · principle]. Dependencies = interactions between problems (comorbidity, drug interaction) — this is the field's form of impact assessment, done per encounter. Breaking down = refining a symptom into diagnoses and sub-problems as information arrives. Ordering = acuity/triage. Record while worked = a progress note per problem per encounter (subjective, objective, assessment, plan), so the many-sessions structure is native: the record is organised by problem across encounters, not by encounter. Closure = resolved and documented. Handover between clinicians = structured handoff (SBAR, I-PASS).

**Where this practice sits.** Of all the families this one maps the session boundary best: an assistant that forgets between sessions is a new clinician at each encounter, and the problem list plus last progress note is exactly what it is handed. Maturity ladder: narrative chart → problem-oriented record → electronic record with reconciled problem list → care pathways. A record organised by piece with per-session notes is at the problem-oriented rung [high · principle].

**What the field knows about failure.** Problem-list rot — stale, duplicate, never reconciled entries — is endemic in electronic records [high · recent]. Copy-forward: last session's note pasted as this session's, so the record grows and says less [high · recent]. Premature closure — settling on an answer and stopping inquiry — is the leading cognitive cause of diagnostic error [high · principle and recent]. Handoff is where most errors enter; structured handoff measurably reduces them [high · recent]. Weed's own complaint: the record kept for billing or audit rather than for thinking, so it is accurate and useless [medium · principle].

#### 6. The task with purpose in mission command (military doctrine)

**Mapping.** Piece = a task. The record is doctrinally task + purpose + end state, and the purpose is mandatory for a reason: so that the executor can abandon the task when it no longer serves the purpose [high · principle]. Done = end state achieved, explicitly distinguished from task completed ("task complete, mission failed"). Dependencies = phasing, sequencing, decisive points. Breaking down = nested concepts: each level turns its task into subordinate tasks each with its own purpose. Assessing effect before start = wargaming courses of action against what can go wrong. Ordering = phases and a synchronisation matrix. Record while worked = orders, situation reports, a running estimate. Closure = the after-action review: what was meant to happen, what happened, why, what changes.

**Where this practice sits.** The ladder runs from detailed orders (what and how) to mission orders (what and why, how left to the executor). A record that leads with "what it is for" and leaves the steps inside the piece open is at the mission-orders end; the assistant is the subordinate given intent [high · principle].

**What the field knows about failure.** Task without purpose yields literal compliance when conditions change — the canonical failure, and the one that matters most when the executor is an assistant optimising a stated criterion [high · principle; medium · recent for assistants]. End states stated so abstractly they constrain nothing; doctrine requires conditions, not aspirations [high · principle]. Synchronisation matrices become fiction at first contact (Moltke) — ordering survives only as intent, not as sequence [high · principle]. The after-action review turns into attribution of blame and nothing is learned; the remedy is facilitation and the four fixed questions [medium · principle]. Relief in place: the running estimate not handed over, so the incoming unit re-learns the ground — a session-boundary failure with a named ritual [medium · principle].

#### 7. The objective under mastery learning (instructional design, self-regulated learning)

**Mapping.** Piece = a learning objective. "What it is for" = its place in a competence hierarchy. Done = a criterion-referenced performance by the learner — production, not recognition [high · principle]. Dependencies = prerequisite structure (learning hierarchies, knowledge-space theory, prerequisite graphs). Breaking down = task analysis into sub-skills. Ordering = prerequisite order crossed with spacing and interleaving. Record = mastery grid or portfolio plus a review schedule. Closure = demonstrated mastery — which decays, so closure is dated and re-verified.

**Where this practice sits.** Bloom's mastery learning → Keller's personalised system → competency-based education; on the self-regulated-learning cycle (forethought, performance, self-reflection) a per-piece record with stated done-criteria is the forethought phase made explicit. The family's structural objection: in this field "done" is a state of the worker, not of the world, so the record can only point at evidence (a performance), never contain it [high · principle].

**What the field knows about failure.** Illusion of competence: fluency with material is mistaken for mastery; recognition tests confirm it [high · principle, large recent evidence]. Over-atomisation produces inert knowledge that does not transfer [medium · principle]. Treating closure as permanent: the forgetting curve makes every "done" provisional; desirable difficulties (spacing, retrieval) are the remedy [high · principle]. The assistant-specific hazard: the generation effect means the learner must produce; an assistant that produces makes the piece "done" for the wrong party, and recent tutoring studies show exactly this when the assistant gives answers rather than prompts [high · recent].

#### 8. The section / draft in composition and editorial practice

**Mapping.** Piece = chapter, section or scene. "What it is for" = its job in the argument or arc (outline, beat sheet, scene card). Done is staged, not binary: drafted → revised → line-edited → copyedited → proofed, each stage accepted by a reader. Dependencies = what the reader must know on arrival. Breaking down = outlining — contested in the field between planners and discoverers; the Snowflake method is decomposition by successive refinement. Assessing effect = continuity and structure checks. Ordering = reading order, drafted in any order. Record while worked = outline, style sheet, revision notes, bracketed notes-to-self inside the draft. Closure = acceptance by the editor.

**Where this practice sits.** The cognitive-process model of writing (planning, translating, reviewing, recursive and non-linear) is the field's settled account; a per-piece record is its planning phase externalised. The field's structural objection is the strongest of any here: the whole is the unit of quality, so per-piece "done" is provisional until the whole is reread, and every revision pass reopens all pieces at once [high · principle].

**What the field knows about failure.** Outline paralysis on one side, discovery drift on the other [high · principle]. Premature polishing of a section later cut — sunk cost at the paragraph level [high · principle]. Local coherence, global incoherence: every piece done, the book not [high · principle]. Revision as fixing rather than re-seeing (Murray) [medium · principle]. Voice fragmenting when sections are produced separately — observed sharply when sections are assistant-generated [medium · recent].

#### 9. The motion / agenda item in deliberative procedure (parliamentary rules, minutes, decision records)

**Mapping.** Piece = a question before the body. "What it is for" = the question itself. Done = a disposition: adopted, rejected, postponed, referred, tabled, withdrawn — done means decided, not implemented; implementation is a separate piece (the action item) [high · principle]. Dependencies = precedence of motions and germaneness. Breaking down = division of the question, amendment, referral to a committee. Assessing effect = debate. Ordering = agenda and precedence. Record = minutes, which record what was done and not what was said. Closure = the recorded vote; an action register carries what follows.

**Where this practice sits.** Ladder: ad hoc discussion → minutes → formal procedure → consent-based decision → decision records with rationale (the architecture decision record descends from here). A one-person body with an assistant has no quorum problem but inherits the decided/implemented split in full [high · principle].

**What the field knows about failure.** Relitigating decided questions — procedure's answer is precise: reconsideration only by one who voted with the prevailing side, within the session; otherwise rescission with notice or supermajority. "Do not reopen without new evidence" is a weaker restatement of this [high · principle]. Minutes as narrative rather than record of decisions [high · principle]. Action items without owner and date — the committee's chronic failure mode [high · principle]. Groupthink and the Abilene paradox, in which a body decides what no member wants [medium · principle].

#### 10. The issue in design rationale (IBIS, wicked problems, reflective practice)

**Mapping.** Piece = an issue. "What it is for" = the question it poses. Positions answer it; arguments support or attack positions. Done = a position selected with its arguments recorded — but Rittel's point is that wicked problems have no stopping rule; "done" is when resources or patience end [high · principle]. Dependencies = issues raised by positions. Breaking down = raising sub-issues, which is itself a contestable design move, not a neutral act [high · principle]. Ordering = follow the argument; no intrinsic order. Record = issue graph; QOC; decision records.

**Where this practice sits.** Ladder: no rationale → informal notes → structured rationale → routine decision records. The field's own finding is that structured rationale capture fails in practice because of a cost–benefit asymmetry: whoever pays the capture cost rarely reaps the benefit [high · principle, with field studies]. A person plus an assistant inverts the asymmetry — the assistant pays the capture cost — so this may be the first setting where rationale capture is sustainable. Unknown; no evidence either way [unknown · recent].

**What the field knows about failure.** The capture problem above. Rationale rot: decisions kept past their reasons [medium · principle]. Symptom mistaken for problem; every solution a one-shot that changes the problem [high · principle]. Schön: problem setting precedes problem solving, and the practitioner learns by a reflective conversation with the situation — the record should hold the reframing, not only the resolution [high · principle].

#### 11. The subgoal in hierarchical planning (AI planning, personal task systems)

**Mapping.** Piece = a subgoal. "What it is for" = its parent in the goal hierarchy. Done = goal predicate satisfied. Dependencies = preconditions. Breaking down = hierarchical task-network methods. Assessing effect before start = the frame and ramification problems — which state the action changes beyond what it names; this is the only family that treats "assess effect before start" as a named hard problem [high · principle]. Ordering = partial-order, least-commitment planning: order only what must be ordered. Record = plan plus asserted world state. In personal practice (Getting Things Done): project = outcome, "what does done look like", a next action small enough to start, weekly review as the maintenance ritual.

**Where this practice sits.** General Problem Solver → STRIPS → hierarchical task networks → personal systems. A dependency-graph record with a review cadence is GTD plus a partial-order planner [medium · principle].

**What the field knows about failure.** The goal predicate is not the intent; an optimiser satisfies the predicate and misses the intent (specification gaming, now well catalogued) [high · recent]. Plans are brittle; replanning on failure must be designed in [high · principle]. Impact assessment is undecidable in general; planners bound it by assuming nothing changes but what is declared [high · principle]. In personal systems: next actions too big to start, lists that rot, the review skipped — the practice decays silently [high · principle].

#### 12. The work order / change in operating a running system (maintenance, service management, reliability engineering)

**Mapping.** Piece = work order or change request. "What it is for" = the fault or need. Done = sign-off by someone other than the doer, then a post-implementation review; closure is probationary — confirmed by absence of recurrence over a monitoring window, not at the moment of completion [high · principle]. Dependencies = parts, outage windows, other changes in flight (the change advisory board exists to find collisions). Breaking down = job plan tasks. Assessing effect before start = mandatory risk assessment with blast radius and rollback plan. Ordering = criticality and risk. Record while worked = the work order with job plan, permit, as-found and as-left. The chain incident → problem → change makes three piece types with different done-criteria.

**Where this practice sits.** Reactive → preventive → condition-based → predictive; a practice with written impact assessment and evidence at closure is at the preventive rung with a documented record; the condition-based rung needs instrumentation of the running thing [medium · principle].

**What the field knows about failure.** Pencil-whipping: the checklist ticked without the work done; documented in aviation and nuclear [high · recent]. Closed-without-verification; the doer signing off their own work [high · principle]. Deferred-maintenance backlog growing until a failure forces it [high · principle]. Change collision in a shared window [high · principle]. Postmortems that produce action items nobody does [high · recent]. Normalisation of deviance: each small departure accepted until the departure is the norm [high · principle].

### What the tasking smuggled in

- **The preferred answer.** "The record and its handling are the same across fields; only the piece's format and the steps inside differ" is stated as a suspicion but functions as the hypothesis to confirm. Several families deny it structurally: research has three terminal states not one; medicine has a monitored-not-closed state; learning dates its closures and re-verifies; writing reopens all pieces at each pass; committees separate decided from implemented. The *handling* differs, not just the format.
- **Decomposition before work.** The tasking assumes pieces are broken down, assessed and ordered before they start. Research, writing and design rationale all hold that a piece is often begun to find out whether it is a piece; project management's answer (stage gates, rolling wave) concedes the same. The cut "assess, then start" is one tradition's.
- **"Done" as binary and permanent.** Mastery learning, operations and medicine each deny permanence for their own reasons (decay, recurrence, chronicity).
- **"One at a time."** This is the flow ideal, not a fact of the fields: medicine carries many open problems with none closing; committees dispose of many items per sitting; writers revise everything at once.
- **Effects foreseeable before start.** AI planning names this the frame problem and bounds it by assumption; research exists to discover the effects. The tasking treats impact assessment as a step; two fields treat it as the unsolved part.
- **The assistant as doer, verification unassigned.** Every field with a second party (editor, reviewer, sign-off officer, the clinician receiving handoff) casts the second party as *verifier*. The tasking casts the assistant as doer and says nothing of who verifies; operations would not accept the doer signing off.
- **The session boundary glossed.** "Across many sessions" is said once and treated as incidental. The fields that know about handover (medicine, military, operations) treat the boundary as the primary hazard and ritualise it.
- **Dependencies flattened.** Fields distinguish hard preconditions, information dependencies and resource dependencies; the tasking has one word.
- **The six-field list is not the fields' own cut.** "Writing software" and "operating a service" are one tradition's split; "learning a subject" and "writing a long text" are units of individual cognition where the others are units of organisation. The list mixes what one person does to themselves with what a body does to the world.
- **Vocabulary.** "Piece", "record", "closed with the evidence" are a ticketing vocabulary; the question was asked already inside the family it has in hand.

### Nothing to take

- **The ledger transaction / double-entry account.** Done = reconciled, order = chronological, no decomposition, no purpose field. The mapping holds on one axis of five; refused.
- **The obligation in contract law.** Strong on two axes — conditions precedent are exactly dependencies, discharge by performance is exactly done, "substantial performance" is a litigated theory of done-enough — but nothing on decomposition, ordering or record-while-worked, and no maturity ladder. Refused as a family; the two mappings are noted for whoever wants them.
- **The quest in game design.** Objective, completion condition, prerequisites, journal — a complete structural match, but it is a designed fiction of work, and its failure knowledge is about player experience, not about doing work. Refused.
- **Objectives and key results, plan-do-check-act, theory of constraints.** Each describes the parent or the cycle, not the piece; their usable content is already inside families 2, 3 and 12. Refused as separate families.
- **Regulated design controls (verification vs validation, traceability matrix, design history file).** Real material — verified against spec versus validated against need is a distinction the tasking lacks — but it is a stricter dialect of families 3 and 12, not a different account. Folded, not refused.
- **The database transaction.** Commit as done, locks as dependencies; no purpose, no decomposition, no record beyond the log. Refused.

## The cadence, 2026-10-23

The person, on when a new idea may enter work, restated by the agent from their words of the day
and put back to them before it is a decision:

- Not every new idea is first. An idea said in open conversation captures the session only if the
  person keeps returning to it; one raised once and left lies as a side question. The store already
  measures the returning: its `struck` count.
- Boundaries do not depend on the method. The ring's step ends are the practice mechanism's and
  cannot be core's boundary; what is core's: the session's start, where `/recall` redraws the path;
  the person's explicit word, entering a unit or dropping it; and a unit's close, which every method
  has, though each names its own.
- Once in the ring — a unit taken into work — the agent holds the ring until the person explicitly
  drops the task. An idea said mid-ring is opened, placed and given its rung as a side question; the
  session's position stays on its unit; the idea does not enter work.
- Consequence for Q1: in the ring, a new question is opened without the session moving onto it —
  `open` without `at`; in open conversation Q1 stands as it is, the position following the message.
- Consequence for L12's count: a session counts as work only while in the ring, not by its position
  in open conversation; how the store tells a session in the ring from one in conversation is open.
