The output below is verbatim.
> 
> Saved conversations can support a retrospective account of observable interaction costs and continuity. They cannot, alone, establish the person’s total effort or isolate why outcomes changed.
> 
> **1. Interaction accounting: count the work required to resume**
> 
> Structural mapping: treat each return after an interruption, assistant change, or task change as a resumption episode. Code user turns for supplying background, locating prior decisions, restating constraints, correcting misunderstandings, and confirming readiness. Compare the burden before substantive work resumes.
> 
> Evidence available: recorded words, turns, repeated facts, and explicitly reported effort. A stronger measure distinguishes necessary updates from repetition of information already available.
> 
> Failure modes: shorter explanations may reflect omitted context or lowered expectations; longer explanations may accompany harder work. Transcript timestamps include unrelated delays. Assistant verbosity changes turn counts.
> 
> Confidence: **high** that this measures visible conversational work; **low** that it measures total effort; **low** that a downward trend by itself identifies improvement.
> 
> **2. Obligation tracing: follow what must remain true**
> 
> Structural mapping: represent each explicit requirement, promise, constraint, or accepted decision as an obligation with an origin, scope, subsequent amendments, and observable disposition. At each transition, ask whether applicable obligations were retained, deliberately revised, fulfilled, or silently lost.
> 
> Evidence available: direct traces connecting an earlier obligation to later action, plus identifiable omissions when an obligation should have governed an observed action. Distinguish obligations the assistant could access from those only the evaluator knows.
> 
> Failure modes: “all existing obligations” is an unknowable denominator if some were never recorded. Requirements can conflict or expire without explicit discussion. Repeated wording can conceal changed meaning. Conversations may describe successful work without showing its artifacts.
> 
> Confidence: **high** for explicit, scoped obligations and directly observed outcomes; **moderate** for inferred applicability; **low** for claims of complete preservation.
> 
> **3. Recurrence analysis: evaluate corrections against later opportunities**
> 
> Structural mapping: convert each correction into a failure pattern, a proposed preventive change, and a set of later situations where the pattern could recur. Count recurrence against relevant opportunities, not elapsed time or total messages. Separate exact repetition from structurally related mistakes.
> 
> Evidence available: evidence that a correction was remembered, consulted, followed, bypassed, or ineffective. Later successful handling of genuinely similar situations is stronger than an absence of complaints.
> 
> Failure modes: later cases may be easier; the person may intervene earlier; prevention may work only through increased supervision. A broad failure category makes everything recur, while a narrow one makes nothing recur. Few later opportunities produce weak evidence.
> 
> Confidence: **high** that exposure matters; **moderate** for demonstrated transfer across well-matched cases; **low** for “the correction prevented the mistake” without a credible comparison.
> 
> **4. Matched comparisons and interrupted time series: examine changes over time**
> 
> Structural mapping: identify dated changes in working practice, then compare comparable episodes before and after them. Match on task type, novelty, assistant continuity, interruption length, and available context. Plot both burden and failures, retaining unfavorable cases.
> 
> Evidence available: within-person associations, plausible temporal ordering, and repeated patterns across transitions. Several changes introduced separately offer more discrimination than a single wholesale change.
> 
> Failure modes: assistant, task, and practice changes may occur together. The person learns, task selection changes, and later records may become more selective. Small samples and overlapping interventions undermine statistical precision. No adjustment recovers an unrecorded confounder.
> 
> Confidence: **moderate** for descriptive trends with stable coding; **low to moderate** for attributing effects to a particular practice; **low** for generalization beyond this person and period.
> 
> **5. Process tracing and counterexample search: inspect how continuity succeeds or fails**
> 
> Structural mapping: reconstruct selected episodes as chains: available context → assistant interpretation → action → user response → downstream consequence. Seek the expected intermediate steps of each explanation and actively search for cases that contradict it.
> 
> Evidence available: mechanisms consistent with particular outcomes—for example, a prior decision was retrieved and explicitly changed a later action. Contradictions can narrow an overly broad explanation.
> 
> Failure modes: polished assistant explanations may be retrospective rationalizations. Memorable failures dominate selection. Detailed narratives can sound causal while leaving competing explanations intact.
> 
> Confidence: **moderate** for mechanisms supported by multiple independent traces; **low** for mechanisms inferred solely from an assistant’s account of its reasoning.
> 
> **What the tasking smuggles in**
> 
> It presumes that “shared context” is identifiable, that lower restoration effort is desirable, and that preserving obligations is usually preferable to revising them. It treats corrections as interventions with recognizable descendants. It also risks treating saved conversations as a complete behavioral record and several weeks as enough exposure to judge prevention.
> 
> An evaluation should therefore declare its units and denominators, distinguish observation from inference, and report unresolved cases. Independent coding of a sample can expose ambiguous categories; it cannot repair missing evidence. Confidence: **high** in these limitations as properties of the proposed evidence.
