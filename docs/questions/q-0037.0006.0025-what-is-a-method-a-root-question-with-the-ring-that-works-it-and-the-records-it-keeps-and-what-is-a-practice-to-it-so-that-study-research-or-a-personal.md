#### Family 1 — Design rationale and argumentation-based knowledge management (IBIS → gIBIS → QOC → Dialogue Mapping → ADR)

**(a) Name and vocabulary.** *Issue-Based Information Systems* (Kunz & Rittel, 1970), the "wicked problems" lineage (Rittel & Webber, 1973); *gIBIS* (Conklin & Begeman, 1988); *QOC — Questions, Options, Criteria* (MacLean et al., 1991); *Design Rationale* as a field (Moran & Carroll eds., 1996); *Dialogue Mapping / Compendium* (Conklin, 2005); the lightweight descendant *Architecture Decision Records* (Nygard, 2011; MADR; Zimmermann's Y-statements). Vocabulary: *issue, position, argument, resolution; question, option, criterion; capture problem; incremental formalization; formality considered harmful; decision record, status (proposed/accepted/superseded), consequences.*

**(b) Mapping.**
- The tree of unsettled matters (part 3) = an IBIS *issue network*: issues nested under issues ("sub-issue of"), positions (the decisions), arguments (the dated evidence), with "refused alternative" = a *position* with "objects-to" arguments. The three fixed roots have no IBIS equivalent — IBIS is root-free by design; this is QOC-like fixed top-level questions imposed on the network.
- "Closes with a kind of closure and a pointer; never deleted" = IBIS *resolution* with the gIBIS convention that resolved issues stay in the graph.
- "Standing summary rewritten whole; evidence appended, never rewritten" = Conklin's distinction between the *map* (edited) and the *dialogue* (accumulated); also ADR practice: the record is immutable once accepted, superseded by a new record, not edited (Nygard 2011). Note the arrangement departs: it *rewrites* the summary in place and *appends* evidence — a hybrid ADR/log, not pure ADR.
- Part 6 (load-bearing decisions as reasons, not facts) = the ADR movement's "document the why" and Zimmermann's Y-statement form ("In the context of … facing … we decided … to achieve … accepting …").
- "Units of work cut from the body of the question they answer" = Conklin & Burgess-Yakemovic's (1991) *process-oriented* design rationale: rationale captured in the flow of work rather than reconstructed after.
- Part 7 (provisional text bound to an open question) = ADR status *proposed*; closest named concept is Shipman & McCall's *incremental formalization* — text is first informal, structure is added as the question matures.
- "Every message first placed at the lowest containing question" — the family has **no word** for compulsory classification of every utterance; gIBIS users classified voluntarily. This is the sharpest departure (see (d)).
- Parts 4, 5, 9, 10, 12 (autonomy threshold, reply form, declared apparatus, rule-failure register, portability): **no word**. Design rationale theory is about *content*, not about who may act or how the apparatus is governed.

**(c) Position on the family's ladder.** Lee (1997, "Design rationale systems: understanding the issues") splits DR systems into *argumentation-based* (IBIS/QOC: structure the debate as it happens) and *documentation-based* (write rationale after the fact). This arrangement is argumentation-based with compulsory structuring at utterance granularity — the most formal end, where gIBIS sat, beyond where the field retreated to (Conklin's facilitated Dialogue Mapping, ADRs as sparse records). On the ADR sub-ladder it is beyond Nygard's "lightweight" (one file per decision) and toward the "full" end (decision + alternatives + evidence + supersession chain).

**(d) What the family knows about failure.**
- **The capture problem** [P, high; still cited in DR, PKM and software-architecture courses]: rationale capture competes with doing the work; the person who pays the capture cost is not the one who benefits (Grudin 1996, "Evaluating opportunities for design capture"). Fix the field settled on: capture *in the flow* (Conklin & Burgess-Yakemovic 1991) and lower the formality (Nygard's one-page ADR).
- **Formality considered harmful** [P, high; Shipman & Marshall 1999, still the standard citation]: imposed formal structure causes cognitive overhead, forces premature structuring, loses tacit knowledge, and users classify wrongly or stop. Their remedy: *incremental formalization* — let structure arrive late. The arrangement's "place every message at the lowest containing question" is exactly the imposed-structure pattern this literature warns against; its own "missing parent" rule is the symptom the literature predicts (misplaced nodes), already turned into procedure.
- **gIBIS field failures** [E, Conklin & Burgess-Yakemovic 1991; Fischer et al. 1991]: designers resisted classifying thoughts as issue/position/argument mid-design; the tool survived only with a facilitator. The lesson the field drew: the human should not be the classifier. Here the classifier *is* the assistant, which may dissolve the original objection — an untested claim (no source observed either way).
- **Rationale rot** [P, high]: records drift from the artefact; the ADR community's answer is *supersede, never edit* so that the trail stays truthful even when stale. The arrangement's rewrite-in-place summary trades that guarantee for currency.
- **"Issue sprawl"** [P, med]: IBIS networks grow faster than they resolve; Conklin's remedy was facilitation and periodic re-mapping. The arrangement's strike-count on questions and its recorded "where it was left" lines are a home-made answer; the family's answer (prune/merge by a facilitator) is explicitly available in the description only as hand re-parenting.

**(e) What sets it apart.** (i) The agent, not the human, bears the classification cost — the family's main failure mode is addressed by relocating it, not removing it. (ii) Three fixed roots: IBIS is anarchic; this is a *taxonomy with an argumentation network inside*. (iii) The rationale store is also the dispatcher of work (work cut from questions) and the lock table for concurrent sessions — two roles DR systems never carried.

**(f) Confidence.** Mapping: high. Ladder placement: high. Failure modes: P-high for capture problem and formality; E-med for gIBIS field results (old, 1991, but never contradicted). The claim that an LLM classifier dissolves the capture problem: low — no evidence found.

#### Family 2 — Methodology of research programmes and the logic of question and answer (Lakatos, Kuhn, Collingwood; preregistration and the lab notebook)

**(a) Name and vocabulary.** Lakatos, *Falsification and the Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes* (1970): *hard core, protective belt, negative heuristic, positive heuristic, progressive vs degenerating problemshift, ad hoc adjustment*. Kuhn (1962): *paradigm, normal science, anomaly, crisis*. Collingwood (*An Autobiography*, 1939) and Gadamer (1960): *logic of question and answer — every statement is an answer to a question; the primacy of the question*. Open-science practice: *preregistration, Registered Reports, the laboratory notebook (bound, dated, append-only), protocol deviation*.

**(b) Mapping.**
- Part 12's held layer (cycle, stops, tree, reply form) = Lakatos's **hard core**, protected by the **negative heuristic** ("do not direct the modus tollens at the core" = "the assistant may never change the threshold"; "do not reopen an accepted decision without new evidence"). The swappable layer (how tests are written, how architecture is documented) = the **protective belt**, adjusted to absorb anomalies. The **positive heuristic** = the delivery cycle: a programme of what to do next.
- Part 3's tree = the Collingwoodian claim made operational: every statement (every message) has a question it answers, and nothing is understood until its question is found. The three roots are the programme's founding questions. "Never deleted" = Collingwood: a question once asked remains part of the history of the enquiry.
- Part 10 (register of rules that did not fire) = Kuhn's **anomaly** ledger; "a recurrence strikes the earlier entry and is counted" = anomalies accumulating toward **crisis**; "what to do about a rule that keeps failing is left open" = the family's own honest position that there is no algorithm for when crisis becomes revolution.
- Part 2's "nothing is built before the person accepts a written statement of where the matter stands" = **preregistration** / Registered Report stage-1 acceptance: the hypotheses and analysis plan accepted before data are collected.
- Part 1 and the evidence-append rule = the **lab notebook**: dated, append-only, never rewritten, summarised elsewhere.
- Part 7 (provisional text marked and bound) = Lakatos's **ad hoc hypothesis** made visible: the arrangement labels each ad hoc patch with the anomaly it waits on, so its ad-hoc-ness can be audited.
- Part 4 (autonomy threshold) and part 5 (reply form): **no word** — philosophy of science has no theory of delegated agency.
- Part 13 (recipient grew a second cycle): in this vocabulary a **rival programme sharing the hard core but with a different positive heuristic**; whether that is one programme or two is precisely Lakatos's problem of programme identity, which he never solved.

**(c) Ladder.** Lakatos: a programme is *progressive* while its belt adjustments predict novel facts and *degenerating* while they only accommodate. The arrangement, judged by what the description says, is in a **self-referential early phase**: its own founding question (what it is for) is open in its own tree, and its evidence of working is mostly internal. On the open-science ladder: it is at *preregistration* but **not** at *Registered Report* — there is no independent reviewer; the same person who proposes accepts.

**(d) Failure knowledge.**
- **Degenerating problemshift** [P, high; still taught in every philosophy-of-science syllabus]: a programme whose adjustments only save the core from refutation. The register of rule-failures and the economy rules are the arrangement's defence; the family would ask what *novel prediction* the arrangement has made and had confirmed (what did it foresee about a project that the project then showed?).
- **Monster-barring and exception-barring** (Lakatos, *Proofs and Refutations*, 1976) [P, med]: redefining terms so counter-examples cease to count. The arrangement's "the contradiction is not counted as a violation" (part 7) is a *sanctioned* exception-barring; the family's warning is that sanctioned exceptions become the habit.
- **Preregistration works** [E, high]: Scheel, Schijen & Lakens, *AMPPS* 4(2), 2021 — 96% positive results in the standard psychology literature vs 44% in Registered Reports, i.e. accepting the plan before the work changes what gets reported. [E, med] Nosek et al., *PNAS* 2018 ("The preregistration revolution") for the mechanism. Caveat the family itself states: preregistration without an *independent* reviewer loses most of its force — here reviewer and author are one person plus an agent with no memory.
- **Collingwood's failure mode** [P, med]: a statement attached to the wrong question is misunderstood even when "true". The arrangement's "a question that only resembles it is not its home" is Collingwood's rule verbatim in spirit.
- **Lab-notebook failure** [P, high; GLP, FDA 21 CFR 11 still in force]: the notebook that is rewritten is worthless as evidence; the one that is only appended becomes unreadable without an index. The arrangement has both halves (append + rewritten summary). The known residual failure: the summary silently diverging from the log; GLP's answer is periodic witnessed review, which the arrangement's maintenance step resembles.

**(e) Apart from the typical member.** A research programme normally has no explicit *list* of what is core and what is belt; here the split is declared and installed. And the programme's own identity is an open question *inside the programme* — Lakatos would call that reflexive and would note it is where programme identity is hardest to judge.

**(f) Confidence.** Core/belt mapping: high. Anomaly-ledger mapping: high. Preregistration evidence: high (2021, confirmed). Programme-identity reading of part 13: med — it restates the designers' question in Lakatos's words rather than answering it.

#### Family 3 — Disciplined software process and continuous improvement (Humphrey's PSP/TSP, CMMI, Deming's PDSA, the Kanban Method)

**(a) Name and vocabulary.** Watts Humphrey, *A Discipline for Software Engineering* (1995): the *Personal Software Process* — a one-person process with scripts, forms, defect logs, postmortems, and the **Process Improvement Proposal (PIP)** form. CMMI (SEI 1990s–; v3.0 ISACA 2023): *maturity levels 1–5, process areas*, notably **Causal Analysis and Resolution** and **Organizational Performance Management** at level 5. Deming/Shewhart: *PDSA, standard work, kaizen, andon*. Anderson, *Kanban* (2010): *make process policies explicit, implement feedback loops, improve collaboratively and evolve experimentally (using models)*; Kanban Maturity Model (2018). Rother, *Toyota Kata* (2009).

**(b) Mapping.**
- Part 2's cycle = a PSP *process script* (planning → design → design review → code → compile → test → postmortem) with the postmortem = "bring the records back into agreement". "Observable completion criteria" = PSP's *exit criteria* on every script phase.
- Part 10's register = Humphrey's **PIP form**, almost line for line: what happened, which process step, proposed change, disposition. Its "recurrence strikes and counts" = PSP's defect-type standard and *defect log* counting recurrences by type. In CMMI vocabulary the whole register is **Causal Analysis and Resolution**, a maturity-level-5 practice.
- Part 4's two scales (the work vs. the way of working) = Deming's distinction between **operating the process** and **improving the process**; in Lean, *standard work* vs *kaizen*. The stop at the threshold = **andon**: the line stops and the person comes.
- Part 9 (every part of the apparatus declares its parts, moments, records, checks) = Kanban's "make policies explicit" and CMMI's *defined process* (level 3): every process has a documented purpose, inputs, entry/exit criteria, roles, measures.
- Part 11's economy rules = Lean *muda*; "a change costs what it weighs" = Deming's warning against tampering.
- Part 1 (reconstruct/record) = Kanban's *feedback loop cadences* (daily standup, replenishment, review), collapsed into a per-conversation cadence because the worker forgets.
- Part 3 (question tree): **no word**. PSP has a task list and an issue-tracking log, not a tree of questions; this is where the family is thinnest.
- Part 8 (documents inform; instructions direct): **no word** in PSP; CMMI has "process assets" but no doctrine of what may authorise.

**(c) Ladder.** PSP0–PSP3: the arrangement is at **PSP3-equivalent** (cyclic development, design templates, process improvement proposals in use) but without PSP's *measurement* core — no size/time/defect estimation, no earned value, no statistical control. In CMMI terms it practises level-5 behaviours (causal analysis of process failure) without level-4 (quantitative management) — the family calls this an *inversion* and is suspicious of it. On the Kanban Maturity Model it is around ML3 ("fit for purpose": explicit policies, feedback loops) without the flow metrics that define ML4.

**(d) Failure knowledge.**
- **PSP adoption decay** [E, med; Humphrey's own data (Hayes & Over, SEI 1997) showed gains in training, but the field found engineers abandon PSP forms within months without external enforcement — Johnson et al., "Beyond the Personal Software Process" (ICSE 2003) attribute it to data-collection overhead and built Hackystat to automate collection]. The lesson: *disciplined personal process survives only when collection is automated*. The arrangement's "a script carries an instruction out" is the Hackystat move. PSP itself is **fading** (SEI certification wound down in the 2010s; still taught in a few universities) — mark as a dying practice whose finding outlived it.
- **Process ossification / cargo cult** [P, high]: standard work followed after its reason has gone; Lean's answer is that every standard carries its reason and a date; the arrangement's "three files: instruction, why, evidence" is that answer.
- **Tampering** [P, high; Deming's funnel experiment, still taught]: adjusting the process on every deviation increases variance. The register's "count the recurrence rather than file twice" and "what to do about a rule that keeps failing is left open" are anti-tampering by design; but amending on a *first* failure (part 10 proposes an amendment on each entry) is what Deming warned against.
- **Level-5 without level-4** [P, med; CMMI appraisal practice]: causal analysis without measurement degenerates into anecdote. The arrangement has counts (strike counts, recurrence counts) but no baselines.
- **Kanban's "evolve experimentally"** [P, high]: a policy change is a hypothesis with an expected effect and a review date; the arrangement has the hypothesis (proposed amendment) but the description gives it no expected effect or review date — Kanban would call that an unfalsifiable change.

**(e) Apart.** PSP assumed the person *is* the process executor; here the executor is an amnesiac agent and the person is the quality gate. The forms are therefore read by a different party than fills them, which is Humphrey's TSP (team) shape with one human and one non-human. Second: PSP measured; this does not.

**(f) Confidence.** PIP mapping: high. Andon/two-scale mapping: high. PSP decay evidence: med (sources 1997–2003; practice dying; the finding about collection overhead is still used to decide tooling). Level-inversion claim: med.

#### Family 4 — Human supervisory control and levels of automation (Sheridan–Verplank, Parasuraman, Billings, Bainbridge, Dekker; mission command)

**(a) Name and vocabulary.** *Levels of automation* (Sheridan & Verplank 1978, ten levels; Parasuraman, Sheridan & Wickens 2000, four stages × levels); *management by consent* vs *management by exception* (Billings, *Aviation Automation*, 1997); *ironies of automation* (Bainbridge 1983); *out-of-the-loop performance problem* (Endsley & Kiris 1995); *automation complacency and bias* (Parasuraman & Manzey 2010); *procedural drift* / *practical drift* (Snook 2000; Dekker 2003, 2011); *mission command / Auftragstaktik*: *commander's intent, decision points, delegated authority*; Appelo's *delegation poker* (Management 3.0, 2010): seven levels from Tell to Delegate.

**(b) Mapping.**
- Part 4 is a textbook **levels-of-automation** scheme: below threshold = Sheridan level 10 (computer acts, informs if it decides to); at threshold = **management by consent** or **by exception** depending on whether "shows before acting" waits for a yes (the description is ambiguous; Billings would insist the two are different machines); above threshold = Sheridan level 3–4 (computer suggests, human decides). The *two scales* = Parasuraman's point that LOA should be set per function, not globally. "The assistant may never change the threshold" = the human-centred-automation axiom that *the automation must not change its own authority* (Billings' principles).
- Part 2's "returns to a structured interview when a step meets a decision" = **decision points** in mission command, pre-identified moments where authority reverts.
- Part 1's reconstruct-then-record = **shift handover** (treated at length in Family 6).
- Part 5's reply form = the **status display / mode annunciation** problem: the operator must always know *what mode the automation is in and what it will do next* (Sarter & Woods, "mode confusion", 1995). "Which step, what was decided, what is next, where it stops" is a mode annunciator written in prose.
- Part 8 (documents inform; only the person authorises) = the **authority–responsibility** principle: authority may not be delegated to an artefact.
- Part 10's register = the *procedures-versus-practice gap* literature (Dekker 2003): the documented procedure and the enacted one diverge; most of the family's accident reports are about this.
- Part 3 (question tree), part 6 (what goes to documents), part 12 (portability): **no word**.

**(c) Ladder.** Parasuraman et al. 2000 recommend high automation for *information acquisition and analysis* and lower for *decision selection and action implementation*. The arrangement is at high automation for acquisition ("looks facts up rather than asking") and *tunable* for decision and action — the recommended profile. In Billings' terms it is **management by consent** at the dial and **management by exception** below it. In Appelo's delegation levels: below the dial = level 7 (Delegate), at = level 4–5 (Agree/Advise), above = level 2–3 (Sell/Consult).

**(d) Failure knowledge.**
- **Bainbridge's ironies** [P, high; 1983, among the most-cited papers in human factors, still assigned]: automate the routine and the human is left with the hardest cases while deskilled and under-practised; the human asked to monitor is bad at monitoring. Prediction for this arrangement: the person, shown decisions only at and above the dial, loses the context to judge them well.
- **Automation bias / complacency** [E, high; Mosier & Skitka 1998; Parasuraman & Manzey 2010 meta-review; replicated in medical decision support, e.g. Goddard et al. 2012]: humans accept a system's recommendation at rates that rise with its reliability and with workload. The arrangement's "with a recommendation and the alternatives" is exactly the format in which the recommendation is over-accepted. The family's mitigations: present alternatives *before* the recommendation, require the human to state a reason, vary the presentation — none of which the description mentions.
- **Mode confusion** [E, high; Sarter & Woods 1995; multiple air accidents]: the operator does not know what the automation is doing. The fixed reply form is the mitigation; the family would ask whether the reply states the *level* in force (it states the step and the stop, which is close).
- **Procedural drift** [P, high; Dekker 2003 "Failure to adapt or adaptations that fail", Snook 2000]: local adaptations accumulate until the written procedure and the practice are strangers; the organisation learns of it in an accident. The register (part 10) is a drift detector; the family's finding is that drift detectors fed by self-report under-report, because the adapter does not see the adaptation as a deviation. Here the reporter is the agent, which cannot see its own slip unless the rule's wording made the slip visible — a circularity the family would name.
- **Mission command's known failure** [P, med; still doctrine, ADP 6-0 2019]: intent stated too abstractly produces paralysis or improvisation; too concretely, it collapses into detailed orders. The two scales' rung names ("what one piece of work delivers", "a structural decision with wide blast radius") are intent at the abstract end — the family would predict boundary disputes about which rung a decision sits on, and notes the arrangement already has a tie-break rule ("a case between two rungs takes the higher"), which is the doctrine's own rule.

**(e) Apart.** In the aviation/nuclear family the automation is deterministic and the human is continuous; here the automation is stochastic and the human is intermittent across months — the ironies run both ways (the agent, too, arrives out-of-the-loop every time). The family has no literature on an *amnesiac* automation; its nearest is crew change in a long-duration operation.

**(f) Confidence.** LOA mapping: high. Automation-bias evidence: high (1998–2012, still replicated). Procedural-drift circularity claim: med (inference, not observed).

#### Family 5 — Constitutional and legal order; governance of norms (Hart, Fuller, entrenchment; delegation-of-authority and maker–checker control; RFC/PEP change processes)

**(a) Name and vocabulary.** Hart, *The Concept of Law* (1961): *primary vs secondary rules, rule of recognition, rules of change, rules of adjudication*. Fuller, *The Morality of Law* (1964): the eight ways to fail to make law. Bryce (1901): *flexible vs rigid constitutions*; *entrenched clauses*; *incorporation by reference*; *travaux préparatoires*. Corporate governance: *delegation-of-authority matrix, approval limits, four-eyes / maker–checker, segregation of duties, audit trail* (COSO 2013). Open-source governance: *RFC process* (IETF RFC 2026, 1996), *PEP* (PEP 1, 2000), Rust RFCs (2014); *BDFL*, *steering council*; *bikeshedding* (Kamp 1999).

**(b) Mapping.**
- Part 8 = Hart's **rule of recognition**: what counts as a valid instruction is settled by its *source* (the person, or an instruction file reached by the installer), not by its content. Text in a document that directs on its own is an *ultra vires* norm — reported as a defect. This is the sharpest legal mapping in the whole description.
- Part 4's two scales = Hart's **primary rules** (the work) and **rules of change** (how the way of working is amended). "The assistant may never change the threshold" = an **entrenched clause** whose amendment lies outside the ordinary amending power. The arrangement is a **rigid constitution** in Bryce's sense.
- Part 9 (instruction / rationale / evidence as three files) = **statute / explanatory memorandum / legislative record**; "installed blocks never edited by hand" = **incorporation by reference**; "rules in one file" = **codification**.
- Part 7 (provisional text bound to a question) = a **sunset clause** keyed to an event rather than a date; "followed until due" = law in force pending review.
- Part 10 = Fuller's eighth failure: **incongruence between rules as announced and as administered**; the register is a congruence audit.
- Part 2's "nothing is built before an accepted written statement" = the **RFC/PEP acceptance gate**: design accepted before implementation; the one-person acceptor = **BDFL**.
- Part 4's at-the-dial behaviour = **maker–checker**; the above-the-dial behaviour = **approval limit exceeded, escalate**; the whole of part 4 = a **delegation-of-authority matrix** with two columns.
- "Do not reopen an accepted decision without new evidence" = *res judicata* / *stare decisis* with the "new evidence" exception.
- Part 3 (question tree), part 1 (reconstruct/record), part 12 (portability) have **no word** here — the law has dockets and registers but not a tree of open questions as its map.

**(c) Ladder.** Hart's ladder: a *pre-legal* order (primary rules only) → a *legal system* (secondary rules present). The arrangement has all three secondary rules (recognition: part 8; change: the self-improvement scale and the controlled amendment process; adjudication: the person at the dial) — a full legal system in miniature, with a rigid constitution and a single judge-legislator. On the open-source ladder: **BDFL with an RFC process**, pre-steering-council.

**(d) Failure knowledge.**
- **Fuller's eight ways** [P, high; 1964, still taught in every jurisprudence course]: notably (5) contradictory rules, (7) too-frequent change, (8) announced/administered incongruence. The arrangement has explicit machinery for (8) and (5) ("records found out of agreement") and a cost rule against (7) ("a change costs what it weighs"). Fuller's own point: these fail *together* — fixing incongruence by amending the rule produces frequent change.
- **Rules vs standards** [P, high; Kaplow 1992]: rules are cheap to apply and expensive to write and over/under-inclusive; standards the reverse. The register's "a wording or boundary defect, not an execution slip" diagnoses every failure as a rule-writing failure, which is the rules-side bias Kaplow names; the family's alternative diagnosis — the rule was fine, the occasion was outside any rule — is ruled out by the register's own form, except via the "slips no rule covers, propose no rule" entry.
- **Regulatory accretion / legalism** [P, high; Weber's iron cage; Merton 1940 "goal displacement"; Graeber 2015]: rules accumulate because deletion needs a champion and addition needs only an incident. The register "nothing authorises deleting a rule" removes the one organ that would prune; the economy rules ask for pruning but assign it to no process. #### Family 6 — Organisational learning, routines and handover (Argyris & Schön; Feldman & Pentland; shift handover and clinical handoff)

**(a) Name and vocabulary.** Argyris & Schön, *Organizational Learning* (1978): *single-loop, double-loop, deutero-learning; espoused theory vs theory-in-use; defensive routines*. Handover: *shift handover* (process industries; Cullen Report on Piper Alpha, 1990), *clinical handoff* (I-PASS, SBAR), *organisational memory loss*.

**(b) Mapping.**

- Part 1 = **shift handover**: the outgoing shift writes state; the incoming shift reconstructs from it; the hazard is the gap. Every conversation here is a shift with total crew replacement.
- Part 3's "each conversation is registered with the question it stands on; collisions reported, not resolved" = the **permit-to-work** board in process plants: who is working on what, so two crews do not act on the same equipment.
- Part 9's declared apparatus = **ostensive routine made explicit** (the routine's description written down, with its artefacts).
- Part 4 (autonomy), part 6/7 (documents, provisional text), part 11: **no word** beyond generic "organisational rules".

**(c) Ladder.** Argyris's: Model I organisation (single-loop, defensive) → Model II (double-loop, public testing of assumptions). The arrangement is **Model II by design**: assumptions (decisions) are public, testable, with the refused alternative recorded. On the handover ladder (Lardner, HSE 1996): verbal only → written log → structured written + verbal with read-back. The arrangement is at *structured written* without read-back (no one confirms the incoming conversation read correctly) — the family would flag the missing read-back.

**(d) Failure knowledge.**
- **Handover failure kills** [E, high]: Piper Alpha (1988, 167 dead) — the Cullen Report (1990) found the permit-to-work for the removed pressure-safety valve was not communicated at shift handover. HSE guidance (Lardner 1996) still cites it. **Structured handoff works** [E, high]: Starmer et al., *NEJM* 2014 — I-PASS reduced medical errors 23% and preventable adverse events 30% across nine sites. The elements that mattered: fixed order, written + verbal, synthesis by receiver (read-back). The arrangement has fixed order and written; lacks receiver synthesis checked by the sender (impossible when the sender is gone — the family has no answer for a sender who no longer exists).
- **Defensive routines** [P, high; Argyris, still taught]: organisations learn to protect the rule rather than examine it; double-loop learning is espoused and not practised. The register's own form (every failure a wording defect) is a candidate defensive routine: it makes the agent's slip a text's fault.
- **Ostensive drift** [P, high; Feldman & Pentland 2003, heavily cited]: the written routine lags enacted practice; the enacted practice is the real routine. Remedy: treat the written routine as a *hypothesis* and periodically re-derive it from enactment. The arrangement's maintenance step does the reverse (brings records to match the change), which is right for artefacts and incomplete for routines.
- **Organisational memory loss on turnover** [P, high; Nelson & Winter; Argote 1999]: knowledge in people leaves with them; knowledge in routines and artefacts stays but decays as context is lost. Here turnover is 100% per conversation — the arrangement is the limiting case the literature extrapolates to but has never studied.

**(e) Apart.** The double loop is run by the party whose practice is in question (the agent registers its own non-compliance). In organisations the second loop is usually a *different* body (audit, retrospective facilitator). Also: the organisation here has exactly two members and one of them forgets everything.

**(f) Confidence.** Single/double-loop mapping: high. Handover mapping: high. I-PASS evidence: high (2014; still the reference trial). Piper Alpha: high.

### What the account leaves out

#### 1. Three roots, not one — and they are not peers
**Parts:** 3 (three fixed roots: purpose, construction, surroundings), 4 (the top rung of the development scale is "what the project is for"), 12 (the arrangement's own purpose "held open in its own tree").
**Tradition:** Organisational cybernetics — Beer's Viable System Model (Beer 1972/1979/1985; Espejo & Reyes 2011).
**Its words:** The account names one question, "how is this project built", and gives it the cycle. Beer would say that is System 1 (operations) alone. "What the project is for" is System 5 (identity/policy); "what surroundings it evolves in" is System 4 (intelligence, the outside-and-then); the verify/maintain steps are System 3/3* (control and audit); the registration of concurrent conversations with collision reporting is System 2 (anti-oscillation coordination). These are not three parallel question-cycle-records bundles; they are the metasystem that makes the operational one viable. The autonomy threshold (part 4) is exactly Beer's "resource bargain" between System 3 and System 1 — how much autonomy operations get before escalation.
**Why it matters:** The account's "each standing question has a cycle of its own" predicts that root 1 and root 3 should each have a cycle. The description gives them none — they are served by the interview and by recall. Beer explains why: metasystemic functions do not run production cycles. Part 13 (a recipient grew a second cycle) is VSM recursion — each System 1 contains a whole VSM — not "a second bundle in the same tree".
**Confidence:** high that the account mis-sorts the roots; medium that VSM's five-system split is the right carving.
**PRINCIPLE** (VSM still taught and applied in organisational design; not dead).

#### 2. The arrangement is a three-level rule system; the account describes only the lowest level
**Parts:** 4 (two scales; the threshold the assistant may never change), 8 (authorization only from the person), 9 (declared parts, owned rule files), 10 (amendments land through the same controlled process), 12 (local overrides beside the entry file).
**Tradition:** Institutional analysis — Ostrom's IAD framework (Ostrom 1990, 2005).
**Its words:** Ostrom distinguishes operational rules (what actors do day to day), collective-choice rules (who may change operational rules, and how), and constitutional rules (who may change the collective-choice rules). The development scale is operational-level decision scope; the self-improvement scale is the collective-choice level; "the assistant may never change the threshold" and "authorization comes only from the person" are the constitutional level, reserved entirely to one party with no ladder at all. Ostrom would also name part 10 as the gap between rules-in-form and rules-in-use, which in her tradition is the diagnostic of an institution, not an appendix to it.
**Why it matters:** An account that says "question + cycle + records, the rest is plumbing" treats the collective-choice and constitutional levels as plumbing. For Ostrom the institution *is* the nested configuration of the three levels; the question-cycle is merely the "action situation" the rules structure. Two installs with the same cycle and different thresholds are, on her account, different institutions.
**Confidence:** high.
**PRINCIPLE** (IAD remains a live framework in commons and governance research).

#### 3. Two loops, not one — the second loop is the distinctive thing
**Parts:** 4 (the second scale), 10 (register of rules that did not fire; "why the rule did not fire — a wording defect, not an execution slip"), 11 (economy rules as governing values), 12 (self-application).
**Tradition:** Organisational learning — Argyris & Schön (1978, 1996): single- and double-loop learning; espoused theory vs theory-in-use.
**Its words:** The cycle in part 2 is single-loop: detect error, correct within the governing variables. Part 10 is double-loop: the governing variables (the rules themselves, their wording, their boundaries) are questioned when they fail. Insisting that a non-fire is a wording or boundary defect, not an execution slip, is Argyris's refusal to blame the actor and his demand to examine the theory-in-use. The economy rules (part 11) are governing variables in the strict sense.
**Why it matters:** The account's bundle has one loop. The arrangement's claim to be more than a procedure rests on the second. Toyota Kata (Rother 2009) says the same from industry: the improvement kata, not the production cycle, is what is coached and what travels.
**Confidence:** high.
**PRINCIPLE** (Argyris & Schön still core in management education and practice).

#### 4. The records are the agent's memory, not the cycle's output
**Parts:** 1 (every conversation opens remembering nothing; reconstruct from records alone), 3 (question records as "standing summary for a reader arriving cold"), 5 (fixed reply form), 9 (what every conversation reads is kept small, since every conversation pays for it).
**Tradition:** Distributed cognition / extended mind (Hutchins 1995; Clark & Chalmers 1998); transactive memory (Wegner 1987).
**Its words:** The cognitive system is person + records + assistant, and the assistant is a component that is re-instantiated each episode. The records are not kept *by* the cycle; the cycle is reconstructed *from* the records. In Hutchins's terms the question records, the decision tables and the reply form are representational states propagated across media; the reply form is the coordinating artefact between a stateless component and a stateful one. The cost of the always-read layer (part 9) is the system's working-memory budget.
**Why it matters:** The account's possessive — "the records that cycle keeps" — inverts the dependency the description states. It also has no term for episodic amnesia or for concurrency (part 3's registration of conversations), both of which are first-order facts of this agent and nothing like the situation of a human team keeping minutes.
**Confidence:** high on the inversion; medium on whether extended-mind vocabulary adds beyond "the records are the substrate".
**EVIDENCE** for the budget claim: long-context degradation and the cost of per-turn context are documented (Liu et al., "Lost in the Middle", TACL 2024); current agent frameworks instruct keeping always-loaded instruction files small (vendor documentation for coding agents, 2025–2026). **PRINCIPLE** for the rest.

#### 5. The arrangement is first a rule of recognition — who may author a norm
**Parts:** 8 (documents inform, instructions direct; a document that would direct on its own is a defect), 9 (blocks copied in by an installer, marked as owned, never hand-edited), 4 (entrenched threshold), 10 (nothing authorizes deleting a rule; what to do about a rule that keeps failing is left open).
**Tradition:** Analytic jurisprudence and constitutional theory — Hart's rule of recognition (Hart 1961); entrenchment and amendment clauses.
**Its words:** Part 8 is a rule of recognition: a text has normative force only through a validated channel (an instruction that reads it), never through its content. Part 9's ownership marks are provenance of enactment. Part 4's unchangeable threshold is an entrenched clause. Part 10's refusal to let the register authorize deletion, and its deliberate silence on persistent failure, is a constitution that withholds a repeal power from the amending body. Hart would also note that the person is the sole sovereign source of authorization and the assistant has delegated, bounded competence.
**Why it matters:** The account is silent on who decides anything. "Question, cycle, records" could describe a solo researcher's notebook. The description's load-bearing content is the allocation of authority between two radically asymmetric parties, and the defence against texts that impersonate authority — which, for a language-model agent, is the prompt-injection problem in constitutional dress.
**Confidence:** high.
**PRINCIPLE** (Hart is still the reference point in jurisprudence). **EVIDENCE** that document-borne instructions are a live threat to such agents: prompt-injection via tool-returned content is a recognised attack class in security guidance for LLM agents (OWASP LLM Top 10, 2023–2025 editions).

#### 6. Hard core and protective belt — identity sits in what is held, not in the question
**Parts:** 12 (held layer: cycle, stops, tree, reply form; swappable layer: tests, architecture docs), 7 (provisional text bound to a question; contradiction not counted as violation), 13 (a second cycle grown in a recipient), 12 (the arrangement develops itself by its own cycle).
**Tradition:** Philosophy of science — Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes (1970); Laudan's research traditions (1977).
**Its words:** What identifies a programme is its hard core (held, not open to refutation) and its positive heuristic (how it extends itself); the protective belt is revised freely. The description's held layer is the hard core, the swappable layer the belt, and the straw-dog mechanism (part 7) is the protective belt made explicit: provisional auxiliary text whose revision is anticipated and not counted as a failure. Part 13's second cycle is then a problem-shift inside the same programme if the hard core held, and a rival programme if it did not — the designers' question "what are they holding" has a Lakatosian test.
**Why it matters:** The account puts the identity in the question. Lakatos puts it in what is refused revision. By the account, two trees with the same question but different held layers are the same kind of thing; by Lakatos they are different programmes.
**Confidence:** medium-high.
**PRINCIPLE** (Lakatos still taught; actively used in economic methodology). Note that Kuhn's paradigms, the obvious neighbour, are refused below.

#### 7. The records are a controller's process model; drift is the failure mode
**Parts:** 1 (reconstruct), 2 (records brought back into agreement with what changed), 5 (table of records found out of agreement), 10 (register), 3 (collision between conversations reported, not resolved).
**Tradition:** Systems safety — Leveson's STAMP/STPA (Leveson 2011; STPA Handbook 2018); Dekker and Hollnagel on "human error" as a systemic symptom.
**Its words:** The arrangement is a hierarchical control structure: the person and the assistant are controllers, the project is the controlled process, the records are each controller's process model, the reply form is the feedback channel, the thresholds are control constraints. Leveson's central claim is that losses arise when a controller's process model diverges from the process; the description's "drift" table, the maintain step, and the reconstruction at each conversation are process-model repair. Part 10's rule — a non-fire is a wording defect, not an execution slip — is Dekker's "new view" of error stated as policy. Concurrency with collision reporting is the classic two-controllers-one-process hazard, handled by detection rather than by locking.
**Why it matters:** "Question, cycle, records" has no failure theory. The description is dense with one.
**Confidence:** medium-high.
**PRINCIPLE** (STPA in active use in aerospace, automotive, medical).

#### 8. Delegation under asymmetric information
**Parts:** 4 (below/at/above the threshold), 2 (interview: one decision at a time, recommendation plus alternatives, facts looked up not asked), 5 (fixed reporting form; machinery hidden unless debugging).
**Tradition:** Principal–agent and delegation theory (Holmström 1977/1984 on interval delegation; Alonso & Matouschek 2008).
**Its words:** The threshold is a delegation set; the "shows before acting" rung is a ratification requirement; the reply form is a mandated report. The interview's "look facts up rather than ask" shifts information-acquisition cost to the agent and keeps the principal's attention for preference-revealing choices. Holmström's result — that under misaligned or uncertain agent judgement, an interval delegation beats both full delegation and no delegation — is the economic argument for a dial rather than a switch.
**Why it matters:** The account treats the threshold as plumbing; delegation theory treats the shape of the delegation set as the whole design problem.
**Confidence:** medium — the mapping holds, but the theory has no place for the question tree except as the state space over which delegation is defined.
**PRINCIPLE** (still taught and used in contract and organisation economics).

#### 9. Argument structure: positions and refused alternatives are the record, not metadata
**Parts:** 3 (each decision with who, when, and what alternative it refused; dated evidence appended never rewritten; closure with a kind), 6 (decisions written as reasons not facts), 2 (recommendation with alternatives).
**Tradition:** Issue-Based Information Systems and design rationale (Kunz & Rittel 1970; Rittel & Webber 1973; MacLean et al.'s QOC 1991; Conklin 2005).
**Its words:** An issue map is issues, positions, and arguments for and against; a decision is a position that won and the record of why the others lost. The account says "records"; IBIS says the records are an argument structure, and the refused alternative is as much the content as the taken one. Rittel's wicked-problem thesis adds: a problem of this kind has no stopping rule and every attempt counts — the "struck count" on each question (part 3) is a Rittelian measure, and a *fixed* sequence of steps (part 2) is a tame-problem assumption his tradition would flag.
**Why it matters:** "The records that cycle keeps" says nothing about their logical form. Decision-record practice in software (Nygard's ADRs, 2011) independently converged on the same point: record the why and the rejected options, or the record is useless to a later reader.
**Confidence:** high.
**PRINCIPLE** (IBIS alive in dialogue mapping; ADRs in wide use).

### What each tradition would put at the centre instead

- **Beer (VSM):** the recursion of a viable system — an operational unit (the cycle) under a metasystem (purpose, environment, control, coordination), the whole repeated at each level of recursion. Part 13 is a recursion, not a second bundle.
- **Ostrom (IAD):** the nested rule configuration — operational, collective-choice, constitutional — with the action situation (the question-cycle) as what the rules shape. The identity is the rules, including who holds the constitutional level.
- **Argyris & Schön:** the double loop — a practice that inspects and revises its own governing variables, with an explicit apparatus for finding the gap between what it says it does and what it does.
- **Distributed cognition:** a composite cognitive system whose memory is external and whose most capable component is amnesic; the records and the reply form are the system's working memory and coordination medium.
- **Hart / constitutional theory:** a rule of recognition and an allocation of authority — who may author a norm, through what channel, and what is entrenched against the agent.
- **Lakatos:** a research programme identified by its hard core (the held layer) and positive heuristic (self-development), with the question tree as the belt where problem-shifts happen.
- **Leveson (STAMP):** a hierarchical control structure whose safety depends on keeping controllers' process models (the records) in agreement with the process, with detection of divergence built in.
- **Delegation theory:** a delegation contract — an interval of authority, a ratification rung, and a reporting requirement — designed under asymmetric information.
- **IBIS / design rationale:** an argumentative map of issues, positions and arguments, in which the cycle is one way positions become commitments.

These are not reconcilable into one centre; each makes a different part of the description primary and a different part derivative.

### What the account gets right

- **The question outranks the ticket.** A casual reading takes the cycle — units of work with completion criteria — as the organising unit. The description says units of work are cut from the body of the question they answer, and that decisions, evidence and the cold-reader summary live on the question. The account correctly subordinates the cycle to the question. Collingwood's logic of question and answer (1939) and Hintikka's interrogative model of inquiry (1999) hold the same: a proposition is intelligible only as an answer to a question, and inquiry is structured by a principal question with operational sub-questions — which is the tree.
- **The standing question never closes.** Rittel: wicked problems have no stopping rule. "Never deleted", roots fixed at the start, the arrangement's own purpose held open — the account's "standing" is the right word.
- **Different concerns need different cycles.** The account indexes cycles to questions, which is what part 13 shows. A reading that took the software cycle as the method would be wrong, and the account avoids it.
- **A genuinely shared layer exists.** Parts 9 and 12 (declaration, installer, local overrides) are cross-cutting and are not part of any one question's bundle. The account's "shared plumbing" is right about their position, if wrong about their weight.

### What the tasking smuggled in

- **"At bottom, IS"** asks for an essence. The traditions above divide on whether the thing has one; the question's form presupposes Aristotle's answer.
- **The account picks root 2 of three.** The description fixes three roots; the account names one as "the" standing question and says nothing of the other two. The choice was made before the testing began.
- **"Cycle"** imports closure and periodicity. The description's own part 2 is a loop that *returns to an interview whenever a step meets a decision* — a loop with an interrupt — and part 4 adds stops. "Cycle" hides the stops; the stops are where the person is.
- **"The records that cycle keeps"** makes records the cycle's property. Part 1 makes the cycle the records' dependent. The possessive decided the dependency direction.
- **The two-bin residual** — "shared plumbing or replaceable detail" — has no bin for constitutive constraints: who authorizes, what is entrenched, what may not be deleted. Every normative part (4, 8, 9, 10) falls into "plumbing" by construction.
- **Ordering and nouns.** The description leads with the cycle and the tree (longest, first) and names the normative parts as apparatus — "a threshold", "a register", "a fixed form" — rather than as what they are: an allocation of authority, a learning loop, a reporting contract. The salience ordering favours the candidate account.
- **"A person and an assistant work together"** is symmetric phrasing for an asymmetric arrangement. Every authority term in the description runs one way.
- **"Written functionally"** still carries values as facts: part 11 ("economy rules") is a value system presented as a part; "load-bearing" is the arrangement's own test presented as a definition.
- **The account is already shaped for part 13.** "Where a tree holds more than one such standing question, each has a cycle and records of its own" is a clause written to absorb the one anomaly; its presence shows the account was fitted to the case it was meant to be tested on.
- **The tasking's own frame** — "traditions outside", "what each would put at the centre" — assumes the arrangement has an inside and that every tradition has a centre. Actor-network theory would deny the second; it is refused below for a different reason.

### Nothing to take

- **Scrum/Agile, PDCA, Boehm's spiral, ISO 12207.** They map onto part 2 only and agree with the account about it. A slice, not an alternative account of the whole; and where they disagree (Boehm: the sequence should be risk-driven, not fixed) the point is already carried by Rittel above.
- **Kuhn's paradigms.** No statable mapping for the autonomy scales, the register, or the records' form. "Paradigm" is too coarse to say which part is anomalous; Lakatos does the same work with mappable terms.
- **Zettelkasten / personal knowledge management.** Maps onto the question tree's record form and nothing else. A slice.
- **Brandom's deontic scorekeeping (inferentialism).** Maps the reply form (claim + entitlement link), the decision tables (commitments undertaken), and the authorization rule (who may undertake them). No place for the cycle, the thresholds, the installer or self-application. Promising for parts 5 and 8 alone; not a whole account.
- **Actor-network theory.** Would map the installer, the owned blocks and the records as actants and inscriptions, but by design refuses to name a centre, so it cannot say what the account misses — only that asking is wrong. Not usable under the task's rule.
- **Bayesian epistemology / formal epistemology.** The tree and evidence could be mapped; nothing in it speaks to authority, thresholds or the two loops. A slice.
- **Dewey's pattern of inquiry (1938).** Mapping holds — indeterminate situation, institution of a problem, reasoning, operational facts — but it adds nothing beyond what Collingwood/Hintikka/Rittel already say about the question, and its one distinctive move (the "situation" precedes the question) is not carried by any described part.
