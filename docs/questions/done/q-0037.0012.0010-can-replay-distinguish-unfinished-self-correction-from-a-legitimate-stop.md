## Where it stands

The session acknowledged a failure of L9 and ended before the required registration and repair
proposal. An independent reader distinguished that omission from three legitimate stops in
[the recorded case](../q-0037.0013-how-does-each-turn-know-its-next-step-and-each-session-where-to-resume.md#evidence--distinguishing-the-stops-2026-10-26).

The authorised isolated prototype is implemented. Two fresh readers agreed with the seven saved
expectations: two returns, four legitimate stops and one unknown in each reading. The program
validated their case versions and exact quotations and reproduced their offline reports. It checks
evidence locations and identity, not semantic entailment; the reader supplies that judgement.
Unknown or invalid readings establish no permission to stop or continue.

The result is limited to supplied excerpts and separately authored controls. It establishes no
live-hook reliability or cause of the difference between hosts. The code, raw readings, test
results and limitations are in [the research record](../../research/repair-replay/README.md).

## Impact — 2026-10-26

### Impact

One research directory, this question and its ticket/RFC. The prototype exercises a reader's
verdict from a saved case through validation to a preview of the documented Stop output.

### Hidden edges

A supplied verdict is not a semantic check. Case labels must stay out of reader packets. A stale
verdict, fabricated quotation, missing review or uncertain context cannot become an allow.
The existing stop-hook-active flag bounds the preview; it does not establish completion.
Saved excerpts and independent reads cannot establish live hook reliability.

### Leave alone

Core scripts, host settings, the voice rule, the general completion-check work and another
session's end-to-end amendment-testing work.

### Recommendation

Proceed with one offline slice. No provider framework, new mechanism, shared ledger or live hook
is needed to test this distinction.
