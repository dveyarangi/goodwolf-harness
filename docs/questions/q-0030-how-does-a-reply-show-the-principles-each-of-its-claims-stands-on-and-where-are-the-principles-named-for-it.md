same way, as the tokens are generated — group or tag the paragraphs by the principles from the
list.* Three corrections shaped the draft: *if no fitting principle is found, a box with that

**Names.** Every principle carries a name at its authored home — in the entry file's general
section the bullet opens with it in bold, in a mechanism's doc and in the glossary the term is
the name — and is cited by that name and nothing else.

**Marks.** Under `debug=on`, a paragraph that claims or recommends opens with the marks it
earns, in this order:
- `💡 *<name>*` — the principle governing its reasoning, by its name; one, the one it is
  governed by, never every one that bears on it.
- `⚓ *<footing>*` — what it rests on: a decision, evidence or the user's word, named by its
  record or by who and when.
Where no principle fits, the paragraph is boxed instead of lamped — `> ⬜ no principle fits —
<what it rests on>` — and the box is the signal: a principle is missing from the list, or the
reasoning has none. A paragraph that links, narrates or restates carries no mark.

**Why two marks.** The lamp says how the thinking went; the anchor says what it stood on. Today's
three overturned recommendations each had an anchor — *no recorded failures*, *no recipient
asked* — and no lamp: the box would have shown that before the user did.

**The box is a blockquote** *(the user, 2026-10-07)*: it lives inside the text and breaks no
paragraph. **Open:** the handles themselves, named at the align that lands this with the sort of
records (q-0024.0009), one work.
