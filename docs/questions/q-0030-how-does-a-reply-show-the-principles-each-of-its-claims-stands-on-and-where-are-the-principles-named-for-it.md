## The instructions, agreed in draft 2026-10-07

Raised by the user after the emoji rows landed: *catch and highlight the governing principles the
same way, as the tokens are generated — group or tag the paragraphs by the principles from the
list.* Three corrections shaped the draft: *if no fitting principle is found, a box with that
instead of an anchor*; *the footing and the principle are separate things — principles govern a
higher level of thinking*; *the footing is a good idea too, and it fits the anchor; principles
take the lamp.*

**Names.** Every principle carries a name at its authored home — in the entry file's general
section the bullet opens with it in bold, in a mechanism's doc and in the glossary the term is
the name — and is cited by that name and nothing else.

**Marks.** Under `debug=on`, a paragraph that claims or recommends opens with the marks it
earns, in this order:
- `💡 *<name>*` — the principle governing its reasoning, by its name; one, the one it is
  governed by, never every one that bears on it.
- `⚓ *<footing>*` — what it rests on: a decision, evidence or the user's word, named by its
  record or by who and when.
Where no principle fits, the paragraph is boxed instead of lamped — `> ⬜ no principle fits —
<what it rests on>` — and the box is the signal: a principle is missing from the list, or the
reasoning has none. A paragraph that links, narrates or restates carries no mark.

**Why two marks.** The lamp says how the thinking went; the anchor says what it stood on. Today's
three overturned recommendations each had an anchor — *no recorded failures*, *no recipient
asked* — and no lamp: the box would have shown that before the user did.

**What a principle is** *(the user, 2026-10-07)*: *an instruction tells the agent what exactly
to do; a principle says what to prefer when it is unclear what to do.* So a principle is a
preference under uncertainty, and the test for one is that it can be written as *prefer A over
B*. A first listing of sixty-nine candidates fell to about forty by the glossary's test (no
moment, no outcome) and to fewer by this one: what remains outside are instructions, invariants
of the method (*every refusal writes nothing*) and definitions (*load-bearing*, *the whiteboard*),
which are records of other kinds and take no lamp. The glossary's entry is rewritten to this at
the align.

**Classifications** *(raised by the user, 2026-10-07)*: a sort with an exhaustiveness claim and
a test — the kinds of record by source of truth, the five kinds of turn, the four kinds of
moment, the six closure kinds, the three tiers, the kinds of written statement this very sift
produced. Neither a preference nor an instruction: a classification says where a thing goes when
it is unclear what it is, as a principle says what to prefer when it is unclear what to do. A
claim like *a description of the environment is of the third kind, so it is rendered* stands on
one, and neither the lamp nor the anchor names it. Those a script reads (closure kinds, statuses)
are mechanical and need no handle; those the agent sorts by hand (kinds of record, of turn, of
statement) need one. Open: a glyph of their own, or under the lamp.

**The box is a blockquote** *(the user, 2026-10-07)*: it lives inside the text and breaks no
paragraph. **Open:** the handles themselves, named at the align that lands this with the sort of
records (q-0024.0009), one work.
