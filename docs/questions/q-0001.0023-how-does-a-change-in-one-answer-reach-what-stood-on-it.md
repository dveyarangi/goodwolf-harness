Raised by q-0001.0022, 2026-10-09: an answer about the landscape a project evolves in can move
both its purpose and its structure, yet the landscape is never answered whole. The store carries
re-examination down *part of* and along *depends on*, and *depends on* also says a question cannot

The user, 2026-10-09: *rests on* is a good option and wants formalizing. The draft, for /align
before anything is built:

- **The part.** `rests on` on entry A, a list of ids, like `depends on`.
- **What it means.** A's answer — or its lean, while A is open — holds only while B's answer, or
  B's lean while B is open, stands. Validity, not order. Beside the other two: *part of*, A is a
  piece of B; *depends on*, A cannot be asked until B is answered; *rests on*, A can be asked and
  answered at any time, and its answer is conditional on B's.
- **Never blocks, cycles allowed.** Nothing is ordered along it, so purpose and landscape may rest
  on each other.
- **Who writes it.** The agent, by a call `rest q-A --on q-B` and `unrest q-A --on q-B`, in the
  turn that writes an answer or a lean of A taking B's as given. A judgement made at answering,
  never seeded.
- **When it fires.** B closes, B is closed again with another answer, or B is marked suspect. A
  reword fires nothing: the same question means the same answers.
- **The rule, the agent's.** Read each question resting on B and mark suspect the ones whose
  assumption broke. One step: a question marked suspect passes it on only when its own answer,
  re-read, changes.
- **The script's half, mechanical.** Each call that touches B's answer prints the questions
  resting on B, so the reading cannot be skipped; a move renames ids in `rests on` as in
  `depends on`; the check reports a closed A resting on a B closed superseded, pruned or moot, A
  not suspect — the ground went away under it. A resting on an open B is normal and not reported.
- **Readers.** The agent at the call's output; the check; the window, which shows nothing new.

The user, 2026-10-09, on the draft: the relation is made toward an answer, not a question — then
what are its two ends, and how is it not *depends on*? Taken: the ends are answers. A question
assumes nothing; its answer, or its lean while it is open, takes another question's answer or
lean as given. So the draft above is corrected:

- **Two relations between questions, one between answers.** *Part of* and *depends on* join
  questions and exist before any answer: *depends on* says A cannot even be asked well until B is
  answered — which database, before what data is kept — and so blocks. *Assumes* joins answers,
  is born when an answer or lean is written, blocks nothing, and dies with the answer that made
  it: a replaced answer states its assumptions afresh, so no stale link outlives it.
- **Pinned to the answer it took.** The link records which of B's answers A's took — a mark of
  B's answer or lean as it stood — so the script, not the agent's memory, finds every A whose
  pinned B has since changed. Whether the assumption broke stays the agent's judgement.
- **The word.** *assumes*, since its subject is an answer; *rests on* fitted questions.

What *depends on* already carries stays: the check reports a closed A whose dependency is open,
so a dependency is also an assumption of A's answer by construction.

The user, 2026-10-09: *which database* depends on the answer to *what data do we keep*, not on
the question — so where is the difference? The table above was wrong at one end: both relations
point at B's answer, and they differ at A's end.

- **depends on: A's question presupposes B's answer.** Without it A's options are undefined —
  which databases are candidates is set by the data kept. A changed answer of B may make A the
  wrong question: reworded, split again, or moot, its answer falling with it. That is why it
  blocks: a question whose options are undefined cannot be answered.
- **assumes: A's answer takes B's answer as a premise.** A is well posed without it — *what is
  this project for?* can be asked with no landscape — but the option chosen was chosen on B's
  answer. A changed answer of B leaves A's question standing and puts its answer in doubt. Nothing
  blocks.
- **The test**, corrected by the user the same day: *which database?* already presupposes that the
  data needs one, so an answer to *what data do we keep?* can dissolve or transform it whatever its
  wording — the claim that a generic wording turns it into an assumption was wrong. Run B through
  its other possible answers. If any of them leaves A moot, ill posed or needing new words, A's
  question depends on B. If under every one of them A stands as worded and only its best answer
  may differ, A's answer assumes B's. Examples of the second: *which language is the service
  written in?* — Python, assuming *who is on the team?* is answered Python developers; a new team
  leaves the question standing and the choice in doubt. *What timeout does the client use?* —
  thirty seconds, assuming the upstream answers within ten; every client has a timeout whatever
  the upstream does. *What is this project for?* — a harness, assuming the landscape answer that
  agents now write code and people lose the lead; every project has a purpose whatever the
  landscape. A dependency always carries an assumption too; an assumption never a dependency.

The user, 2026-10-09, against the examples: *which language is the service written in?*
presupposes a service, and so does *what is this service for?* — every question presupposes
something; whether depends on or assumes holds feels like a matter of how set the project is.
Taken, and it unmakes the test: whether a change of B unmakes A's question or only moves A's
answer is decided by the change — how far B's answer moves — not by the link. One more developer
moves the language's answer; no team at all unmakes the service. At the moment the link is
written nobody knows which change will come. A presupposition nobody expects to move is the
ground, and nobody records it; as a project sets, presuppositions sink into the ground, and what
could unmake a question earlier can later only move an answer — the shift of weight
q-0001.0022.0001 asks after.

So the draft becomes: **one link for validity**, *assumes* — A's position, answer or lean, took
B's as given — written when A's position is written, for a B still live; **its consequence
judged at the change**, per A: untouched, its answer in doubt (suspect), or its question in doubt
(reword, split, moot). What is left of *depends on* that *assumes* lacks is order — ask A after
B — a separate concern from validity.
- **Lifetimes follow the ends**: a dependency lives with A's wording, an assumption with A's
  answer.

In the logic of questions these are a question's presupposition and an argument's premise; the
