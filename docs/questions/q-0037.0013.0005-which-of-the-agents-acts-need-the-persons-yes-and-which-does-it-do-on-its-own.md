## Where it stands

**The problem.** How far the agent goes alone is set by five switches in the entry file — `commit`,
`push`, `next-cycle`, `breakdown`, `repair` — each `ask` or `auto`. They grew one occasion at a
time and do not say what the person cares about. They contradict each other: the installing agent
must say where it stops for the person's yes, while `push` may be mentioned by `/conclude` alone.
They gate the two acts practitioners leave free — a local commit, a push to one's own branch — and
say nothing of joining the shared line. Four areas have no line at all: irreversible git
operations, the environment, other sessions' work, local decisions on how to build. The harness's
own rules change after the agent shows the text, without a yes.

**The shape agreed.** Autonomy is a **dial on a ladder of scale**, one ladder per area of work:

- at the dial the agent settles the decision and asks before acting on it;
- below it the agent settles and acts on its own;
- above it the agent settles nothing: it brings the options and a recommendation, the person
  decides.

A reply reports what was settled at the agent's own rung, describes the rung below, and is silent on
the lowest. A decision's rung is read from what it decides — the reach of its consequence — never
from the record that holds it; a case between two rungs takes the higher.

| ladder | rung | what a decision on it changes |
|---|---|---|
| development | code | how a feature is written, inside a plan, touching no contract |
| | a feature | what one piece of work delivers |
| | load-bearing | what several parts rely on |
| | a product slice | which answer is built next, and its shape |
| | the whole product | what the project is for |
| self-improvement | a wording | words, meaning unchanged |
| | a local rule | one project's own answer |
| | a core rule | one rule every recipient receives |
| | a skill's act | what a skill does, in what order |
| | a mechanism | its parts, moments and records |
| | the method | the rings, the principles, the entry contract |

Self-improvement's dial is at a core rule. Commit and push are not decisions on a ladder: work
closes with its commit, and push follows the project's way of working with git, which is the
environment's to propose (q-0037.0006.0022.0003). Mending a violation is the work of the rung the
mended thing is on; that it happened is shown under debugging, a mechanism of its own
(q-0037.0009.0021). An act off the ladder — `reset --hard`, a force-push, a message to a person, an
install outside the tree — is a security setting held by a mechanical check, never a rule; the dial
itself is among them.

**What changes.** The entry file's autonomy table and the switch names in its *Human checkpoints*
line give way to the ladders and the dial's meaning, with an entry-contract bump; a project's dials
live in its local file. The skills reading a switch — commit, conclude, implement, ticket, verify,
recall, questions, harness — read the dial instead. Product rows A13 and A14 are rewritten through
`/product`. A recipient's switches become its dial on update, the person asked only what has no
translation. The Self-improvement section reads its own dial.

**The work**, sliced and approved 2026-10-14, not yet minted: the agent asks at its dial
(01-0020.0010, the tracer, carrying the install's proposal); a reply reports by its dial
(01-0020.0020, against the reply form of 01-0011.0110.0020); a recipient's switches become its dial
on update (01-0010.0210); self-improvement's dial (01-0019.0010). The host's check of off-ladder
acts is 01-0010.0125's.

**How it is checked.** Telling a decision's rung is judgement, so it is tested on cases: real
decisions from this tree's history, each with the rung the person gives it, read by an agent without
the session's context and graded against the person's. The switches-to-dial translation is a pure
function, tested over every combination recipients hold. The host check is tested in both
polarities. An install into an empty folder is read for the dial it proposes and the stops its
introduction names.

**Open.** Whether the ladder also sets the path a change takes (q-0037.0013.0005.0003); the dial an

## Discovery, 2026-10-14

A separate pass, told not to read this tree, given the shape in no house terms: record (R), send
(S), join the shared releasable line (J); the person's two switches gate R and S. Its material, as
it came back, condensed only by dropping links it gave inline:

### Families

1. **Trunk-based, committing straight to trunk.** R and J are one act; S often. No person gates J:
   the author's discipline before R, CI detecting after S, fast repair or revert; unfinished work
   kept out by flags. Protects against late integration; accepts a bad change reaching the line.
   Ladder: direct commit is the small-team end, legitimate, not immature (medium-high). Fails:
   broken trunk blocks everyone, flags pile up, review vanishes or moves after the commit (high).
   DORA; trunkbaseddevelopment.com.
2. **Short-lived branch plus pull request (GitHub Flow).** R and S free on a personal branch, S
   being backup, sharing and CI trigger; J gated by PR, required checks, approvals, branch
   protection. Protects the shared line from unreviewed or untested change, leaves a record of why.
   Solo: self-merged PR, ceremony without protection. Fails: PRs grow large and long, review
   latency, rubber stamps, green-on-branch red-on-main. Two machines: the pushed personal branch is
   the usual carrier; the hazard is rewriting history on one and force-pushing over the other,
   `--force-with-lease` the mitigation (high).
3. **Ship / Show / Ask** (Wilsenach, 2021). The author picks per change: commit straight to main,
   PR merged at once with review after, or PR awaiting approval. R and S free. Fails: depends on
   honest classification, drifts to Ship under pressure; disagrees with family 2 that the author
   may judge their own change (medium).
4. **Pre-commit review of patches** (Gerrit, Phabricator, stacked diffs). The unit is the commit; S
   goes to a review queue, not a shared line; J only after approval and verification. Protects every
   commit on the line as reviewable and bisectable. Large-organisation tooling; a solo person is off
   its ladder. Fails: tooling overhead, restacking pain, reviewer availability (medium).
5. **Integration by robot** (the not-rocket-science rule, merge queues). No person performs J; a bot
   tests the candidate merge and advances main only if green. Protects main from ever being red,
   semantic conflicts included. For one person: latency for a race that rarely happens. Fails:
   slow or flaky tests stall it, emergency bypasses break it (medium-high).
6. **Release-branch models** (Git Flow). Adds a fourth movement: joining the integration line and
   joining the release line are separate. Protects what customers run from unstabilised work. Its
   own author (2020) puts continuously delivered software outside its range. Fails: long branches,
   forgotten back-merges, ceremony; anti-correlated with delivery performance (high).
7. **Maintainer-held integration** (fork and pull, emailed patches). Contributors R and S to their
   own copies; only maintainers perform J — the gate is identity, write access. Raises whether an
   assistant should be a contributor without write access to the line. Fails: maintainer
   bottleneck, bus factor of one (high).
8. **Checkpoint commits, curated before sharing** (Aider auto-commit, Jujutsu, local history). R
   continuous and automatic, undoable and private; the gate moves to curation before S or J.
   Protects against lost work and an assistant's wrong turn. Conflicts with families 1 and 3, where
   every checkpoint would land on the shared line. Fails: curation skipped, rewritten pushed
   history across machines, secrets captured before anyone looks (medium).

**When the one making changes is an assistant.** Hosted agents are kept off the shared line by
design: GitHub's Copilot coding agent works on its own branch, opens a draft PR, the requester
cannot be the approver, and CI does not run on its pushes until a human approves (high). Local
agents (Claude Code, Aider, Cursor) converge on R cheap and automatic, S gated, J through PR —
because S is the first act visible to others, triggers CI or deploys, and is hard to retract
(medium). Agent-specific failures: diffs too large to review, destructive git operations on the
agent's initiative, secrets committed, prompt injection reaching exfiltration once it can push
(medium). Families 1 and 3 let the author choose review; platforms never give an agent Ship.

### What the tasking smuggled in

1. Three movements as the right cut — family 6 needs four, family 8 makes R continuous, family 4's S
   reaches no shared copy, family 7's J is someone else's act.
2. A gate as prior agreement — families 1 and 5 protect after or beside the act: detection, revert,
   the bot's post-merge test.
3. Agreement as the mechanism — others use reversibility or identity.
4. **The two switches as the axes: none of the families puts its main gate on R or S; most put it
   on J. The switches gate the two acts the families leave free, and say nothing of J, which in
   the current arrangement happens silently inside R.**
5. The remote and the line others build on as one place — on a personal branch S touches no shared
   line; across two computers S is synchronisation, not publishing.
6. *A few others soon* invites choosing for a future team; family 1 holds the solo trunk a correct
   rung, not a stage.
7. One assistant for one person — worktrees and requester-cannot-approve bite only with several.
8. Release as a property of the shared line — family 6 denies it.

### Nothing to take

Refused for want of a structural mapping: environment branches (deployment promotion), monorepo
against polyrepo (how many lines, not how movements reach them), pair or ensemble programming
(changes who is present at R; it sits inside family 1). Agent-specific claims rest on practitioner
reports and vendor docs; no source addresses the solo-with-assistant case directly.

## Discovery of action boundaries, 2026-10-14

The user: *do another discovery on the boundaries of agents' actions — not necessarily in
development.* A third pass, given neither earlier one, told only that one party acts for another,
some acts alone and some after agreement, and asked how that line is drawn in any field. Its
material, as it came back, condensed:

### Families

1. **Grant by enumeration** — the actor asks unless the act is listed (special power of attorney,
   express authority, OAuth scopes, allowlists). Gaps filled by incidental authority and agency of
   necessity; the principal may ratify after. Gives predictability and audit; the list cannot
   foresee everything, tends to many small asks or an empty *general* grant; the written list
   drifts from the systems enforcing it. Machines: scope lists are the default control; no
   equivalent of incidental authority (H/M).
2. **Reservation by enumeration** — the actor acts unless the matter is reserved (reserved
   matters, denylists, subsidiarity). Speed, suits an absent principal; the unforeseen harm is
   allowed by default, a reserved result reached by unreserved steps — for machines, a forbidden
   delete done through an allowed script (M).
3. **Stake threshold** — the line follows how much is at risk or how hard the act is to reverse
   (spending tiers, one-way and two-way doors, pre-approved standard changes, risk-scaled consent
   in medicine). Unclear cases go to the higher tier. Fails by splitting under the limit, small acts
   adding up, reversibility misjudged. Machines act at volumes where aggregation is the main risk;
   agentic-AI governance puts approval on consequential or irreversible actions (Shavit et al.,
   2023) (M).
4. **Ends and means** — the principal decides goals and values, the actor decides how (ABA Model
   Rule 1.2: the client decides objectives and settlement, the lawyer the means after consulting;
   medicine's consent to the intervention, not the technique). Uses the actor's expertise and keeps
   the principal in charge; the line blurs (*Jones v. Barnes*), paternalism, consent as a signed
   form. Its central case is one client and one professional. Machines lack the licence and
   liability that enforce it (H/M).
5. **Intent (mission command)** — the principal gives purpose, end state and limits; the actor
   does whatever serves them, reports what the principal said it must know, asks when the intent
   no longer fits (ADP 6-0; Marquet). Adapts when contact is slow; fails on a badly stated or
   differently understood intent, and on recentralising once communication is good. For machines,
   reading intent as meant is the specification problem itself (H/M).
6. **Timing of the principal's say** — not yes or no but when: ask before, tell before and proceed
   unless vetoed, tell after, don't tell (lazy consensus; *I intend to…*; Sheridan level 6).
   Silence may be absence, not agreement; rubber-stamping, notice fatigue, a window shorter than
   the principal's reaction. Machine speed squeezes the window (M).
7. **Earned autonomy** — the line moves per activity with demonstrated reliability (entrustable
   professional activities; solo endorsements). Tracks competence; routine record does not predict
   rare hard cases; trust overshoots into complacency. One person and one actor is its natural
   case: Anthropic's 2026 study reports about 20% of new Claude Code users auto-approving against
   over 40% after 750 sessions, experienced users also interrupting more. A model update resets
   the identity the record was earned by (M).
8. **Function allocation and operating domain** — a level of automation per stage (gather,
   analyse, decide, act), or a declared domain the machine acts in and hands back outside (SAE
   J3016). Fails by the ironies of automation, mode confusion, the supervisor out of the loop. Most
   to say about handover, least about the principal's values (H).
9. **The conflict line (fiduciary)** — wide discretion, but wherever the actor's own interest
   touches the act, informed consent. Targets divergent interests; disclosure buried, the actor
   blind to its own conflict. A machine's interest includes its vendor's and operator's (H/M).
10. **The actor judges its own uncertainty** — nothing drawn in advance; it escalates when the case
    seems abnormal or it is unsure (tiered support, triage, learning to defer). Scales; fails by
    not knowing what it does not know — language models are documented as overconfident — and by
    defensive over-escalation (M).
11. **Role convention** — the line is what someone in the role customarily does (usual authority).
    No list needed; principal and actor learned different customs. Machines carry a statistical
    version of role norms, whose fit to this principal is open (L).

Across families, for machines: those enforced by sanctions (1, 4, 9, 11) lose force, the vendor a
hidden third party; those depending on time or attention (6, 7, 8) strain under machine speed and
volume.

### What the tasking smuggled in

Two sides — most families have more positions: tell before with a veto, tell after, ask a third
party, abstain, decline. One principal present, competent and knowing their mind — formal
authority drifts to whoever is informed, and the uninformed principal rubber-stamps (Aghion and
Tirole, 1997). Agreement as a clean yes — **asking costs the principal, and over-asking wears
consent down; it is not a safe default**. Acts as separate units — acts combine; several families
draw the line on outcomes, intent or states. One line drawn in advance by the parties — law,
custom and runtime judgement draw others, and real systems hold several that can disagree.
*Capable but fallible* makes error the axis, where agency theory's is divergent interest.

### Nothing to take

Refused as families: four-eyes and separation of duties (who gives the yes, not which acts need
one), RACI, delegation poker and Sheridan's ten levels (ladders inside 6, 7, 8), incentive
contracting. Low confidence: that narrowing is socially underused; that learned role norms work as
convention.
