## The prior corpus, 2026-10-22

At inception this tree's rows folder held 129 rows, 125 of them ended, the oldest from 2026-10-02:
no row had ever been removed, by the question format's own sentence — *kept by design, an ended
row lets a later wake offer to resume* — written for the single file and carried into the folder
unchanged. The wake printed every one into each session's context, 19 KB of it. Of the 125, 43
were desktop-app tags, reopenable by their conversation id; the rest were probes, Codex and
Cursor ids, and the carry-over from the single file. The removal after seven days is the prior
violation this mechanism clears: at the first wake after it landed, 2026-10-22, 108 rows went,
21 stayed, the oldest of them ended 2026-10-15, and the wake said nothing of it.

## Refused, 2026-10-22

- **A session as a host process.** The desktop app renames a conversation's process on every
  reopen (2026-10-09, 2026-10-10); a row keyed on it loses its position. A session is the chat,
  known by the steadiest id the host gives it.
- **The pacer taking `/recall`.** The pacer is method and owns no machinery (q-awqz6b); a
  session's two ends are the session's whatever method runs between them, and the method's rules
  reach them as installed blocks.
- **The rows kept in the question format.** A format file describes a record; an instruction on a
  session's work is not a format, and the row is not a question record (rule failure 58). The row
  is declared beside `/conclude`, and the folder moved under `docs/sessions/`.
- **The code left in the question script, declared as embedded.** A part is checked by the
  mechanism's name at its head, which a shared file cannot carry.
- **The ended row's time kept at a silent ending.** The seven days would then run from the last
  `at`, not the session's last message: a long session that never moved would be gone at the wake
  that ended it. The ending writes the time the session was last seen.
- **A declaration slice and a removal slice.** The removal is a moment of the mechanism; declared
  without it the doc would say *not yet* of a rule already decided. One ticket, four stages.

## What one session writes to another, tried before it landed — 2026-11-16

S10 was written from [a count of the messages between sessions](../research/inter-session-messages-2026-11-15.md)
and tried before it landed: ten situations drawn from the count's cases, put to two fresh readers
who saw only the rules a session would — one with S10, one with S9 and the commit rule alone — and
both sets of answers judged by a third against the count.

- **What it changed.** Five situations improved and none worsened. The clearest: a decision the
  person changed three times went to its owner once, settled, where the reader without the rule
  sent three; a guess of whose uncommitted lines stood in a file went as a question after git was
  read, where the other asked for them to be committed; an approval was passed on as the person's
  words and no authority. In five the readers did the same with the rule and without — no
  acknowledgement, a break told at once, nothing to an ended session.
- **Refused, and why.** Five wordings of the draft, each read two ways by its reader: *what it
  must act on* shut out questions, which the count found useful every time, so a question is
  named first; *before you leave the file* became *before your turn ends*; *took its lines*
  became *took its uncommitted lines*; *one message to a receiver* forbade the mend's hand-off
  after the break's notice, so *per occasion*; *where they are recorded* made a reader invent a
  record, so *if they are*.
- **Left out.** A change to a contract, a data form or a tool others read, told to every reader
  before it lands: the count has some eleven cases, the judge recommends a fifth bullet, its
  wording untried. Shared state — a hold while a suite runs, a broken main, who touched a file —
  is a record every session reads and no message, the shared state's to build.
- **What the trial cannot say.** One reader an arm, one run; answers on paper, not a session
  under a long context; the situations drawn from the cases the rule was written from; the
  person's three words shown at once, where a live session meets them a turn apart.

The whole rule is a straw dog while the record of who touched a file is not built: that record
will carry the file and the lines its first bullet asks a message for.

## The rule on messages reworded the day it landed — 2026-11-16

The person read S10 within the hour and found four gaps the trial had not: it took a row's word
that a session had ended, and a row reads so after three hours of silence, so a role's sleeping
session would be kept from what it must act on; it named two things not to send where the count
has four; it spoke of the sender alone; and it said nothing of what the person is told. The
trial could not have caught the first: its situations told the readers that *ended* means not
running.

Reworded on all four and tried again: a fresh reader with the rule alone, ten further
situations, most from the receiver's side and with sessions that hold a role beside one that
does not, judged by another against the count. The reader was right in all ten — it answered
what asked and what waited unsaid, sent nothing to thanks and to a note that asked for none,
wrote to a role's session whose row read ended and not to a one-task session's, said what it
waited for, and told the person of the work and not of the exchanges.

- **Refused, three wordings**, each permitting a second reader otherwise: *what the receiver
  reads from git or a record without you*, standing alone, covered an answer that happens to
  stand in a committed file, so it stands under *what nobody waits on*; *in a line* bounded a
  decision put to the person as well as a retelling, and left; *the rest is not retold* had no
  exception for a person who asks what a message said, so *unasked*.
- **Left out, untried.** That a role's sleeping session is written only what will still hold
  when it wakes: a notice of uncommitted work or of a break is overtaken before a sleeper reads
  it, and stale on arrival is the waste the count saw grow. Whether a message wakes a sleeping
  session or waits is the host's, and decides the cost.
- **What this trial cannot say.** One reader, one run, right at every collision, so the three
  defects are read from the words; the last paragraph has no ground truth in the count, which
  judged messages by what the receiving session did, never by what the person was told.
