## What the 2026-09-08 align refuted

**A ticket as a dossier.** The shelf licensed narrative sections *0..n* and *date anything that
changed after minting*, and the four closed tickets before this one ran 2,272 to 6,176 words, with
verification sections and delivery logs that the RFC and the session record already held. Asked
whether the dossier had done any work at this align, the answer was: the live ticket's routed
finding and its agenda, yes; no closed ticket's verification or struck history, no. A ticket now
holds what is actual at its stage.

**The stage read from the status.** Agreed at the align, refuted at `/plan`: the parent ticket is
`In progress` on its children's account, has no RFC, carries ten sections outside the shaped list,
and twenty files link into their anchors. The `Plan` bullet marks the moment a ticket was actually
aligned and planned; that is the cut.

**The ticket mechanism as owner of the corpus reader.** Both existing docs had forward-declared
it. The spec's ownership decision names the always-on mechanism where a shared part's claim bites,
and the shape's own check and installer import the reader. `mechanism-shape` owns both files.

**P4 as a ticket rule.** *A spec's agreements, obligations and open issues before archiving it* was
copied into `/maintain` as one of the ticket mechanism's rules. The shelf never mentions a spec.
It is `/spec`'s, and `/spec` is undeclared; it waits in `/maintain`'s body inside a straw dog bound
to the ticket that declares the rest of the corpus.

**A fixer.** Of every check the shape yields, two are mechanically repairable without a judgment —
the header's bullet form and the field order — and both had zero live instances once the two old
headers were repaired. Check-only, until a violation kind with instances arrives.

**`Parent scope addressed` as dead weight.** Proposed for removal as written-and-never-read;
withdrawn in the same align, because its numbers had been read at the gate an hour earlier and
`/ticket`'s breakdown step shows them at approval. A missing coverage reader, not dead weight.

## The first run over the tree, 2026-09-09

Sixteen live records, nine diagnostics, nothing skipped, before any repair:

- **Three were the reader's fault**, not the records': three tickets carry the heading
  *Acceptance criteria — provisional, firmed at the align*, which the shelf itself licenses, and
  the reader wanted the bare title. The reader now takes the shelf's provisional suffix as the
  section; the records were not touched.
- **Three were this ticket's own**, the one shaped record in the tree: two sections outside the
  six and a prose line among its criteria, from the align's strike-and-answer form. Rewritten to
  the shaped form in the same stage; the resolutions live in its RFC.
- **One was the parent's bare header**, known since the align and repaired by hand along with the
  one archived record written the same way.
- **Two were tickets minted before the `/verify`-box rule** — the parent and the pacer — repaired
  under repair-and-report by adding the box, each marked with the date and the reason.

So the prior corpus, read honestly, held three violations of the shelf as it stood before this
slice: one header form and two missing boxes. The rest was the new rule meeting the record that
had been written under the old one, and a reader that had not read the shelf closely enough.
